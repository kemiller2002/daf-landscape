# Regulatory applicability map: DAF sponsor vs technology/administration provider

**Prepared 2026-11-20. Desk research only. This is research, not legal, tax or investment advice.** No regulator, sponsor or vendor was contacted. Every row needs review by qualified counsel before anyone relies on it, especially the rows marked conditional, low confidence or [K].

**Scope.** Two entities are mapped separately:
- **Sponsor**: a U.S. §501(c)(3) public charity (§170(b)(1)(A)(vi) / §509(a)(1)) that maintains donor-advised funds (§4966(d)(1)).
- **Provider**: a for-profit technology or administration company that serves one or more sponsors and is not itself a sponsor. "Via contract" means the duty is the sponsor's, but the provider has to produce the controls and evidence because the sponsor will require it by contract. "Conditional" means it depends on the provider's business model, most of all on whether it **holds or moves funds** (§7 and §14).

**Companion file:** [`regulatory-applicability-matrix.csv`](regulatory-applicability-matrix.csv), which has the same 65 rows (REG-001 to REG-065) with machine-readable columns.

**Obligation type.** (a) **legal**: a statute, regulation or binding contract rule (Nacha, card-brand) imposes it. (b) **risk control**: a sound control that is often mistaken for a legal mandate. (c) **convention**: industry practice, voluntary guidance or a disclosure question.

**Status.** `current_law` | `proposed` (published proposals, anticipated regulations, plan items) | `guidance` (IRS notices or revenue procedures, voluntary Treasury guidance, FATF) | `not_enacted`.

## 0. Answers in brief

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What is the legal core for a sponsor? | Six federal tax duties: §4966 (taxable distributions and expenditure responsibility), §4967 (prohibited benefits), §4958(c)(2) (automatic excess benefit), §4943(e) (excess business holdings), §170(f)(18) and (f)(8) acknowledgments, and §6033(k) reporting. Add substantiation for noncash gifts (Form 8283 signature, Form 8282), staying a public charity, OFAC, state charity registration and AG oversight, and the PPA 1995 securities exclusions. |
| Which of these apply to a non-sponsor provider directly? | Few. OFAC and the material-support statutes apply to every U.S. person. Privacy laws apply to it as a processor and sometimes as a controller. E-SIGN and UETA, breach notification and Nacha (as a third-party service provider or sender, through the bank contract) apply too. **Money transmission licensing applies if it holds or moves funds.** Almost everything else reaches the provider **via contract**: it must produce the evidence that lets the sponsor meet its own duty. |
| Does §4944 (jeopardizing investments) apply to DAFs? | **No.** PPA 2006 applied only §4943 to DAFs (§4943(e)(1): "for purposes of this section"), and §4944's text never mentions DAFs or sponsors [F]. Prudence comes from state law (UPMIFA, which is itself uncertain for DAFs) and fiduciary duty. |
| Does BSA/AML apply to the sponsor? | **No** program mandate. A charity paying its own grants is not a BSA "financial institution". OFAC applies regardless. Banks impose risk-based KYC by contract. |
| Is the sponsor a money transmitter? | **Probably not** when it pays its own funds from sponsor-titled accounts [I]. **A provider that takes possession of funds for several sponsors probably is** (FinCEN MSB plus state licences), unless it runs on a bank or licensed processor (§14). |
| Status of the 2023 proposed §4966 regulations | Proposed. Neither final nor withdrawn (Federal Register API, 2026-11-20). The 2026–27 Priority Guidance Plan lists "guidance under §§4966 and 6033 regarding certain DAF arrangements", with no scope stated. |
| AI law | No federal AI law or DAF-specific rule. Colorado's ADMT Act (SB 26-189, signed 2026-05-14) takes effect **2027-01-01** and covers consumer consequential decisions in listed domains. Grant decisions about organizations are likely outside it [S/I]. NYC LL144 and the EU AI Act do not apply absent hiring in NYC or an EU nexus. |

## 12. Obligations that require an accountable human decision

Software can prepare these decisions, but a named person with delegated authority has to make them. Either a statute attaches liability to a person's knowing agreement, or a signature is required, or the decision is a matter of judgment that courts and AGs review as the sponsor's discretion.

| Decision | Why a human | Rows |
|---|---|---|
| Final approval of each grant, or approval of the policy that auto-approves a defined grant class | The §4966 and §4967 manager taxes attach to a fund manager who **knowingly agrees**. Sponsor discretion ("exclusive legal control") is what defeats donor suits (*Pinkert*) and wins the donor's deduction (*Keefer*). The board's delegation must say which grant classes may be auto-approved. | REG-001, 003, 055, 058 |
| Expenditure responsibility and equivalency determinations | Pre-grant inquiry and the decision on the foreign or non-charity grantee are judgment, with a signed agreement. | REG-002, 020 |
| More-than-incidental-benefit calls (tickets, pledges, memberships, tuition) | The term has no definition in statute or final regulations. | REG-003, 016, 017 |
| Correcting an excess benefit; approving advisor compensation | Rebuttable-presumption process; board approval with comparables. | REG-004, 005 |
| Accepting complex assets and deciding when to divest | §4943(e) exposure, Form 8282 consequences. | REG-006, 011, 012 |
| Signing Form 8283 Part V, Form 990, Form 4720 and state registrations | Signature by an authorized official, some under penalties of perjury. | REG-011, 013, 014, 053 |
| Disposition of a potential OFAC true match; blocking or reject reports | Strict liability. Automation may clear documented false positives but must not close a high-score match. | REG-036, 037 |
| Escalating credible terror-finance or diversion indicators | Knowledge-based criminal statutes. | REG-041 |
| Holding, declining or reversing a grant on reputational or policy grounds | Discretion reviewed by AGs (the 2026 SPLC hold letters) and courts. | REG-055 |
| Investment policy and manager selection | State-law prudence and fiduciary duty. | REG-031, 057, 058 |
| Breach notification, privacy-rights exceptions, data protection assessments | Content and timing judgments with regulatory exposure. | REG-048, 049 |
| Payments structure, licensing and escheat positions | Counsel decisions, made once and reviewed when the model changes. | REG-042, 043, 045 |

**Design rule [I].** A board-approved **delegation-of-authority matrix** should list each decision class, say whether it is automatable under a versioned policy, and name the role that signs. Every automated approval should carry the policy version and the delegation that authorised it.

## 13. Commonly assumed requirements that do not legally apply to a DAF sponsor

| Assumption | What the law actually says | Rows |
|---|---|---|
| "Sponsors must run a BSA/AML program, verify identity under the CIP rules and file SARs." | BSA program duties attach to "financial institutions". A public charity paying its own grants is not one. A risk-based know-your-donor and grantee-diligence program is prudent and banks expect it, but it is not a BSA mandate. **OFAC does apply.** | REG-038 |
| "§4944 jeopardizing-investment rules apply to DAF assets." | Only §4943 was extended to DAFs. §4944 is silent. | REG-007 |
| "There is a minimum DAF payout or an inactivity rule." | No federal or state payout mandate is in force. The ACE Act and CA AB 1712 were not enacted. Payout and inactivity policies are sponsor choices. | REG-056 |
| "IRS rules forbid paying any pledge from a DAF." | Notice 2017-73 §4 allows it if the sponsor does not reference the pledge, no other benefit is received and no one claims a deduction. Bans on binding pledges are sponsor policy. | REG-016 |
| "Receipts must go out by January 31" / "must be on paper". | The deadline is the earlier of the donor's filing date or the return due date. IRS Pub 1771 allows email delivery. January 31 is convention. | REG-009 |
| "Signing Form 8283 means the sponsor agrees with the appraisal value." | The instructions do not say that. The signature acknowledges receipt. | REG-011 |
| "Federal law requires a conflict-of-interest, whistleblower and document-retention policy." | Form 990 Part VI only asks about them. Some states require them (e.g., NY N-PCL, unverified [K]). | REG-025, 059 |
| "Federal law requires an independent audit." | It does not. State thresholds apply (CA $2m; NY $1m audit and $250k–$1m review), and so do bank and lender covenants. | REG-054 |
| "GLBA/FTC Safeguards Rule governs the sponsor." | The FTC Act reaches entities organized for profit, so a bona fide nonprofit is generally outside it. Safeguards-equivalent controls are a sound risk control and a contract term for bank-affiliated programs. | REG-047 |
| "NY DFS Part 500 applies because we hold donor financial data." | It applies only to NY-licensed or chartered entities. | REG-052 |
| "SEC Reg S-P applies to the sponsor." | Only to SEC registrants, i.e. an affiliated adviser or broker-dealer, and to the provider by contract. | REG-051 |
| "The DAF sponsor must register its pooled funds as an investment company or under state securities law." | The Philanthropy Protection Act excludes or exempts charity-maintained funds, subject to conditions (§7(e) disclosure; the no-commission rule for solicitors). | REG-027 to 032 |
| "The 2023 proposed §4966 regulations (including the personal-adviser rule) are in force." | They are proposed only. | REG-021, 034 |
| "The Colorado AI Act regulates grant decisions now." | SB 24-205 was repealed and replaced. The new ADMT Act starts 2027-01-01, covers consumer decisions in listed domains, and charity grant decisions are likely outside it. | REG-060 |
| "FATF Recommendation 8 imposes duties on the sponsor." | It binds countries, and the 2023 revision expressly rejects treating NPOs as reporting entities. | REG-040 |
| "Charity-side callback verification before a bank change is required by law." | No statute requires it. Nacha's 2026 fraud-monitoring rules require risk-based processes, not a specific callback. A callback is a risk control (repo b49-04: no top-20 sponsor publishes a mandatory callback). | REG-044 |

## 14. Money transmission: implications for a multi-sponsor infrastructure provider

**Legal test [F].** FinCEN defines a money transmitter as a person that accepts currency, funds or other value that substitutes for currency from one person and transmits it to another location or person by any means (31 CFR 1010.100(ff)(5)(i)). Whether a person is one depends on the facts and circumstances. The exclusions most likely to be argued are:
- **(ii)(B), payment processor:** facilitating the purchase of, or payment of a bill for, a good or service, through a clearance and settlement system, under an agreement with the seller or creditor.
- **(ii)(F), integral services:** accepting and transmitting funds only as integral to the sale of goods or services.

A charitable grant is neither the purchase of a good or service nor the payment of a bill, so both exclusions fit poorly [I].

**Three architectures [I]:**

| Model | Who holds the funds | Likely status of the provider | Implications |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. **Instruction-only.** Funds stay in sponsor-titled bank and custody accounts. The provider generates payment files or API calls under the sponsor's ODFI agreement and credentials. | Sponsor | Not a transmitter: the provider never accepts value [I]. It is a Nacha third-party service provider. | Nacha TPSP duties via contract (2026 fraud monitoring, data security). SOC 1/SOC 2 for the sponsor's auditors. Sponsor-side approval controls. **Lowest regulatory load.** |
| B. **Licensed partner.** A bank or licensed processor (e.g., the PayPal Grant Payments or Chariot-style models the repo documents) receives and disburses. The provider orchestrates. | Bank or licensed processor | Provider generally not a transmitter if it never controls the funds. Possible agent status under the partner's licences, depending on contract. | Partner's KYC/AML requirements flow down. The provider must not touch FBO balances. Diligence on the partner's licence coverage. |
| C. **Provider custody.** Sponsors or donors fund an account the provider owns or controls, often pooled across sponsors, and the provider pays grantees. | Provider | **Likely a money transmitter.** FinCEN MSB registration, a BSA/AML program (CIP-style KYC, SAR/CTR, OFAC), state licences in up to ~49 states plus DC (MTMA adopted in full or in part by 31–35 states as of Aug–Sep 2026 [S]), surety bonds, net worth, permissible investments, exams. If NY-licensed, NY DFS Part 500 also applies. | Agent-of-payee exemptions generally require acting **for the payee**, here the recipient charity. A provider acting for the paying sponsor does not fit [I]. Commingling across sponsors also raises a sponsor-side problem: §6033(k) and audit need fund-level segregation, and in the provider's insolvency the funds are at risk (repo: the Flipcause remittance-delay exit). |

**Further points [I]:**
- **The sponsor.** A sponsor paying grants from its own accounts is a payor of its own funds after the gift. Exclusive legal control is what makes the money its own. It is not transmitting value for the donor, so it is likely not a transmitter. This is an inference: no FinCEN ruling on DAF sponsors was found.
- **"Deposit and forward" or pass-through designs**, where the sponsor commits in advance to forward a donor's money to a named charity, weaken both the exclusive-legal-control position and the "own funds" position.
- **Donor-side collection.** Card payments and ACH debits run through processors under their own licences. The provider stays a merchant-side technology vendor, subject to PCI DSS (REG-050) and Nacha WEB-debit account validation.
- **State charity-platform laws** (CA AB 488, HI) can apply to a provider that lists charities publicly for giving, independent of money transmission (REG-046).
- **Recommendation.** Build on Model A or B. Treat Model C as a licensing programme needing a board decision and state-by-state counsel. The provider should not take custody incidentally (for example, through settlement accounts used for "faster grants").

## 15. Uncertainties requiring counsel

1. **Money-transmitter status** of the provider in Models B and C, and of any "faster payments" float. State-by-state analysis is needed, including agent-of-payor and agent-of-payee exemptions and any 2026 MTMA amendments (REG-043). *Verify:* the CSBS MTMA tracker, and each state's statute and regulator interpretive letters.
2. **Which ICA §3(c)(10) prong** the sponsor's pooled DAF investments rely on, and therefore whether ICA §7(e) donor disclosure and the Exchange Act §3(e) no-commission condition bind referral, revenue-share and adviser-fee arrangements (REG-027 to 031). *Verify:* SEC no-action letters on charitable pools (sec.gov was not used in this pass).
3. **State securities opt-outs** under PPA 1995 §6(c) (REG-032). *Verify:* state securities codes for statutes enacted 1995–1998 that "specifically refer" to the Act.
4. **UPMIFA's application to DAF assets** (institutional fund versus not; prudence for donor-recommended or impact options) (REG-057). *Verify:* the enacted UPMIFA in the sponsor's state of incorporation and any AG guidance.
5. **Escheat of uncashed or voided grants.** Is a voided grant property owed to the grantee or a cancelled internal allocation? Do state charity exemptions apply? (REG-045) *Verify:* the holder rules and exemptions in each state, and the state of incorporation's priority rules.
6. **Foreign grants.** Can a sponsor rely on equivalency determinations for §4966, and does Notice 2006-109 interim guidance still apply? (REG-020) *Verify:* IRB 2006-51 and the 2023 NPRM preamble.
7. **The deemed-distribution concept** in the 2023 NPRM, its interaction with the §4966(c)(2)(A) public-charity exception if finalized, and what "certain DAF arrangements" in the 2026–27 PGP will cover (REG-021, 022).
8. **State privacy-law thresholds and coverage** for each state where the sponsor has donors, and whether the provider is a processor or a controller for its own analytics or AI training on sponsor data (REG-048).
9. **Colorado ADMT Act.** Could donor account acceptance or closure of Colorado residents be a "financial or lending services" consequential decision? Check the AG's disclosure rules, due by 2027-01-01 (REG-060). *Verify:* the enacted SB 26-189 text on leg.colorado.gov.
10. **Reg BI and adviser fiduciary duties** when a broker or adviser recommends funding a DAF with securities. This is a facts-and-circumstances question with no DAF-specific guidance (REG-033).
11. **State-law requirements for COI and whistleblower policies** and audit committees in the state of incorporation and registration states (REG-025, 054, 059).
12. **Whether e-delivered donor acknowledgments are E-SIGN "consumer disclosures"** that need §7001(c) consent. IRS Pub 1771 permits email delivery, which suggests no, but the statute was not read this pass (REG-064).
