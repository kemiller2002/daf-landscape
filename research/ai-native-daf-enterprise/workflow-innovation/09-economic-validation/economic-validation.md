# Economic validation

**Rule (brief §11):** a design is not superior because it has fewer manual steps. It must show an advantage on defined criteria: cost per outcome *at equal or better control effectiveness*, under stated assumptions, across sizes.

**Sources:**
- experiment outputs in [`../08-experiments/results/`](../08-experiments/results/);
- network and infrastructure outputs in [`results/`](results/), generated by `Daf.Experiments.NetworkEconomics`;
- the parent cost model in [`../../09-financial-models/`](../../09-financial-models/financial-and-staffing-models.md).

**Loaded human cost:** $103 per hour [A], the parent model's $165k loaded FTE cost ÷ 1,600 productive hours.

**Every figure is [X] or [S] on assumptions [A]** unless marked as sourced.

## 2. Per-workflow economics from the experiments

### Grant approval (E2, 200,000 grants per year, 1,359 seeded defects)

| Design | Human hours | Human cost | Per grant | Escaped defects | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Manual review of all (baseline) | 31,972 | $3.29m | $16.47 | 704 | Comparator |
| Rules + manual all | 31,972 | $3.29m | $16.47 | 646 | Same cost, better control |
| **AI-native flag-only + canaries** | 15,389 | $1.59m | $7.93 | **404** | Better on cost and control *under base assumptions*; **not robust** (see note) |
| Radical: sampling, no AI | 12,532 | $1.29m | $6.45 | 1,069 | Cheaper but **worse control: not superior** |
| **Ecosystem: shared determinations** | 10,219 | $1.05m | **$5.26** | 404 | Cheaper at equal escapes *by construction*, given reuse of 50% [A] |
| AI auto-clear (unsafe) | 13,789 | $1.42m | $7.10 | 729 | Rejected (control) |
| Pay-first | 15,385 | $1.58m | $7.92 | 1,277 | Rejected (control and law) |

**Robustness note (adversarial audit, 07-).** The base-case dominance depends on parameters:
- With AI recall equal to human catch rates, flag-only has the same control as rules plus manual review of everything (651 against 646 escapes), at about half the hours.
- With a 1-minute routine manual review, hours are equal and control is better.

So the AI-native design is superior on **one** criterion under each sensitivity, never independently on both. Which one depends on GAP-001 and GAP-004, which only a design-partner shadow study can measure.

**Context.** Observed whole-organization opex per grant in FY2024 runs from $61 (Fidelity) to $2,487 (SVCF) (EV-136). The approval-labor component modeled here is a fraction of that. **Approval is not where most cost sits.** This matches the parent finding that the AI value pool in operations is thin ($445k calibrated at a large national, EV-107).

**What the escaped-defect difference is worth depends on the cost of an escaped error**, and that cost is unmeasured (GAP-004). The repository's prior work bounds the multiple between 1.0 and 88.5 handling-times and puts a priced sanctions miss near 128 (strategic synthesis §0 B). At any multiple ≥ 1, the AI-native design's 300 fewer escapes add to its advantage.

### Disbursement (E3, 100,000 grants)

| Design | Sponsor payment cost | Recipient reconciliation | Uncaught fraudulent payee changes | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline | $152,100 | 8,333 h (~$858k at $103/h, borne by nonprofits) | 14.4 | |
| Grant ID in remittance | $152,100 | 833 h (~$86k) | 14.4 | **~$772k a year moved off nonprofits for near-zero sponsor cost [X]** |
| Daily netting | $93,339 | 833 h | 14.4 | 39% fewer payments; larger blast radius per return |
| Registry + netting | $41,116 | 833 h | 2.4 | Needs two-sided adoption |

The largest economic effect in disbursement falls **on recipients, not the sponsor**. That is why incumbents have not prioritized it (industry report §3, "weak nonprofit leverage"). It is also a philanthropic-outcome improvement, not just a cost one.

### Reconciliation (E4)

Continuous: 370 hours. Month-end: 1,137 hours with a 2-minute-per-day age penalty [A]. **With no age penalty both modes cost 370 hours** (audit rerun). The labor saving is unevidenced; only earlier detection (0 against 15.5 days) is supported.

### Specialist capacity (E6)

**Deferral windows save about 29% of specialist FTE in the model** (35 → 25, corrected to defer December work into January). This holds *only if donors accept* that December recommendations may execute in January, which is untested. Pooling across sponsors saves about 3–6%, mostly through integer-FTE granularity.

At the parent model's 100k-account scale (30 grants-compliance FTE), smoothing alone is worth about 8–9 FTE, roughly $1.4m a year [S]. If donors accept the windows, this is the largest single human-cost lever found, and it needs no AI. Its evidence class is a model plus an untested behavioral premise.

## 4. Network effects: mechanism demonstrated, value tested

**Mechanism (E7, public data):** recipient overlap means a shared verification record would avoid 51.3% of sponsor-recipient verifications across 48 top-50 sponsors. Network effects in *work avoided* exist **mechanically**.

**Value**, from [`results/network-sensitivity.csv`](results/network-sensitivity.csv). This is savings per reused recipient record, valuing only *human* work: the share of recipients needing a determination × cost per determination, plus payee verification of $15 [A]. Automated eligibility lookups are nearly free (E1), so reusing them is worth nothing.

| Cost per reused determination | Share of recipients needing one | Total member savings, top-48 | Operator revenue (40% fee) | Operator break-even, large-first |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $67.50 (45-minute routine review) | 8% | $2.88m | $1.15m | **never** (fixed cost $1.2m [A]) |
| $67.50 | 20% | $4.02m | $1.61m | 17 members |
| $270 (about 3 hours of diligence) | 8% | $5.17m | $2.07m | 9 members |
| $1,080 (foreign equivalency-grade work) | 8% | $14.31m | $5.73m | 3 members |

**Join order matters as much as size** ([`results/network-join-curve.csv`](results/network-join-curve.csv)):
- Large sponsors first: the second joiner saves about $217k a year.
- Small sponsors first: the second joiner saves $408.
- Random order: about $11k.

**Conclusions on network effects:**
1. The network effect is **real but conditional**. It is a data-network effect in *verification work*, with strong positive returns only after the largest sponsors join.
2. **For routine eligibility it is economically thin**, because automated checks are already cheap. It **is worth building around costly determinations**: foreign equivalency, fiscal-sponsor diligence, new-organization review and payee verification. That is exactly where human work and fraud risk concentrate.
3. **Cold start is the binding problem.** The sponsors who create the most value for others, the large nationals, gain least from joining, because their own records already cover most recipients. A neutral operator would need large sponsors as anchor members, for example by giving them governance rights, or would need to seed the network from public data plus its own platform clients (the Model C route). This is the classic data-network chicken-and-egg problem, now quantified.
4. **The data cover only the top-50 itemizing sponsors.** The ~1,450 smaller sponsors are the ones with least verification capacity, and they would plausibly gain most per member. The public data cannot measure that (Schedule I of small sponsors would need a further harvest; listed as a next experiment).

## 5. Break-even and sensitivity summary

| Claim | Break-even or condition | Most sensitive assumption |
|---|---|---|
| AI-native approval beats manual on cost and control | Holds whenever human per-item catch rates on benefit and related-party defects are below the AI flagger's recall (E2) | Human catch rates (GAP-004) and AI recall under attack |
| Shared determinations add savings | Reuse ≥ ~20% of non-routine reviews, **and** a grants-compliance team big enough to cut whole FTE (≥ ~100k accounts; parent 09 B1 update: +$0.36m at 100k, −$30k at 10k for 50% reuse) | Reuse factor and determination minute share (measure on partner history) |
| Grant ID in remittance | Always positive for recipients | Recipient bank passing addenda |
| Netting | Positive if per-payment cost > ~$0.10 | Return rate × grants per payment |
| Verification network | Determination value ≥ ~$270, or anchor members | Cost per determination; anchor participation |
| Shared compliance provider | ≥ ~40 sponsors at $60k | Willingness to pay (GAP-014) |
| Per-grant infrastructure API | ≥ $3 per grant and many customers | Approval labor share of cost |
| Surge smoothing | Donor acceptance of execution windows | Behavior (untested) |

## 6. What this does to the parent recommendation

**The staged C→D path survives**, with three refinements:
1. Price C on **per-account administration** (the RCF→Ren reference, about $745 per account, EV-044), not per grant. Per-grant API pricing is weak.
2. The verification network is a **feature of the multi-tenant platform** first, valued on costly determinations. It is not a standalone first business.
3. Shared compliance policy packs are a **plausible second product** for sponsors that keep their incumbent recordkeeper.
