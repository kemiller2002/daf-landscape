# Proposals to the Echelon AI-Native Enterprise Design Framework

**Status:** proposals only. They are not applied. The framework repository (`kemiller2002/Echelon-AI-Native-Enterprise-Design-Framework`) was inspected on 2026-11-20 at commit `57c53f7` and contains only an empty README. Per brief §16 it is **not modified**. Each proposal is stated industry-independently. The DAF case appears only as the validation evidence behind it.

| ID | Proposed reusable element | Generalized statement | DAF validation evidence | Status of evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FP-01 | **Outcome decomposition with D/P/H decision typing** | Decompose every outcome into decisions typed Deterministic, Probabilistic or Human-authorized. A probabilistic decision may never change money or legal state alone; it must resolve to D (a fixed threshold under approved policy) or to H | 81 decision types; the census is tested (parent 01-) | L3 (method applied; census tested) |
| FP-02 | **Request ≠ instruction** | Separate the *request* entity (from customers or their agents) from the *instruction* entity (the organization's authorized act). Only policy code or authorized people create instructions | Parent DD-003; INV-A3 tests | L3 |
| FP-03 | **Flag-only rule for AI** | AI may raise a risk flag but never clear one. Clearance types exclude agent actors | E2: flag-only bounds AI downside at "as if absent" under prompt injection. E10: any auto-close threshold escapes defects after distribution shift | L3 (synthetic) |
| FP-04 | **Canary test cases for every deterministic rule** | Every deterministic control must be continuously exercised with known-answer synthetic cases. Monitoring by AI or sampling does not detect a silently disabled rule | E2: flag-only AI never detected a disabled rule; 2% sampling had no power; canaries detected on day 1 | L3 (synthetic) |
| FP-05 | **Verify properties of entities, not transactions; refresh by diff** | When a check depends on an attribute of a counterparty, store a reusable, versioned evidence record per counterparty and update it from *source changes*, not on a fixed schedule or per transaction | E1: naive full refresh cost more than per-transaction checks; diff-driven refresh cut operations ~90% | L3 (synthetic) |
| FP-06 | **Source-latency floor** | The accuracy of automated checks is bounded by the publication latency of the authoritative source. Architecture cannot beat it; only a faster source or a compensating control can | E1: all monthly-refreshed designs escaped the same 21 grants | L3 (synthetic) |
| FP-07 | **Measure reuse before claiming network effects** | A claimed network or reuse effect must be shown with a mechanism metric (overlap, reuse curve) *and* a value metric (value per reuse), *and* tested for join-order dependence (cold start) | E7 (public data): 51.3% verification overlap, but reuse at the 2nd joiner ranges 4.3–69.4% by order; value thin unless per-reuse work is costly | L2 (public data) |
| FP-08 | **Correlated-demand test for pooling** | Before pooling capacity across units, test whether demand peaks are correlated. Time-shifting demand often beats pooling when peaks coincide | E6: pooling −6% FTE, smoothing −29% | L3 (synthetic) |
| FP-09 | **Exactly-once side effects need two halves** | A durable intent (outbox) *and* counterparty idempotency are both required for exactly-once external effects. Counterparty idempotency is a selection criterion | E9: exhaustive crash enumeration | L3 (logical model) |
| FP-10 | **Missing-signal alarms accompany continuous controls** | A continuous control silently degrades to periodic when its input feed stops. Every continuous control needs a missing-input alarm | E4: masked outage raised maximum break age from 1 day to 10 | L3 (synthetic) |
| FP-11 | **Five-design minimum with a mechanism-difference test** | Two designs differ meaningfully only if they differ in decision right, timing relative to the request, authoritative data source, or performing organization | 105 designs catalogued; superficial variants rejected (03-) | Method applied |
| FP-12 | **Maturity ladder L0–L4** | Invented hypothesis → plausible → evidence-supported → tested prototype → operationally validated; never skipped | Used throughout this research | Method applied |
| FP-13 | **Who-bears-the-cost test** | Evaluate every redesign for cost moved to parties outside the payer relationship (customers, counterparties, reviewers) | E3: the largest disbursement saving (~$772k per 100k grants) accrues to recipients, not the operator | L3 (synthetic) |
| FP-14 | **Independent adversarial review by a non-author agent** | Adversarial review is done by an agent or person that did not author the designs and is given documents, not conclusions | [`../07-adversarial-reviews/`](../07-adversarial-reviews/) | Method applied |

**What should *not* be generalized:** DAF-specific legal constraints (§4966/§4967, exclusive control), payout norms, specific fee levels, and the IRS data cadence. These belong in a domain profile, not the general framework.

**How to submit:** open an issue or pull request on the framework repository with these items, once its owner authorizes contributions.
