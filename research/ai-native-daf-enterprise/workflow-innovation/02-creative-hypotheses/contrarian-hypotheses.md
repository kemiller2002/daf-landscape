# Contrarian hypotheses and the enterprise inversion ledger

**Method:** creative methods B (inversion) and F (contrarian). Each hypothesis contradicts an industry convention and is stated so that evidence can falsify it. **Maturity** follows the methodology ladder (L0–L3). "Evidence for" never means "proven".

## 2. Additional contrarian hypotheses (H-08 to H-15)

| ID | Falsifiable statement | Evidence for | Evidence against / risks | Test | Maturity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H-08 | **Routine grants need no per-item human review at all.** Statistical acceptance sampling with seeded defects gives equal or better control effectiveness than two-person review at lower cost | Revoked-EIN dollars 0.082% of top-50 (strategic synthesis §0); statutory exception for listed public charities (§4966(c)(1)) | Two-person review incidentally catches account takeover and anomalies (Clarity AC-14); adversaries adapt to sampling | E2: per-item vs tiered vs sampling architectures on synthetic streams with seeded and adaptive-adversary defects | L1 |
| H-09 | **The grant approval queue is not the bottleneck; payee data and remittance are.** Most donor- and recipient-visible delay and cost sits after approval | Processor complaints describe payout rules, checks and EFT vetting (gap19-complaints-with-denominators.md); 33% of Fidelity grants not by EFT; no grant ID in addenda (EV-086) | Approval latency is unmeasured (EV-076); the claim may reflect only who complains | E3 path-cost decomposition; partner latency telemetry | L1 |
| H-09b | **Non-routine review cost is dominated by repetition, not difficulty.** With determinations reused, specialist minutes per non-routine grant fall by more than half | Exceptions cluster by recipient (12.5 grants/recipient); foreign 1.46% of dollars across 4 nationals | Reuse factor unmeasured; foreign facts change; reliance on others' EDs legally uncertain (regulatory map) | Measure reuse factor on partner history; E2/E6 sensitivity | L1 |
| H-10 | **Portability increases a sponsor's inflows more than it costs in outflows.** Publishing a transfer protocol with full history attracts donors who would otherwise avoid lock-in | None direct; challengers compete on transparency (challenger-economics) | Incumbents' asset-based fees oppose it; no donor survey evidence | Market interviews; A/B in a pilot program | L0 |
| H-11 | **Recipients, not sponsors, should own their payment and identity data.** A recipient-enrolled registry reduces hijack fraud and payment cost across sponsors | Payee verification repeated per sponsor; fragmented rails (industry report §2.5) | Small recipients and congregations may never enroll (none of 14 congregation pages showed a DAF option, b4026-06); registry is a high-value target | E3; registry enrollment would need a pilot | L1 |
| H-12 | **The year-end surge is a scheduling problem, not a staffing problem.** Flexible execution windows plus pre-clearing known recipients remove most surge SLA breaches without extra staff | Twin: 34 days over SLA at surge; halving exceptions removes the breach | Donor acceptance of windows is unknown | E6 twin variants | L1 (simulated) |
| H-13 | **Dormant capital is a product-design outcome.** Optional spend-down horizons and standing plans chosen at opening raise grant activity without lowering contributions | 22% of accounts made no grants 2020–22; median 3-year payout 9% (EV-015) | Asset-fee incentive conflict; donor backlash; mechanism of dormancy unknown | Partner pilot with randomized offer (Phase 6) | L0 |
| H-14 | **Most of what a DAF administrator does is evidence management, so the auditable evidence ledger, not the portal, is the core product.** | No sponsor publishes operating metrics (EV-076); none of 13 vendors publishes a scoped SOC report (strategic synthesis §0 C) | Buyers purchase portals and distribution, not evidence (GAP-014) | Willingness-to-pay interviews; Phase 4 terms | L0 |
| H-15 | **Charity verification is a public good best supplied by a neutral utility, not a competitive feature.** | IRS data free; duplication across sponsors [I]; Candid Charity Check exists | Neutral utilities need funding and governance; incumbents (Candid, Chariot) partially fill it | E7 overlap curve plus funding model analysis (09-) | L1 |

## 3. Enterprise inversion ledger (10 prompts)

| Inversion prompt | What it suggests for the enterprise | What replaces the underlying function | Survives constraints? |
|---|---|---|---|
| How could this process be eliminated? | Eliminate per-grant verification, status inquiries, re-keying, batch check runs and month-end reconstruction | Recipient passport (INN-01); shared state (INN-14); single structured intake; electronic rails with remittance (INN-08); continuous close (INN-22) | Yes, as modified designs |
| What would make the process unnecessary? | Pre-verify the recipient universe; give every contribution a unique reference | Universe re-screening (INN-03, INN-34); per-donor references (INN-23) | Yes |
| What if no employee touched the transaction? | Routine contributions and grants flow without people | Deterministic policy (INN-05); people own the *rules* and the exceptions | Yes for the routine tier; never for H decisions (outcome-decisions.csv) |
| What if the donor never completed a form? | Progressive collection, reuse of upstream attestations, advisor-supplied structured data | INN-24, INN-25, INN-13 | Partly: agreement acceptance and acknowledgment content are irreducible |
| What if there were no conventional departments? | Outcome teams, one exception queue, agent owners | INN-31; parent organization model | Yes, with segregation-of-duties boundaries retained |
| What if compliance were evaluated continuously? | Obligations as code; evidence emitted per operation | INN-20, INN-21, INN-34 | Yes; counsel and filings stay human |
| What if accounting were continuously reconciled? | Invariants evaluated per event; close is a checkpoint | INN-22 plus CTL-048 attestation | Yes |
| What if there were no manual grant approval queue? | Rules decide routine; reviews only on flags; sampling for assurance | INN-05, INN-06 | Partly: non-routine grants require a person (regulatory map §12) |
| What if the process began before the donor submitted a request? | Pre-verified recipients; liquidity forecast from intents; pre-cleared November recipients | INN-03, INN-27, INN-37 | Yes |
| What if an external partner performed part of the function? | Custody, rails, appraisals, non-cash liquidation, specialist pools, verification network | INN-02, INN-12, INN-28; DD-008 | Yes, if decision rights and funds stay with the sponsor (regulatory map §14) |
