# Cross-industry transfers

**Method:** creative method D, as defined in [`../00-methodology/methodology.md`](../00-methodology/methodology.md) §3.

Each transfer names:
- the source mechanism;
- the DAF application, linked to the [innovation register](innovation-register.csv);
- why it might work;
- **where the analogy breaks**;
- the maturity level, L0–L3.

No transfer is evidence on its own account. An analogy is a source of hypotheses, not of proof.

| T1 | **Payment networks** | Payee directories (account-validation, alias registries such as proxy lookups); the network, not each payer, holds verified endpoints | Verified payee registry (INN-07); recipient inbox (INN-38) | Payee verification is a property of the recipient, repeated today by every sponsor; four rail camps already exist (industry report §2.5) | Networks have scheme rules and liability frameworks that DAFs lack; a registry that *held* funds would trigger money transmission (regulatory map §14); churches and small recipients may never enroll | L1 |

**Pattern across transfers:** the mechanisms that survive the constraint pass share three properties.
1. They move work from the **transaction** to the **entity**: recipient, determination, payee or identity.
2. They move work from **request time** to **source-change time**.
3. They keep **the decision right with the sponsor** while sharing *evidence*.

The transfers that fail (T2) are the ones that try to move the *decision* after the money.
