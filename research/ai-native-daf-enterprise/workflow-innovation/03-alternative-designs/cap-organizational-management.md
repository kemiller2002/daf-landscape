# Capability: organizational management

**Outcome:** the right people, with the right authority, do the judgment work, and management decisions rest on evidence.

**Evidence:**
- FY2024 990 staffing benchmarks (EV-135): 117 to 5,670 grants per employee.
- Parent org model and twin findings: the surge and single-specialist concentration.

| ID | Type | Mechanism | Maturity |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-ORG-1 | Conventional | Departments by function; managers allocate tasks; headcount budgeting by ratios | Observed [I] |
| D-ORG-2 | Automation-first | Departments plus workflow tools and dashboards | L1 |
| D-ORG-3 | AI-native | Outcome teams; one exception queue with skill and authority routing; agent owners; twin-informed staffing (INN-30, INN-31) | L3 (simulated twin) |
| D-ORG-4 | **Radical: decision-rights market** | No standing teams for exception work. Cases are posted to a pool of credentialed specialists, some internal and some fractional or shared across sponsors (INN-28), who claim cases within their delegated authority; SLAs are enforced by the router. Management becomes credentialing, quality sampling (CTL-046) and authority design | L0 |
| D-ORG-5 | Ecosystem: shared services for small sponsors | Grants compliance, complex assets and finance operations provided by a shared center under each sponsor's delegation | L1 |

**Constraint pass:**
- SoD and conflict rules apply to every claimant (INV-A7).
- Confidentiality across sponsors.
- Employment law for fractional specialists.
- D-ORG-4 survives only with strong credentialing and audit.

**Perspectives:**
- **Economist:** D-ORG-4 and D-ORG-5 smooth the surge peaks the twin shows.
- **Adversarial:** a gig model risks quality and accountability drift.
- **Regulatory:** each decision must still be attributable to someone with the sponsor's delegated authority.

**Experiment:** E6 (pooled vs dedicated specialists under surge).
**Recommendation:** D-ORG-3 now; test D-ORG-5; hold D-ORG-4.
