# Capability: philanthropic intelligence

**Outcome (BO-51, BO-52, BO-54):** donors make better-informed decisions that respect donor intent, without unsupported impact claims.

**Evidence:**
- Ratings coverage and terms for AI use (b43n26-03 charity-data terms).
- Church-status recipients receive 8.3% of top-50 dollars with sparse data (strategic synthesis §0 G4).
- Community foundations keep 60% of grant dollars in-state (strategic synthesis §0 E).

| ID | Type | Mechanism | Maturity |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-PI-1 | Conventional | Links to ratings sites; curated lists by staff | Observed |
| D-PI-2 | Automation-first | Embedded ratings widgets and search | L1 |
| D-PI-3 | AI-native | Goal elicitation; evidence-graded recipient profiles with disclosed criteria; fairness monitoring (INN-33) | L1 |
| D-PI-4 | **Radical: donor-collective learning, not recommendations** | The sponsor publishes no rankings. It offers (a) the donor's own giving analytics, (b) anonymized, aggregate patterns of what similar donors funded, and (c) recipients' self-reported outcomes with evidence grades. Judgment stays with the donor, and the sponsor never steers | L0 |
| D-PI-5 | Ecosystem: local intelligence partners | Community foundations and field experts provide vetted local or issue knowledge through APIs, with revenue share | L1 |

**Constraint pass:**
- Neutrality, with no sponsor-paid placement.
- Consumer-protection exposure for impact claims.
- Data licensing for charity data used with AI.
- Privacy for aggregate patterns, with minimum cohort sizes.

**Perspectives:**
- **Customer advocate:** D-PI-4 respects intent.
- **Economist:** monetization is weak (10-: philanthropic intelligence scored 2.35).
- **Adversarial:** herding toward popular recipients disadvantages small and faith recipients unless fairness monitoring applies.

**Recommendation:** D-PI-3 plus D-PI-5 as an engagement feature, not a revenue line.
