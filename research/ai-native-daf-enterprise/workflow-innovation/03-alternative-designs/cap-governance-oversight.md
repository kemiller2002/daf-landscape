# Capability: governance and oversight

**Outcome (BO-45, BO-46):** the board, executives, auditors and compliance officers exercise real, evidenced oversight.

**Evidence:**
- Vendor-related directors (Charityvest/Foundation Source; industry report §2.3).
- The RCF→Ren $19.23m related-party payment (EV-044).
- The Clarity review found parameter drift (FM-08).

| ID | Type | Mechanism | Maturity |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-GOV-1 | Conventional | Quarterly board packs assembled by management; annual conflict forms | Observed [I] |
| D-GOV-2 | Automation-first | Board portal with automated pack assembly | L1 |
| D-GOV-3 | AI-native | Live evidence portal; governed parameters with expiry (CTL-047); agent-drafted narrative linked to evidence; board approves deltas (INN-32) | L1 |
| D-GOV-4 | **Radical: governance by exception and pre-commitment** | The board pre-commits to risk-appetite bounds encoded as policy parameters. The system escalates to the board only when a metric leaves its bounds or a parameter expires. Meetings focus on exceptions and strategy, not reporting. Independent directors receive the same raw evidence access as management | L0 |
| D-GOV-5 | Ecosystem: independent governance-as-a-service | External independent directors and an internal-audit provider shared across small sponsors | L1 |

**Constraint pass:**
- State nonprofit law requires board oversight and cannot be delegated to software.
- D-GOV-4 survives if the board still meets and approves policies.
- The risk is information overload or false comfort.

**Perspectives:**
- **Regulatory:** D-GOV-4 must not become "the system governs".
- **Adversarial:** bounds set loosely once are never revisited, which is why expiry is mandatory.
- **Customer advocate:** transparency to donors.

**Recommendation:** D-GOV-3 now; pilot D-GOV-4 elements.
