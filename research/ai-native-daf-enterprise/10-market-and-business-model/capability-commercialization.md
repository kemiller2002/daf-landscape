# Commercializing the operating infrastructure (addendum §22)

**Status:** analysis, 2026-11-20. Each internal capability ([capability map](../02-business-capabilities/capability-map.md)) is assessed as a possible product.

**Pricing:** all pricing entries are **hypotheses [A]**. Where a public price for a *different* product exists, it is cited as an anchor and its difference is stated.

**Regulatory column:** this is research, not legal advice; the authoritative analysis is [`../08-governance-and-compliance/`](../08-governance-and-compliance/). The money-transmission point applies to any product that touches funds in flight. The design rule is to **never take possession of funds**: payments go sponsor bank → recipient through the sponsor's bank or a licensed rail (DD-008).

**Attractiveness score (1–5):**

| Weight | Factor |
|---|---|
| 25% | Measurable pain |
| 20% | Reachable buyer |
| 20% | Defensibility |
| 20% | Low regulatory or technical burden |
| 15% | Strategic pull-through to Models C/D |

The scores are judgments, with the rationale given in each row. Subscores are in [`capability-scores.csv`](capability-scores.csv), and the totals are recomputed from them.

## Reading

1. **The highest-scoring products are the ones that touch the record:** P11 and P5 (3.55), P7 (3.45), and P2 and P9 (3.4). This reproduces the A–D comparison: value and defensibility sit where the operating record is, which is why the C→D sequence matters.
2. **Standalone eligibility (P1) is a crowded, near-commodity market.** Candid, Goodstack, Benevity, Chariot and Every.org already offer it, and the raw data is free from the IRS. It is worth building for internal use and selling as a *by-product*. Its differentiator is bitemporal evidence ("what was known at decision time"), which the repository found no one publishing.
3. **Servicing (P6) and intelligence (P8) are the weakest standalone products.** They depend on someone else's record, generic AI vendors compete, and they carry steering and tax-advice risk. Keep them as features of D.
4. **Nonprofit-side reconciliation (a variant of P7) is the one product whose buyer is the party that bears the industry's friction.** The economics are uncertain: nonprofits are not payers of DAF administration, and Chariot or CRM vendors could bundle it (entrant S2 falsifier [S]). It is a good candidate for an early, cheap demand test.

## Must not do

- **Hold funds**, or take title to donations in transit (failure pattern P1; Flipcause and Ammado).
- **Rely on donor credentials** for automation. Six of the top 20 sponsors restrict it [V wave 19]; use sanctioned APIs or delegated grants (DD-017).
- **Sell "AI approves grants".** The product sells *sponsor-approved deterministic policy with AI flags*. Selling AI approval contradicts the control rule (DD-002, DD-005) and would not pass sponsor counsel (entrant falsifier).
