# Audited fee transfers and customer-cohort evidence: Ren, TIFIN Give and Chariot
**Research through November 11, 2026 | U.S. DAF technology sector**

## Executive conclusion

We can now establish **five consecutive years of audited, disclosed payments by Renaissance Charitable Foundation (RCF) to the related-party Ren administration provider**. This materially improves the prior analysis that lacked verified Ren service-fee capture. In fiscal 2024, audited RCF financial statements report **$19,225,546** paid to Ren for administration services, compared with **$15,543,682** in fiscal 2023. The RCF/administration contractual compensation basis in Note 9 is **98% of administrative fees earned from individual fundholder accounts**, while 2022 and earlier footnotes state 95% [F01–F04].

The disclosure is unusually useful because it identifies a *real payer*, a *real recipient*, an *administration services arrangement* and *actual amounts*. It is **not Ren's complete company revenue statement, standalone software ARR, profitability, EBITDA, or net revenue retention**. We have not inspected Ren's own books, RCF's signed services contract, or any individual enterprise customer's invoices.

The same level of customer-specific fee transfer evidence was **not** located for TIFIN Give or Chariot within these public documents. That does not establish that their business models are weaker: it indicates a difference in observable evidence.

## 1. Audited Ren service fee evidence: 2020–2024

|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 2020 | $7,910,232 | **$7,514,718** | 95% of individual fundholder fee base | 10,119 |
| 2021 | $11,453,357 | **$10,880,690** | 95% of individual fundholder fee base | 15,938 |
| 2022 | $14,200,144 | **$13,490,137** | 95% of individual fundholder fee base | 18,543 |

*Year-end DAF accounts are an **entirely different metric** from the number of *paying enterprise customers*, the mean number of funds during each year, the individual fundholder administrative revenue **fee base**, retained cohorts or churn. End-year accounts and same-year vendor fees should not be divided and presented as a real per-customer subscription price or ARPU.*

### Verified descriptive changes

- Payments disclosed to Ren increased from **$7,514,718 in 2020** to **$19,225,546 in 2024**, up **$11,710,828** or about **2.56×**; their four-year compound annual *observed payment growth* was **26.47%** [F01–F04].
- FY2024 payments to Ren were **23.69% above FY2023**; RCF reported **20.53% more DAF accounts at year end**, from 21,426 to 25,824 [F01,F05,F06].
- Five-year **sum of disclosed payments** was **$66,654,773**, using the audited annual series (not an enterprise valuation or lifetime contract value).
- The documented contractual percentage of *individual fundholder* administrative-fee revenue changed from **95% to 98% beginning in 2023** according to the 2023/2022 comparative audit [F02]. This means fee transfers can change because of contract economics, asset values, account activity, account additions or product mix, **not simply retention**.
- In FY2023, the **$15,543,682 Ren payment was only 96.56% of RCF's total $16,097,870 admin-fee income** even though Note 9 describes a 98%-based formula. This is *not* a demonstrated arithmetic error or contract breach: the note specifies **individual fundholder account** revenues, which may be a subset of the financial statement's broader admin-fee revenue [F01,F02]. Do not apply the 98% rate mechanically to all reported income.
- In FY2024, the audited RCF total administrative fee income of **$19,617,095** minus the stated Ren payment of **$19,225,546** equals **$391,549**. That residual is **not RCF's true economic margin** and is not a claim that all sponsor expenses are covered. It is only the difference between these two specific audited lines.

### Who owns the actual service arrangement?

The audited notes describe RCF and Ren as **related parties**, identify two Ren employees on RCF's board, and identify a formal Administrative Services Agreement [F01–F04]. The operations note identifies a Renaissance Acquisition Company LLC (Ren) service relationship covering contribution and grant transaction processing, accounting, reporting, donor customer service, regulatory reporting, tax support, and charitable planning. Thus **these fee transfers purchase an integrated administrative service bundle**, not simply a software license.

This governance relationship creates **related-party fee-setting and concentration diligence questions**: What approvals, independence safeguards, contract tendering, renegotiation rights, service levels, and exit provisions apply? Board overlap and paid services are disclosed facts, **not evidence of improper conduct**. Without Ren's company accounts, one cannot quantify what percentage of total Ren revenue comes from RCF. Without RCF's detailed cost center analysis, one cannot quantify Ren's service gross margin or any standalone software margin.

### Important accounting and cohort limitations

- RCF's *charitable contributions* and *investment returns* are not Ren's technology revenue. The fee transfer described above is the relevant directly observed business payment.
- **Growth in service payments is not gross or net revenue retention.** No beginning cohort, individual contract attrition, same-customer expansion, cross-sell, price changes, churned institutions, or account closure history was disclosed.
- Five years of continuity of audited payment lines establishes a **continuing provider/sponsor relationship** across five reported financial years, not evidence that every donor fund remained, that the signed contract was identical, or that another enterprise client renewed.
- The disclosure is for RCF. Ren may have **many other unrelated institutional contracts**, but their invoices, fees, and profitability are unobserved in this assessment.
- RCF's FY2024 **$5.9 billion charitable balance** and **25,824 DAF accounts** belong to the charity, not Ren's balance sheet or a single-platform paying-customer cohort [F01,F05].

## 2. TIFIN Give: the closest comparable public financial evidence is different in nature

TIFIN Give's dated March 31, 2025 investment-adviser brochure discloses **potential fee structures**, including technology/admin basis-point fees, investment management, possible sub-advisory fees, SaaS implementation/subscription and sponsored investment model visibility [F10]. It provides a mechanism through which TIFIN may monetize an institutional account, **not five years of observed cash transfers from a named sponsor**.

GiveClear Foundation's **2024 IRS Form 990** reports:

- **$214,891,040 total revenue**, of which **$210,477,594 (97.9%) is charitable contributions** [F07]. Therefore **$214.9m is NOT TIFIN company revenue**, a sponsor admin fee stream, or software ARR.
- Approximately **$1.6m of investment management fees**, **$142k of management fees**, and **$21k of information technology expense**, among other 990 expense categories [F08]. **The public Form 990 extracts do not identify TIFIN as payee**, so none of these categories may be assigned to TIFIN without more evidence.
- **228 DAF accounts** with **about $215.3m DAF assets** at FY2024 end according to Schedule D data [F08]. This is not 228 paying *technology customers* or a 2024 TIFIN adviser assets figure.

GiveClear also self-reported **$372m** sponsor assets, **$83.2m** grants and **16,249 grants** in its 2025 program report, and names TIFIN Give as technology partner [F09]. Those figures describe a sponsor, not a vendor's revenue or net retention. The source site's links to 2024 audited statements and 990 PDF returned HTTP 403 during this review; the available 2024 IRS-derived extracts were examined instead. **No TIFIN-specific paid contract value has been established from them.**

A 2024 zero or negligible *program service revenue* entry in a sponsor's Form 990 must also not be read as evidence that the technology company generated zero revenue: its contracts and separate legal accounting are not visible in that line [F07,F10].

## 3. Chariot: pricing ≠ transactions realized

Chariot's current public nonprofit terms and product pricing describe a default **2.9% DAFpay fee** under specific nonprofit/fundraising integration conditions, subject to separate signed agreements and possible subscriptions [F12,F13]. This is a **gross published contractual fee rate**, not net recognized revenue. We have **no observed distribution of customers across plan tiers, processed donation counts by actual fee schedule, refunds, gross-to-net adjustments, cancellations, payment-bank costs or retained subscription cohorts**.

The distinct Chariot **Disbursements** service charges grantmakers under a separate product contract; **do not apply the nonprofit's DAFpay 2.9% fee to grantmaker-paid Disbursements**, nor infer that its partner TIFIN uses the same pricing [F12,F13]. Thus Chariot has potentially recurring/usage-based revenue mechanisms, not audited customer retention evidence here.

## 4. Why AEF does not yet provide a Ren migration cost

AEF's 2024 Form 990 includes a reported **$2.3m information technology expense** [F11], but this is **FY2024**, before AEF publicly selected Ren in **July 2025**. It cannot be attributed to Ren or called the cost of the migration. AEF's 2025/2026 platform change, with limits on historical self-service access, establishes real technical portability consequences in our [switching analysis](revenue-retention-and-switching-2026-11-11.md), but no financial migration expenditure has been demonstrated.

## 6. Request list ranked by information value

### Ren
1. Provide separate legal entity audited financials or trial balance of Ren/Renaissance Acquisition Company LLC and a GAAP bridge reconciling RCF's **$19,225,546 FY2024 payment** to recognized company revenues, receivables and cash settlements.
2. Provide the signed RCF Administrative Services Agreement, amendments changing the percentage **from 95% to 98% in FY2023**, and any renewal, exclusivity, fee tier, related-party approval, exit, and staffing obligations.
3. Disclose payments from *other institutional sponsors* separately from RCF, removing related-party double counting and reconciling intra-group transactions.
4. Provide customer contract cohorts for 2022–2026, logo retention, same-customer GRR/NRR, fixed versus AUM-based and usage-linked fees, customer concentration and gross profit after human grant/accounting/support costs.
5. Explain if the RCF fee-share arrangement is assignable or terminable when a sponsor changes its technology provider, and compare to independent sponsor deals such as AEF.

### TIFIN Give
1. Provide current 2026 adviser Form ADV and signed GiveClear/SEI service and SaaS agreements, including payer entity, sponsor fees, asset billable base and payment waterfall.
2. Reconcile the firm's recognized administration/investment/software revenue to **specific named institutional contracts** and to GiveClear's 2024 990 expense disclosures, **without assigning unverified payees**.
3. Disclose renewal and concentration cohorts by sponsor, wealth firm and module, and the cost to serve a multi-custodian charity program.
4. Document investment-model sponsorship economics and independent control over recommendations, with actual financial materiality.

### Chariot
1. Provide paid nonprofit and grantmaker cohorts separated into **DAFpay**, subscriptions, Disbursements and technology integrations; who pays each fee and who absorbs pass-through payment costs?
2. Reconcile default **2.9% pricing** to actual blended gross/net DAFpay fee take for the same transaction date range, with plan/partner overrides, rebates, refunds and donated amounts.
3. Disclose renewal, grantmaker and nonprofit concentration, churn and total payment operations cost per successful, *actually received* grant.

### Across all
- Executed agreements, five years of dated invoices, renewals, termination notices, credits, adoption and historical data exports.
- A **contract by unique paying legal customer** dataset with gross and net retention bridge. Do not infer churn from sponsor assets or donor grant payout.
- A reproducible real migration run and full grant/ledger reconciliation, with authorized benchmarking rights.
- No unpublished employer, client or paid expert network information should be added to this public repository.

## 7. Proposed research decision

**Priority 1:** Use the **five-year audited RCF→Ren payments** as a verified counterparty-specific contract-economics case study, not as all-company Ren ARR.  
**Priority 2:** Work backward from GiveClear's IRS data and TIFIN's adviser disclosures to determine whether any real TIFIN vendor-fee counterparty statement exists publicly; so far we cannot identify it.  
**Priority 3:** Continue to look for financial and contract evidence that separates Chariot's nonprofit and grantmaker revenue lines.  
**Priority 4:** Hold all vendor-level customer retention/NRR/enterprise contract valuation decisions pending signed actual cohorts, not modeled proxies.
