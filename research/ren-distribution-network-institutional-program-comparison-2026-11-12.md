## Architecture and commercial models
**Model A, sponsor plus branded distributor:** RCF is legal sponsor; Ren provides technology and operations; Edward Jones, Northwestern Mutual, Ameriprise or advisers in an LPL channel distribute a branded DAF to clients. Do not assume identical pricing or exclusivity.
**Model B, independent sponsor plus Ren administration:** Morgan Stanley GIFT has its own sponsoring nonprofit and uses RenPSG as back-office administrator; do not label it an RCF-sponsored fund.
**Model C, private/high-net-worth offering:** Edward Jones announced a private DAF; sponsor and technology not publicly verified. Keep separate.

## What a shared vendor could mean
1. **Economies of scale:** Common software, charity status data, compliance workflows, payments and donor portals can spread fixed costs. This is a hypothesis; no internal cost evidence.
2. **Concentration:** Multiple wealth brands may depend on the same administrator, service organization or vendor stack. The economic and operational exposure depends on actual shared infrastructure and business continuity plans, not merely a common logo.
3. **Client relationship continuity:** Advisers can stay involved in philanthropic planning while sponsor owns charitable assets; do not equate with retaining personal AUM.
4. **Switching costs:** Account and grant history, approval provenance, charity records, adviser authorizations and payout reconciliation may be difficult to migrate. No specific Ren migration failure has been established.
5. **Regulatory authority:** Sponsoring charity retains grant approval authority even when vendor vets and prepares a recommendation.

## Clarity: claims requiring correction
- 'All Ren clients are RCF-sponsored': **false as a generalization**, Morgan Stanley GIFT is a distinct sponsor.
- 'Edward Jones portal redirects to Ren, therefore Ren approves grants': **not established**; RCF retains final authority.
- 'Every LPL adviser uses Ren': **not supported** by a channel onboarding page.
- 'All Ren programs have the same fees': **not supported**; branded program circulars vary.
- 'Ren's asset coverage is Ren revenue': **incorrect**; service fees, sponsor assets, custody, adviser fees and managed assets differ.
- 'One platform means one shared database': **not established** without architecture evidence.
- 'Private Edward Jones DAF is Ren-powered': **unverified**.

## Strategic conclusion
Ren's documented relationships show **multiple go-to-market models**: a repeatable RCF-sponsored white-label DAF program and back-office administration for independent institutional sponsors. This is stronger evidence of its distribution strategy than aggregate marketing claims about assets served. It does **not** establish monopoly power, profit, automation quality or exclusivity.
