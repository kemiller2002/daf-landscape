## Synthesis

### The strongest verified incumbent weaknesses

These are VERIFIED-LIMITATION, but none is shown to change purchasing decisions.

1. **Migration is painful for donors and staff, on every vendor.**
   - User-visible freezes: CNY ~3.5 weeks; Vermont ~9 weeks for recommendations; GWCF ~5.5 weeks; AEF 3.5 weeks.
   - Credentials reset in every notice reviewed.
   - In-flight drafts were deleted (AEF 2024).
   - History was truncated, for example to 2 years at AEF.
2. **Exit terms in public templates are narrow.**
   - iPhi: 15 days of access after termination.
   - NPact: data deleted 10 business days after hosting ends; export is a paid service; 3-year term with 90-day notice.
   - Ren RAC: returns "Customer Materials" only.
   - Foundant: CSV "as stored".
3. **Ren's iPhi installed base is leaking to CF specialists.** 5 of 28 iPhi community foundations left, and one faith sponsor (WaterStone) left to Give Interactive.
4. **Ren publishes inconsistent scale and assurance claims.**
5. **Fragmented legacy stacks.** This is the only weakness that sponsors themselves state as the reason they switched (Vermont, CNY, Arizona).

### The strongest incumbent defenses

1. **Distribution economics.**
   - RCF passes 98% of fundholder admin fees to Ren (audited).
   - Firms receive revenue sharing (Commonwealth, reported).
   - TIFIN pays DAF advisers 0–100 bps in subadvisory fees.
   - Morgan Stanley receives $4.24M as investment adviser to MS GIFT.
2. **Distributor programs cannot be "migrated".** Their assets belong to the vendor-affiliated charity (RCF, Charityvest, GiveClear, NPT). Moving them means grant-by-grant transfers that the incumbent sponsor must approve.
3. **Contract mechanics:** auto-renewal, notice periods and fees to the end of the term.
4. **Switching cost sits on the client side:** staff time, consultants costing $100k–700k+ a year, and dual-run fees (JCF paid $417,742).
5. **Incumbents keep transforming.**
   - Foundant wins nearly every observed CF switch.
   - TIFIN and Chariot ship AI features.
   - Foundation Source and Ren raise capital and acquire.
   - The prior replication analysis estimates each single lever can be copied in about 6–24 months.

### What the switch record says

These points describe **observed behaviour**:
- About 30 platform-level institutional switches were found for 2015–2026, almost all at **community foundations** and **faith/single-issue sponsors**.
- Destinations were mostly Foundant, then Give Interactive, GiftingNetwork, akoyaGO, Salesforce builds and Ren (AEF).
- National sponsors on Ren rarely leave: 1 of 26 faith or national iPhi sponsors left between 2023 and 2026. NPT, JPM and ICGF moved deeper into Ren.
- Advisor channels **add** providers rather than replace them:
  - SEI kept its Ren program while adding TIFIN;
  - Confluence works with multiple sponsors;
  - Commonwealth stayed on RCF/Ren through the LPL conversion.
- Vendor-forced events open switching windows: Blackbaud's sale of FIMS in 2022 dispersed customers to Foundant and NPact.
- Stated reasons for leaving are **integration** (Vermont, CNY), **vendor deadlines** (Arizona) and generic "best-in-class technology" (AEF). Price, exit terms and charity networks are never cited.

These points are **inference**: why institutions stay.
- Aligned incentives, through revenue sharing and fee pass-through.
- Aversion to risk, given the documented freezes and resets.
- The legal structure of distributor programs.
- Template contract terms.

No public win/loss or exit-interview data exists. Every causal claim is therefore HYPOTHESIS until it is tested with buyers.

- The ownership of NPact, GiftingNetwork and akoyaGO was not established.
- No SOC report was inspected for any vendor.
- The Commonwealth revenue-share figure comes from a search-index snippet only.
- GTCR's ownership of Foundation Source rests on deal-database listings.
- Bonterra's ownership (Apax) is recalled and was not re-verified.
- Switch dates marked INFERENCE come from DNS evidence or from the dates on notices.
