## 2. Underserved segments (positive evidence only)

Only three segments show **positive** evidence of motion, and all three are switching, not unmet demand: sponsors replacing iPhi or legacy FIMS (SEG-012), community foundations (SEG-002) and faith foundations (SEG-010). In each, the observed destination is an existing vendor with an integrated product. No segment shows demand for a new DAF-only platform. The absence of visible competitors in credit unions is **not** evidence of a gap: credit unions show no member DAF demand and the one prior white-label attempt (Amicus) was redirected to advisors.

## 4. Revenue potential and concentration (indicative)

At $81-150 per account (software) a 10k-account sponsor is worth $0.8-1.5M a year; at 20 bps and $250k balances, $5M a year for administration. Any first customer is >25% of revenue for years (CNV-ECO-004 concentration cap breached by every single-sponsor start; PR #88). Concentration risk is therefore unavoidable in every route and must be priced into terms (prepaid implementation, minimum fees).
