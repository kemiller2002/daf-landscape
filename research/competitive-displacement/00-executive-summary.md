# 00 - Executive summary: competitive displacement and structural economic advantage

Status: research (2026-11-26), reconciled after ten independent adversarial reviews. Public sources and the owner's repositories only; no interviews, logins or outreach. **Not legal, tax or investment advice; not a launch authorisation.** Economic figures come from a reproducible F# model with labelled assumptions; none is measured. Authors, reviewers and integrator are the same model family, so nothing here is independent validation.

## The answer in six sentences

1. **An AI-native entrant can run DAF administration at a much lower variable cost than conventional administration, but not at a fundamentally lower total cost**: break-even at 20 bps is about $1.9B operating, $2.3B fully loaded and $2.5B at the Monte Carlo median, and against a well-automated incumbent the advantage is only about $11-71 per account-year.
2. **AI is not the engine**: about 6% of the modelled saving comes from AI; deterministic workflow, exception design and prevention supply the rest, and incumbents can adopt all three.
3. **Distribution, not technology, is the binding barrier**: incumbent revenue is shared with the people who decide purchases (RCF passes ~98% of fundholder fees to Ren), only 1 of 26 national and faith sponsors left Ren's iPhi platform in 2023-26, advisor channels add providers rather than replace them, and no observed switch cites a technical weakness an entrant could exploit.
4. **Unbound's ratified entry plan (OD-M-05) targets the least mobile segment with the most capital-intensive vehicle**, so the entry path is not supported by the evidence, although the product destination remains sound.
5. **No candidate advantage compounds as a network effect**: the charity directory and the BMF archive are public (NCCS), paid eligibility evidence is a commodity (Candid from $2,750 a year), migration commitments are copyable clauses, and incumbents lead on learning economies.
6. **The cheapest credible route is partner-first licensing**: $0.15-0.35M of validation, a $1.0-1.5M partner pilot, then about $6.5M (P50; P90 ~$9M) to a self-sustaining niche business, with explicit kill rules, a 37% chance of no payback within ten years and no venture-scale outcome in the base case.

## Tracks

- **A. Charity intelligence beyond the BMF** ([02](02-charity-intelligence-sources.md)-[04](04-charity-intelligence-architecture.md)). Thirty-eight new sources documented, for 68 in total. Free, commercially usable bulk sources (trade.gov CSL, FCDO UK Sanctions List replacing the closed OFSI list, Federal Audit Clearinghouse, SAM exclusions) extend screening, but free data covers **status facts, not the full eligibility determination**. ProPublica, GivingTuesday, OpenSanctions and EMMA cannot be redistributed. Nine evidence classes keep AI and inferred records advisory; screening results stay sponsor-scoped. Re-vetting can verify change instead of repeating work, and the 180-day cycle is a policy, not a legal rule.
- **B. Registry economics** ([05](05-charity-registry-economics.md)). The $316k reconstructs exactly; 39% is a 1 FTE staffing assumption. The lowest defensible run cost is ~$90-170k for one sponsor and ~$18-42k per sponsor at ten sponsors. The registry is not a barrier and not a product.
- **C. Competitive displacement** ([06](06-competitive-displacement.md)-[07](07-institutional-switching-incentives.md)). Sixteen competitors, 25 classified weaknesses and 44 switch events are analysed. Verified weaknesses are migration pain and narrow template exit terms. Ren's CF losses are normal churn. Switching happens at community and faith foundations at forced or integration-driven windows, mostly to Foundant. Satisfied buyers stay for people, audit, board and vendor-viability reasons.
- **D. Onboarding** ([08](08-frictionless-onboarding.md)). A standard, a guarantee and a clock make switching routine elsewhere. A level 0-5 maturity model is proposed. Pre-contract work runs T0 with no install, T1 after IT approval, and T2+ under MSA and DPA. A migration assessment is customer-acquisition cost, not a product. Full history needs a contracted incumbent export.
- **E. Event-level economics** ([09](09-event-level-operating-model.md)-[10](10-zero-touch-administration-economics.md)). The model covers 25 events and 7 operating modes. Grant approval and vetting are 46% of conventional cost. Failures are ~19% of AI-native cost, so prevention beats speed. Fixed cost and balance, not labour efficiency, set viability.
- **F. Distribution and segments** ([11](11-institutional-distribution.md)-[12](12-market-entry-segments.md)). Fourteen segments are covered. Positive motion appears only in re-platforming segments. Credit unions show no member DAF demand. RIAs are price-compressed. Small DAF technology firms get acquired, not licensed.
- **G. Compounding advantages** ([13](13-compounding-competitive-advantages.md)). There are no network effects. Learning and scale economies are modest and incumbents lead them.
- **H. Enterprise models** ([14](14-alternative-enterprise-models.md)). Licensing is the cheapest self-sustaining mode and the least sensitive to productivity assumptions, but it is still regulated: a platform creating ACH files is a TPSP, and SOC 1 and SOC 2 apply. Echelon Foundry need not become a conventional administrator.

## Recommendations

See [16](16-commercial-entry-strategy.md), [22](22-commercial-validation-plan.md), [23](23-executive-decision-register.md) and [24](24-final-recommendations.md). In priority order:
1. **Test the thesis cheaply first**: channel-partner conversations (V-6), win/loss with 5 switchers **and 5 stayers** (V-1), and confirmed active evaluations (V-5), within 12 weeks.
2. **Reopen OD-M-05** with this evidence (DEC-001) and adopt gated funding with a kill rule (DEC-008).
3. **Enter partner-first as a licensor**; keep full administration behind a signed anchor (DEC-002).
4. **Re-order product priorities**: licensing mode, sponsor-scoped screening, prevention-first inputs, Transitus phase 1, process-only SLAs.
5. **Do not build** a payment network, charity portal requirement, standalone charity-intelligence product, paid assessment business or CF fund-accounting suite.
