## 2. What would cause an institutional customer to leave Ren? (Q3)

| Trigger | Status | Segment where it operates |
|---|---|---|
| Need for an **integrated platform** (accounting, grantmaking, donor services) | **Observed, stated** (Vermont, CNY, Arizona; CLM-016) | Community foundations; some faith sponsors |
| **Vendor-forced event** (product sale, re-hosting, end of support) | Observed (Blackbaud FIMS sale; NPact Azure re-host; Arizona "90-day crunch") | CFs and sponsors on legacy stacks |
| Sponsor builds or owns its own platform | Observed (Signatry → Give Interactive; Vanguard Charitable) | Large national/faith sponsors with capital |
| Channel partner adds a second provider | Observed (SEI) — additive, not a departure | Wealth platforms |
| Price, exit terms, history loss, SOC inconsistencies | **Not observed as stated reasons** | — |
| Leadership change, board review, contract end | Plausible (Arkansas leadership change noted in prior research) | All; unmeasured |

**Answer.** A Ren customer leaves when **its scope need outgrows a DAF-only product** (CFs wanting integrated fund accounting), when **a vendor event forces a migration anyway**, or when **it owns an alternative**. Where Ren's economics are entangled with the customer's revenue (RCF, advisor programs), departures are rare.

## 4. Who decides, who feels the pain

The people who experience the documented pain (donors, charities, operations staff during freezes; compliance during audits) are not the people who control the purchase (sponsor executive and board; in advisor programmes, the wealth firm). The controllers are paid by the incumbent arrangement in advisor and RCF-type programmes. **Solving user pain does not, on present evidence, change purchasing behaviour** except where the pain is fragmentation felt by the sponsor's own staff.

## 5. Exit costs in adjacent markets

Core banking shows exit pricing used as a lock-in lever (Jack Henry deconversion revenue $33.9M FY2025, doubled; credit unions litigating Fiserv exit fees; interagency guidance requiring orderly transition "without prohibitive expense"; CLM-033). DAF templates show the same direction (paid export, short data windows). A published, symmetric exit price is a cheap credibility signal for an entrant, but there is no DAF evidence that it wins deals (W04).

## 6. Implication for the commercial thesis

- The **ratified Unbound entry segment** (OD-M-05) sits where switching is least observed (CLM-027).
- The **segment that does switch** (CFs, faith) prefers integrated suites and was rejected by Unbound on economics (median 46 DAFs per CF).
- Therefore a direct-displacement strategy has no proven entry point. The commercial strategy must create a reason to choose that does not yet exist in the record, or enter through a channel that already controls switching ([16](16-commercial-entry-strategy.md)).
