## Five-year audited RCF → Ren fee series
| Fiscal year ending Dec 31 | RCF fee share paid to Ren | Audited payments | YoY payment change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2020 | 95% | $7,514,718 | n/a |
| 2021 | 95% | $10,880,690 | +44.79% |
| 2022 | 95% | $13,490,137 | +23.98% |
| 2023 | 98% | $15,543,682 | +15.22% |
| 2024 | 98% | $19,225,546 | +23.69% |

**Sources:**
- 2021/2020 audited Note 9 p17: https://renaissancecharitable.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2021RCFAuditedFinancialStatement.pdf
- 2022/2021 audited Note 10 p17: https://renaissancecharitable.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2022RCFAuditedFinancialStatements.pdf
- 2024/2023 audited Note 9 p17: https://renaissancecharitable.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2024RCFAuditedFinancialStatements.pdf
- RCF source directory: https://renaissancecharitable.org/legal-resources/

**Interpretation:** Payment rose from $7.515m to $19.226m, a 2.56× multiple over four annual intervals (roughly 26.5% compound annual growth). This is growth in **one sponsor's related-party administrative payments**, not Ren's company-wide revenue growth, EBITDA, or software ARR. The disclosed 95%→98% change occurred after 2022 and by 2023. The audit does not identify amendment date or reason. Avoid attributing payment growth solely to fee-share change; asset levels, donor accounts, pricing and other service arrangements may explain much more.

**Governance:** The audits state RCF and Ren are related parties, with two Ren employees on RCF's board. A related-party transaction is a disclosure category, not proof of impropriety. Assess independent trustee oversight, pricing fairness, conflict policy and termination rights with the governing documents and Form 990 Schedule L.

## Independent data portability observation: AEF
AEF's public transition notice: https://engage.aefonline.org/dc-sunset/
- AEF moved from DonorCentral to Ren's DAF platform.
- DonorCentral access ended Oct 31, 2026.
- New donor portal displays **two years** of account history; older grants/gifts must be requested from AEF.
- **Observed user-access limitation**, not proof of missing archival data, bad migration, or inability to export.
- Distinguish migration into Ren from the cost or feasibility of migrating away from Ren.

Ren's published services: https://www.reninc.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Ren-Inc.-Product-Descriptions-v.1.pdf
- Lists SSO setup, data migration onto DFX, API integration and branding as add-ons.
- Does **not** establish sponsor-owned complete outbound data exports, approval provenance portability, termination pricing, SLA or recovery guarantees.

## Technical exit-readiness test (unexecuted)
Request a contractually authorized **full-fidelity export** including:
- Legal donor, sponsor and adviser identities, permissions, historical account ownership/authorization changes.
- Contributions and tax receipt provenance, transaction timestamps, assets, investment NAVs and balances.
- Grant recommendation text, purpose, recipient identity, IRS snapshot, sanctions and policy version, exception investigation, staff approval, override and appeal history.
- Liquidity requests and adviser handoffs; disbursement instructions, payment confirmation, returned/reissued payments and recipient acknowledgment.
- Supporting files, messages, audit logs, IDs, relationships and integrity hashes.
- Explicit retention schedule, archival access, deletion/return obligations, read-only transition window, RTO/RPO, migration test and reconciliation.
Success criteria: account and grant totals reconcile; no orphaned identity/payment references; immutable audit history verifiable; sample donor histories display correctly; critical sponsor approvals can be reconstructed. **No evidence yet Ren fails these tests.**

## Concentration and competitive analysis
- RCF's 98% formula indicates its operating service model allocates nearly all **specified fundholder administrative fee revenue** to Ren; this is not 98% of assets, all sponsor revenue, or investment fees.
- RCF is one channel; Ren also serves independent sponsors such as Morgan Stanley GIFT. Do not infer same terms.
- White-label distribution through Edward Jones, Northwestern Mutual, Ameriprise and LPL-related programs may lower acquisition costs and improve access to adviser relationships; not quantified.
- Sponsor-level platform migration has material historical-data and governance considerations; AEF is a concrete example of limited donor-visible history after migration.
- The strongest differentiated competitor might offer **verifiable exportability and policy-decision provenance** alongside workflows, but only if sponsors value and pay for these capabilities. Treat as market hypothesis.

## Clarity counterfactuals
1. Payments growing ≠ software margins growing; outsourced labor may rise proportionally.
2. Higher fee share ≠ market power; contract could reflect shifted services and costs.
3. Related-party board seats ≠ wrongdoing; require independent approval evidence.
4. AEF two-year portal history ≠ destroyed data; archived records remain available on request.
5. Migration add-on ≠ proven exit portability.
6. Historical 2024 RCF amounts ≠ current 2026 Ren financial performance.
