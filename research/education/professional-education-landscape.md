## 2. Readings [I]

1. **DAF education is dominated by sponsors and their adjacent networks.** Examples:
   - Fidelity Charitable's free CE has reached 31,000+ advisers.
   - The DAFCP lineage and Forum are tied to sponsors, and Ren's CEO wrote the required textbook's foreword.
   - Fidelity Charitable commissions donor-AI research from authors who work at vendors.
   - Sponsorship is not evidence of improper influence. It does mean the field's education is produced by parties with an interest in how DAFs are framed.
2. **Two kinds of education teach AI, and neither is DAF-specific:**
   - **tool operation** (Lilly, PG Calc, CGP, Exponent, AFP);
   - **governance and risk** (CFP Board, FINRA, Kitces, TAG, Fundraising.AI).

   No programme was found that teaches the redesign of DAF or grant operations around AI.
3. **Wealth-management bodies are ahead on AI governance.** FINRA RN 24-09, the CFP Board guide and Kitces compliance articles carry supervision, record-keeping and model-risk concepts. DAF education has not imported them. Advisers who hold both CFP and DAFCP learn AI governance from the CFP side only.
4. **Reinforcing established practice.** Curricula across the field share the same pattern:
   - they teach DAFs as tax and estate vehicles that sit inside existing sponsor systems;
   - they leave out fees, payout distributions, portability and operations ([gap analysis](curriculum-industry-gap-analysis.md));
   - they treat AI as a productivity or donor-access tool.

   That pattern reinforces incumbent operating models. It does not show deliberate intent [I].
