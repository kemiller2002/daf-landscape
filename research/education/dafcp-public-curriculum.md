# DAFCP public curriculum, reconstructed by domain

**Date:** 2026-11-20.

**Sources:**
- the programme page (SS01);
- the launch release (SS02);
- the 2025 agenda, used only as context for the predecessor (SS05);
- the publisher's description of the textbook named in the addendum (register §2).

The syllabus (SS04) was not read: robots.txt disallows it.

**Coverage labels:**
- **Explicit:** a module bullet names the topic.
- **Implied:** a module bullet plausibly covers it, but does not name it.
- **Not in public text:** absent from every public description. This does **not** mean the course omits it.
- **Textbook theme:** named only in the textbook's seller description.

The "what a modern curriculum should cover" column is the repository's judgement [I]. It is supported in [daf-professional-knowledge-guide.md](daf-professional-knowledge-guide.md) and [curriculum-industry-gap-analysis.md](curriculum-industry-gap-analysis.md).

**The six public modules:**
1. Fundamentals.
2. Tax Fundamentals.
3. Gift Acceptance.
4. Investing and Grant Management.
5. Estate Planning and Planned Giving.
6. Ethics, Compliance and Technology.

The verbatim bullets are in [03-curriculum-and-competency-audit.md](../dafcp-investigation-2026-11-20/03-curriculum-and-competency-audit.md) §1.

**Reading [I].** Public coverage is densest in fundamentals, tax and estate planning. It thins where the work is operational: administration, payments, custody, adviser-managed assets, privacy and automation. Those are the domains the repository has researched most heavily. The two bodies of knowledge are complementary, not overlapping.
