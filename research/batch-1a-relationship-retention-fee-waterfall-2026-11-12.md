# Batch 1A: DAF relationship continuity, sponsor mandates and net-fee economics
**Research date:** 2026-11-12
**Execution register:** R02, R03, R04, M01, M02 (partial). **Status:** Evidence collected, causal retention claim remains unproven.
**Scope:** Primary public sponsor policies, not private agreements or actual Pave customer contracts.

## Executive conclusion
An adviser can continue serving a donor after charitable contribution, but the *type* of continuity depends on sponsor approval and account economics. Open-architecture AEF and approved independent adviser programs at Fidelity Charitable, DAFgiving360 and NPT establish real institutional routes. These are **not** evidence that Pave has sponsor integration, that all client DAF assets remain adviser-managed, or that retention improves.

**Novel strategic finding:** DAFs are not one relationship-retention product. They create (a) planning continuity with no investment mandate, (b) sponsor-approved investment continuity, and (c) family/successor continuity. These must be measured separately. Some firms can retain the relationship while losing both assets and revenue; others may preserve a subset of advisory fees through sponsor contracts.

## Fee continuity: test the *net* effect, not headline DAF AUM
**Illustrative household:** $2m taxable assets with RIA at 0.75% annually. Donor contributes $500k in-kind to a DAF. No investment return, other flows, or fees assumed. Numbers are scenario inputs, not market averages.

Before donation: $2m × 0.75% = **$15,000/year** client-owned investment advisory revenue.

**Scenario A: no approved sponsor investment mandate**
- Personal AUM $1.5m × 0.75% = $11,250.
- DAF adviser investment revenue = $0.
- Annual advisory fee change **-$3,750** (-25%); planning relationship may remain.

**Scenario B: approved $500k sponsor mandate at 0.50%**
- Personal AUM fee $11,250 + DAF mandate fee $2,500 = $13,750.
- Annual advisory fee change **-$1,250** (-8.3%); relationship and some investment economics continue.

**Scenario C: approved $500k sponsor mandate at 0.75%**
- Personal AUM fee $11,250 + DAF mandate fee $3,750 = $15,000.
- Fee-neutral **before** additional sponsor fees, custodian expenses, compliance and service cost. A sponsor may reject the fee or account.

**Scenario D: sponsor mandate $500k at 1%, only if permitted by sponsor and agreement**
- Personal AUM $11,250 + DAF mandate fee $5,000 = $16,250, +$1,250.
- DAFgiving360 publishes a **maximum** 1% adviser fee, not evidence 1% is typical, appropriate or obtainable. Sponsor administrative fee and investments costs additionally reduce charitable assets.

**Critical caveat:** Household fee changes above are **RIA gross advisory revenue**, not donor financial benefit or net profitability. For the donor, donation irrevocably transfers $500k away; fees charged to charitable assets reduce funds available for future grants. The appropriate objective is charitable and household welfare, not fee retention.

## A second economic mechanism: relationship-only continuity
Adviser can retain annual planning engagement and trust even without any DAF investment fee. Its commercial value requires measurable changes in client attrition, referrals, next-generation engagement, and willingness to pay for planning. None established here. If $500k leaves the original fee base, an adviser must not describe retained relationship as retained AUM.

## Competitive positioning and Pave Finance
Pave may serve **approved RIA firms** in tax-lot selection before the gift and sponsor-compliant investment management after the gift. Its portfolio optimization software is a plausible *enabler*, not an independent DAF sponsor or grant processor. Core differentiators to test:
1. Cross-account household planning and contribution-lot selection.
2. Transition workflow and legal ownership change without confusing custody with sponsor authority.
3. Investment policy, liquidity for expected grants and fee transparency on sponsor-owned mandates.
4. Successor and family adviser engagement subject to donor consent.
5. Reports showing separate client-owned AUM, sponsor-owned adviser-managed balances, sponsor fees, RIA fees and net charitable grants.

**Competition hypothesis:** Most adviser-managed DAF programs already allow conventional portfolio management. Pave must demonstrate *incremental* value versus current RIA portfolio software and sponsor portals, not merely the ability to manage a DAF.

## Clarity adversarial tests
- **Claim: DAF integration increases retention.** Not demonstrated. Loyal/high-wealth households may self-select into philanthropic planning. Need matched longitudinal cohorts and attrition events.
- **Claim: all donated assets can be retained under management.** False. Sponsor eligibility, approved adviser, custody, grants and asset liquidity limit mandates.
- **Claim: sponsor fee is RIA revenue.** False. Sponsor administration, advisory and underlying investment fees accrue to different legal entities.
- **Claim: Pave's custodian integrations authorize DAF mandates.** False inference; sponsor approval separate.
- **Claim: adviser-managed DAF is always best for donors.** Not established; higher fees, restricted investment choices and charitable mission may favor pooled funds.
- **Claim: 1% maximum is the expected adviser fee.** False inference; published ceiling is not observed fee.
- **Claim: AEF's $25k opening minimum is a $25k external-management minimum.** Not proven.

## EDF diagnosis
**Structural handoff:** client account→gift transfer→sponsor-owned account→investment authority→grant liquidity→recipient.
**Failure mechanisms:** asset ownership confusion, sponsor permission missing, fee stacking, grant-funded forced sales, investment mandate mismatch, adviser conflict, heir privacy and successor access.
**Controls:** sponsor approval registry, fee waterfall, account status, custodian/sponsor reconciliation, separate household/sponsor AUM reporting, donor consent, written conflict disclosure, grant liquidity stress test.
**Outcomes:** donor goals and grant execution first; then client retention, net fee economics and adviser service cost.

2. **R04/M01:** Extract audited and 990 related-party fees for Vanguard, Fidelity, DAFgiving360, NPT, Morgan Stanley GIFT and AEF; reconcile reporting periods and fee beneficiaries.
3. **R02/M02:** Collect actual RIA fee schedules and sponsor-authorized fee contracts to replace scenario rates; compare revenue and costs, not just assets.
4. **R01:** Acquire permissioned anonymized CRM cohorts for 12/24/36-month retention and matched control groups; absent data, state causal effect unknown.
5. **Pave:** Obtain Pave's actual pricing and SEC investment-adviser status, distinguish software SaaS from regulated advisory services, test two design partners.
6. **Conflict review:** Interview donors and advisers about whether investment retention is a reason for DAF recommendations and how disclosures affect trust.

## Decision
**Proceed with investigation of RIA-led DAF continuity.** **Do not** yet assert that DAFs cause higher client retention, that Pave has a viable enterprise sponsor contract, or that retaining investment mandates produces net value after all costs.
