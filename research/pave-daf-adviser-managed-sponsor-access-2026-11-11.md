# Pave Finance: DAF investment-management access and distribution channels
Date: 2026-11-11
Status: Primary-source sponsor policy comparison; commercial partnership and Pave eligibility unverified.

## Executive finding
Multiple DAF sponsors already authorize qualified external advisers to manage sponsor-owned assets. Pave's likely initial buyer is **an eligible RIA using Pave**, rather than a DAF donor or sponsor directly. Sponsor permission, custody and advisory agreement are separate requirements. Do not treat charitable assets as Pave AUM.

## Crucial differences
**DAF sponsor legal control** is not donor ownership. Donor recommendation is not investment-manager authorization. A Pave-supported adviser must have independent authorization, meet policy/custody rules, and disclose conflicts.
**Fee stacking**: Sponsor administration, RIA adviser fee, Pave SaaS/technology fees, custody, and underlying fund costs must be separated. Whether Pave's license is borne by RIA or sponsor affects incentives.
**Donor portfolio tax optimization**: Tax-lot selection is useful before donation; after donation, the charitable sponsor owns assets and has a different tax profile. Standard taxable-account tax-loss harvesting is not automatically valuable inside the DAF. Pave's differentiation may be contribution selection, transition, grant-liquidity forecasting and household-level reporting rather than DAF tax-loss harvesting.
**Existing channels**: Fidelity and Schwab integrations for RIAs do not imply Fidelity Charitable or DAFgiving360 API or program eligibility.

## Market-entry tests
A. RIA-led: Pave sells existing portfolio tools to RIA firms, adds DAF-aware lot-selection, donation-in-kind workflow and sponsor-specific rules. Validate advisor demand and software value.
B. Sponsor investment-operations: Pave provides portfolio rebalancing/mandate monitoring to qualified external advisers managing sponsor assets. Test liquidity, prohibited investments, custody, performance reporting.
C. Sponsor enterprise white-label: Sell sponsor direct. Need existing sponsor procurement pain, investment-policy authority and competing solution analysis.
D. Sponsor launch/acquisition: Defer, as governance and charitable operations are non-core and may erode neutral software position.

## Quantitative example (hypothetical)
100 RIAs × 10 eligible DAF accounts each × $250k average eligible account = $250m potential *advisor-managed DAF balance*. At 5 bps technology revenue = $125k gross annual, but no evidence 5 bps is Pave's price or that this many accounts are reachable. If Pave charges SaaS per adviser instead, balance-based TAM is misleading. Model advisory fees separately and do not add same dollars to multiple fee pools.

## Clarity challenges and findings
- 'All major sponsors lock out independent advisers': refuted by AEF, Fidelity, DAFgiving360, NPT.
- 'Pave can manage $130b of DAF assets': false inference; company-reported assets associated with Pave users are not addressable sponsor assets.
- 'DAF is a tax-loss harvesting opportunity': generally misframed; optimize before contribution and portfolio constraints after sponsor acceptance.
- 'Vanguard is a good direct distribution partner': not yet supported; published program favors sponsor pools and affiliated services.
- 'A lower outside-manager threshold means lower customer acquisition cost': unproven; small accounts may be uneconomic.
- 'RIA retention improves with DAF': plausible but causal evidence and fee cannibalization must be tested.
