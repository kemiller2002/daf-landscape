## B. What AI actually changes

**Superficial productivity (speeds work up; does not change who is accountable or what work exists):** drafting donor and adviser communications, grant-status answers, document extraction for complex assets, reconciliation-break triage, and proposal generation. Incumbents already market these. ([Fidelity Charitable virtual-assistant terms](https://www.fidelitycharitable.org/legal/virtual-assistant.html); [TIFIN Give Louise release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/tifin-give-launches-louise-ai-capabilities-for-philanthropy-302595069.html); [Chariot gift processing launch](https://givechariot.com/resources/insights/chariot-launches-first-gift-processing-platform-for-nonprofits))

**Structural changes (alter operating economics only when paired with redesign):**
1. **Policy-as-code.** Sponsor grant policy becomes versioned, executable and back-testable against historical grants, so a final §4966 guidance change can be re-run across the book.
2. **Continuous eligibility monitoring.** Every active payee is checked against BMF/Pub 78/revocation/OFAC refreshes, replacing per-grant lookups. ([IRS EO BMF extract](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/exempt-organizations-business-master-file-extract-eo-bmf); [IRS TEOS bulk data downloads](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/tax-exempt-organization-search-bulk-data-downloads); [IRS Auto-Revocation List](https://www.irs.gov/autorevocationlist); [OFAC Framework for Compliance Commitments](https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/16331/download)) Diligence moves off the grant's critical path.
3. **Exception-only human review.** Each routing decision records which rule or person decided.
4. **Event-sourced ledger.** Grant status becomes one state machine shared by donor, adviser, sponsor and nonprofit, which also makes portability tractable.

**The economic finding [S] (internal-demonstration; the ranking is an artifact of assumptions, the mechanism is reasoned):**
- Applying AI to existing workflows (option C) ranked last of four interventions in all 16 modelled processes under base assumptions. ([Anthropic API pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing); [BLS compliance officers (May 2025)](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/business-and-financial/compliance-officers.htm); [BLS ECEC June 2026](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ecec.nr0.htm); repo [`ai-process-economics.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/ai-process-economics.csv)) **[RT] Wave 2A showed this ranking is fixed by the inputs:** with manual improvement set at 15% and AI-amenability capped at 0.45, C cannot beat A in any process (that would need amenability > 0.50). (repo [`ai-process-economics.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/ai-process-economics.csv); repo [`validation-register-wave2a.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/validation-register-wave2a.csv)) P07 non-standard diligence is effectively a tie with conventional automation ($512 apart). (repo [`ai-process-economics.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/ai-process-economics.csv); repo [`validation-register-wave2a.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/validation-register-wave2a.csv)) The ranking is **not evidence about AI performance**.
- What survives the red team: inference cost (≈$0.45 per heavy document review vs ≈$83 of reviewer time) is immaterial. ([Anthropic API pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing); [BLS compliance officers (May 2025)](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/business-and-financial/compliance-officers.htm); [BLS ECEC June 2026](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ecec.nr0.htm); repo [`ai-process-economics.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/ai-process-economics.csv); repo [`validation-register-wave2a.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/validation-register-wave2a.csv)) **[RT] Correction:** QA/re-review intensity decides whether AI value is *positive* (at 25% re-review it is negative everywhere) (repo [`ai-process-economics.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/ai-process-economics.csv); repo [`red-team-wave2a-ai-and-entrant.md`](../../research/mission-2026-11-14/red-team-wave2a-ai-and-entrant.md)), but the *ranking* is driven by the unsourced amenability and redesign-share assumptions. With zero QA, C still ranks last in 12/16. (repo [`ai-process-economics.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/ai-process-economics.csv); repo [`validation-register-wave2a.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/validation-register-wave2a.csv))
- The directional rule "prevent exceptions before automating their handling" rests on reasoning (redesign removes exception classes; AI only shortens handling), not on the model. ((source: repo inference — no primary URL)) AI is best placed after redesign, on the residual unstructured tail: complex assets, non-standard grantee diligence, succession documents.

**Market reality:**
- Of 30 AI use cases inventoried, 10 are DAF-direct, and only 2 of those have deployment evidence (a navigation assistant at Fidelity Charitable; a corporate grantmaker's agents on the donor side). ([Fidelity Charitable virtual-assistant terms](https://www.fidelitycharitable.org/legal/virtual-assistant.html); [Chronicle of Philanthropy on Circle Foundation](https://www.philanthropy.com/news/ai-recommends-humans-decide-circle-foundations-bet-on-agentic-philanthropy/); repo [`ai-use-case-inventory.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/ai-use-case-inventory.csv))
- **No public evidence of AI in sponsor-side approval or eligibility determination.** (repo [`ai-use-case-inventory.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/ai-use-case-inventory.csv); repo [`validation-register-wave2a.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/validation-register-wave2a.csv)) **[RT] Exception:** Daffy, which operates its own sponsor, announced back-end AI that unifies charity data and "flags issues for review" (2025-04-09; announced only). ([FF News on Daffy AI](https://ffnews.com/newsarticle/paytech/daffy-is-using-ai-to-reshape-the-future-of-charitable-giving/)) This is consistent with the flag-only rule below. Bonterra Que was missing from the inventory and has been added to the correction list. ([Bonterra Que release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251001579571/en/Bonterra-Launches-Que-The-First-Agentic-AI-Platform-for-Funders-Nonprofits-and-Supporters))
- The agentic frontier is **donor-side**: TIFIN Give's Louise, and Daffy's MCP server exposing `make_donation`. ([TIFIN Give Louise release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/tifin-give-launches-louise-ai-capabilities-for-philanthropy-302595069.html); [Daffy MCP docs](https://docs.daffy.org/agents/mcp)) Sponsors will receive agent-originated recommendations before they deploy agents themselves.

**Risk:**
- **Taken unconstrained,** AI creates risk in 13 of 16 failure modes and redistributes it in the other 3. (repo [`ai-failure-mode-register.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/ai-failure-mode-register.csv); repo [`validation-register-wave2a.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/validation-register-wave2a.csv))
- **The one rule that turns AI from a risk creator into a risk reducer:** *AI may add a flag, never clear one.* The most dangerous failure is a missed sanctions hit or prohibited benefit, and this rule reduces that risk instead of adding to it. (repo [`ai-governance-failure-modes.md`](../../research/mission-2026-11-14/ai-governance-failure-modes.md))
- **Why this is a category error:** DAF law assigns liability to the sponsor and to knowing fund managers (§§4966, 4967, 4958). ([26 U.S.C. §4966](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4966); [26 U.S.C. §4967](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4967); [26 U.S.C. §4958](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4958)) "AI approval" therefore misplaces accountability.

**Bottom line (Clarity posture: Guardrail for any AI touching status determination or money; Experimental for donor-facing assistants with spend limits; Fast for drafting):** AI changes DAF economics mainly by making redesign cheaper to specify, test and audit, not by accelerating the inherited workflow. Its economic case is strongest exactly where governance burden is highest (unstructured, judgment-heavy work). That tension, not model capability, is the binding constraint.

## E. What becomes more valuable as administration becomes cheaper

Administration is becoming cheaper through shared rails, continuous monitoring and AI-assisted servicing. Scarcity then moves to the assets that cannot be copied:

1. **Distribution access (most valuable).** Wealth platforms decide which DAF appears beside client accounts, as the SEI dual-stack, Edward Jones/RCF and TIAA/Charityvest/Foundation Source arrangements show. ([SEI Giving Fund portal](https://sei.donorfirstx.com/); [SEI Charitable Fund release](https://www.seic.com/about-sei/newsroom/sei-launches-sei-charitable-fund-bringing-charitable-giving-advisor-and-client-wealth-experience); [Edward Jones Charitable Gift Fund portal](https://edwardjones.donorfirstx.com/); [TIAA Wealth Management DAF release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/tiaa-wealth-management-makes-charitable-giving-part-of-the-financial-plan-with-new-donor-advised-fund-program-302891438.html)) Adviser AUM on DAF assets (55 bps at OneGive; up to 1% elsewhere) ([OneAscent Form ADV Part 2A](https://files.adviserinfo.sec.gov/IAPD/Content/Common/crd_iapd_Brochure.aspx?BRCHR_VRSN_ID=1054956); [DAFgiving360 fees and minimums](https://www.dafgiving360.org/features/fees-and-minimums)) is the incentive that keeps distribution loyal. *Medium-High.*
2. **Sponsor trust and legal standing.** Irrevocable gifts make the sponsor's governance the product. Discretion events (2026 SPLC-related restrictions **[RT]**) ([California AG release](https://oag.ca.gov/node/623769)) show the sponsor's judgment is visible and contested. *Medium.*
3. **Custody and affiliated investment economics.** At the affiliate-administered nationals, administration fees flow to the parent and 35 of 37 Vanguard Charitable pools are Vanguard funds. ([Fidelity Charitable FY2025 audited statements](https://www.fidelitycharitable.org/content/dam/fc-public/docs/fc/audited-financial-statements-2025-2024.pdf); [Vanguard Charitable FY2026 financial statements](https://www.vanguardcharitable.org/sites/default/files/2026-11/VCEP_Financial_Statements_2026.pdf)) Cheap administration *raises* the relative importance of investment margin. *Medium-High.*
4. **Cross-sponsor data.** Eligibility outcomes, exception patterns, foreign-grantee equivalency determinations and recipient payment details are duplicated across about 1,500 sponsors. ([DAFRC Annual DAF Report page](https://www.dafresearchcollaborative.org/research/annual-daf-report)) Whoever aggregates them (with consent) holds a compounding asset. No one does today; Chariot comes closest on the payment side. ([Chariot llms.txt](https://www.givechariot.com/llms.txt); (source: repo inference — no primary URL)) *Suggestive, Low-Medium.*
5. **Compliance evidence, not compliance labor.** As review labor shrinks, value moves to provable control: event-sourced audit trails, versioned policy, and attested SOC reports that match purchased scope. ((source: repo inference — no primary URL)) *Medium.*
6. **Nonprofit-side access.** Nonprofits bear reconciliation costs across 1,200+ DAF providers. ([Chariot gift processing launch](https://givechariot.com/resources/insights/chariot-launches-first-gift-processing-platform-for-nonprofits)) The rail that nonprofits adopt (Chariot's DAFpay at a 2.9% default fee, or a sponsor-funded rail) ([Chariot nonprofit terms](https://www.givechariot.com/legal-nonprofit)) gains network power. *Medium.*

**Less valuable:** standalone grant-approval software, generic donor portals, and per-grant charity lookups.
