## 0. Current A–G and top ten (governing, 2026-11-19)

This section is the current reading. It draws its figures from the latest validated sources:
- the wave 22–30 layer below;
- the industry report's [§11.28–§11.33](../DAF-INDUSTRY-REPORT-2026-11.md) (waves 31A–33B) [V wave 34S];
- the governing appendices of the documents cited.

Where it conflicts with A–G, the wave 14–21 additions or the wave 22–30 additions, this section governs. Those layers are kept for their evidence text and history. **[I]** marks inference; **[S]** marks a scenario figure.

**A. Beliefs that may be wrong**
- **"Millions of DAF customers."** DAFRC reports 3.59m FY2024 accounts, but 79.5% sit in 22 donation processors holding 0.9% of assets ([regulation-market-structure-portability.md](regulation-market-structure-portability.md)). On corrected FY2024 totals (3.31m accounts, a different basis from DAFRC's 3.59m) the ongoing-advisory basis gives 2.59m, or 0.79m without Givinga and PayPal Giving Fund [V wave 33B] ([queue-batch36-metrics-rails.md](queue-batch36-metrics-rails.md); wave 21). *Contradicted as stated; High.*
- **"DAFs pay out about 25%."** FY2024 aggregate payout is 25.9%, 22.8% net of DAF-to-DAF transfers and 12.7% at the median sponsor with at least $1m ([queue-batch38-data-followups.md](queue-batch38-data-followups.md); wave 23). The median account pays about 9% over three years in voluntary samples ([direction-academic-literature.md](direction-academic-literature.md) F1; wave 24B). First FY2025 filings net $3.05–3.80bn on covered filers, a lower bound ([gap19-fy2025-refresh.md](gap19-fy2025-refresh.md); wave 31A). *Suggestive; Medium-High on distribution.*
- **"Three giants dominate."** Sponsor-asset CR3 is 45.5%, CR5 55.8% and HHI 830, unchanged after graph consolidation ([gap19-graph-consolidation.md](gap19-graph-consolidation.md); wave 31A). On the league-table basis, asset concentration rose over FY2021–24 (CR5 51.0% → 56.9%) while grant concentration fell ([direction-league-table-concentration.md](direction-league-table-concentration.md); wave 29). *Partially contradicted; Medium.*
- **"Ren powers over half of DAF assets."** Any confirmed Ren/Stellar link covers 32.2% of FY2024 DAF assets as a floor ($105.6bn, 54 sponsors; 32.9% with six EIN-unmatched sponsors) ([gap19-graph-consolidation.md](gap19-graph-consolidation.md) §4; wave 31A). NPT, 12.8% of FY2024 DAF assets ($42.12bn of $327.87bn, per the wave-31A text), still rests on hosting evidence alone [V wave 33B]; no second source names Ren as NPT's vendor ([gap19-second-source-pass.md](gap19-second-source-pass.md) C01; wave 31B). 19 sponsors holding $31.56bn name Ren in their latest 990. One of them is Ayco's final return, which is not a live client ([validation-wave31a-report.md](validation-wave31a-report.md); [gap19-fy2025-refresh.md](gap19-fy2025-refresh.md)). [V wave 33B] *Unsupported on public evidence; Medium.*
- **"Grant approval is the bottleneck."**
  - The like-for-like non-standard share is about 3.6% of the four largest nationals' FY2023–24 dollars.
  - A broader review-need proxy (the source's "core" definition) is 7.4% of top-50 recipient lines and 11.0% of dollars (7.41% / 10.96% without a $212.2m Greater Horizons totals row); the source's "broad" definition gives 25% / 20% [V wave 34S]. That proxy measures recipients, not review ([gap19-exception-load-proxy.md](gap19-exception-load-proxy.md); wave 32A).
  - Grants to EINs under an IRS revocation are 0.082% of top-50 Schedule I dollars ([direction-recipient-compliance-screen.md](direction-recipient-compliance-screen.md); wave 26).

  *Suggestive; Medium.*
- **"Donors can move a DAF easily."** None of 12 sponsors publishes an outbound transfer policy ([sponsor-transfer-portability-terms.csv](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/sponsor-transfer-portability-terms.csv)). NPT is the one top-10 sponsor documenting a donor export (Excel) ([queue-batch38-vendors-ledgers-portals.md](queue-batch38-vendors-ledgers-portals.md); wave 23). *Suggestive; Medium-High.*
- **"The 2023 regulations are imminent; states will act."** The rules are still proposed, and the docket holds 249 submissions ([direction-policy-lobbying-obbba.md](direction-policy-lobbying-obbba.md); wave 24A). A 51-jurisdiction search found no 2021–2026 state payout, reporting or donor-intent mandate; Connecticut was not checked ([gap19-state-law-50-state-check.md](gap19-state-law-50-state-check.md); wave 32B). *High on federal status; Medium on states.*
- **"Challengers are cheaper; adviser fees are capped."**
  - All-in cost at $250k runs from 0.26% (Daffy) to 2.13% (San Francisco Foundation) ([queue-all-in-cost-dafpay-access.md](queue-all-in-cost-dafpay-access.md); wave 10).
  - Only MS GIFT, DAFgiving360 and TIFIN Give/GiveClear publish an adviser-fee cap, at 1.00%. MS GIFT's cap excludes Legacy Pools and Select UMA manager fees, and Fidelity CIAP, NPT, AEF and RCF publish none ([queue-all-in-cost-dafpay-access.md](queue-all-in-cost-dafpay-access.md); [validation-wave10-report.md](validation-wave10-report.md) V10-05; [queue-adviser-fees-pools-npact.md](queue-adviser-fees-pools-npact.md)). [V wave 33B]
  - Fidelity's active asset-allocation pools trail benchmarks by about its admin fee over five years [I] ([direction-investment-menus-performance.md](direction-investment-menus-performance.md); waves 27, 31B).

  *Suggestive; Medium.*
- **"AI agents can give safely on a donor's behalf."** No product of 30 meets the RED test. Only TIFIN Louise and Daffy's MCP server can move DAF money ([direction-ai-product-census.md](direction-ai-product-census.md); wave 28). *Unsupported; Medium.*
- **"A DAF is more tax-efficient than giving directly."** For itemizers the tax is identical. For non-itemizers a DAF loses from 2026 ([direction-donor-tax-scenarios-2026.md](direction-donor-tax-scenarios-2026.md); wave 27; illustrative). *Contradicted; Medium-High.*
- **"Complaint rates show service quality."** Among the 15 largest sponsors, only Fidelity Charitable and RCF have review-venue records at all. Fidelity's rate is 1.32 per 10,000 accounts, and the ranking against AOGF flips with the denominator ([gap19-complaints-with-denominators.md](gap19-complaints-with-denominators.md); wave 32B). *Contradicted as a quality measure; Medium.*
- **"DAF startups fail often."** 18 entrants from 2021–2026 show no documented shutdown: 10 active, 1 acquired, 1 pivoted, 6 unknown. The one pivot, Uncommon Giving, fails the window rule (its DAF launched in 2020); without it the cohort is 17 with no pivot, and the strict subset (n=8) is the cleaner read [V wave 34S] ([gap19-startup-cohort.md](gap19-startup-cohort.md); wave 32A). *Suggestive; Low-Medium (small n).*

**B. What AI changes.** The flag-only rule (AI may add a flag, never clear one) still governs ([ai-governance-failure-modes.md](ai-governance-failure-modes.md)).
- Inference cost is immaterial: about $0.45 per heavy review against about $83 of reviewer time ([ai-economics-replacement-analysis.md](ai-economics-replacement-analysis.md); wave 2A).
- Calibrated to 21 published studies, the large-national AI value pool is $445k ($202k–$793k across framings; $110k on the all-touched basis). Shared-platform break-even falls to 16% of draws, and no process changes its most likely winner [S] ([gap19-ai-calibration.md](gap19-ai-calibration.md); wave 31A).
- AI work is at vendors: AI appears in 29.1% of vendor job postings (44 of 151) against 13.3% of sponsor postings (2 of 15, a small sample) [V wave 33B] ([direction-news-hiring-signals.md](direction-news-hiring-signals.md); wave 24B).
- First-party reading of 13 of 20 entities (7 were blocked) found none documenting AI in grant approval, eligibility or money movement. This is an absence finding; the wave-31B validator spot-checked 12 [V wave 33B] ([gap19-ai-coverage-20-entities.md](gap19-ai-coverage-20-entities.md); wave 31B).

**C. Where incumbents are vulnerable**
- **Shared single points of failure.** RCF pays Ren 98% of individual-fundholder admin-fee revenue ($19.23m, FY2024) ([RCF FY2024 audited statements](https://renaissancecharitable.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2024RCFAuditedFinancialStatements.pdf)). Named Ren receipts are a $44.98m floor (sponsors' top-five contractor lines only), 85.8% from three customers [V wave 34S] ([queue-batch37-vendor-economics-owners.md](queue-batch37-vendor-economics-owners.md); wave 22).
- **Community-foundation cost gap.** Median admin fees are 100 / 100 / 67 bps at $100k / $1m / $10m, about 1.7x / 2.2x / 3.6–4.7x the nationals' ([direction-cf-consolidation-fees.md](direction-cf-consolidation-fees.md); waves 25A, 31B).
- **Legacy attrition.** Ren iPhi lost 6 of 57 sponsors in 2023–Nov 2026 ([queue-ren-iphi-attrition.md](queue-ren-iphi-attrition.md); wave 11). FY2025 filings show no Ren attrition yet, but the largest Ren clients (MS GIFT, RCF, US Charitable Gift Trust, NCF) have not filed FY2025 ([gap19-fy2025-refresh.md](gap19-fy2025-refresh.md); wave 31A) [V wave 34S].
- **Unproven challenger claims.** Give Interactive's nine known clients hold $36.74bn on DNS evidence, of which only $23.90bn rests on first-party or filing evidence. It lost three community-foundation tenants, not five ([crtsh-resourcing-2026-11-19.md](crtsh-resourcing-2026-11-19.md); wave 31B).
- **Assurance and service.** None of 13 vendors publishes a scoped SOC report ([queue-assurance-everyorg-givinga-monitoring.md](queue-assurance-everyorg-givinga-monitoring.md); wave 5). No readable top-20 sponsor publishes a response-time promise ([direction-sponsor-accessibility-service.md](direction-sponsor-accessibility-service.md); wave 25A).
- **Not vulnerable.** Fidelity holds 20.5–21.7% of corrected assets every year in FY2021–24 ([direction-league-table-concentration.md](direction-league-table-concentration.md); wave 29). Foundant serves 282 of 351 tail community foundations with an identified vendor ([direction-long-tail-sponsors.md](direction-long-tail-sponsors.md); wave 30). [V wave 33B]

**D. What entrants should avoid building**
- **Credential-based integrations.** Six of the top 20 sponsors (about $84bn) contractually limit automated or third-party access ([queue-batch34-escheat-terms.md](queue-batch34-escheat-terms.md); wave 19).
- **Waiting for an open rail.** No open multi-sponsor grant-request standard exists ([direction-data-standards-interoperability.md](direction-data-standards-interoperability.md); wave 25B).
- **Holding donor funds.** In the three documented loss cases (Ammado, Flipcause, SDG Impact Fund), one operator held donor money that was not segregated; the causes are not adjudicated [V wave 34S] ([failed-daf-startups-deep-research.md](failed-daf-startups-deep-research.md)).
- **A standalone long-tail business** [I]. The 1,240 sponsors under $50m hold 2.84% of assets ([direction-long-tail-sponsors.md](direction-long-tail-sponsors.md); wave 30). Tail community foundations' allocated operating cost is a median 205 bps of DAF assets, twice top-50 CFs' 102 bps on rule A but 1.6× on an admin-only floor; rule A compares whole organisations ([b45n33-long-tail-operating-cost.md](b45n33-long-tail-operating-cost.md); wave 33A) [V wave 34S].
- **Selling the DAF as tax-superior** to a direct gift (wave 27).

**E. What becomes more valuable**
- **Distribution access.** The fastest-growing sponsors are channel- or platform-led [I] ([direction-sponsor-marketing-spend.md](direction-sponsor-marketing-spend.md); wave 28).
- **Nonprofit identity and remittance data.** Only 36% of sponsors in the CF-weighted DAFRC survey default to sharing full contact data ([direction-nonprofit-side-experience.md](direction-nonprofit-side-experience.md); wave 29). At the FY2024 top 20 the default is mixed. Full contact is documented for 33.2% of assets (Fidelity, RCF, NCF, ImpactAssets, FFTC) and withholding for 17.2%. RCF's 2.4% rests on Ren's October 2026 ‘Giving Platform’ guide; RCF's own 2023–24 guide said Fund Name Only, which would make the split 30.9% and 19.5% (b4428-02) [V wave 35B]; the rest is undocumented ([b44n28-01-donor-info-defaults.md](b44n28-01-donor-info-defaults.md)) [V wave 34A]
- **Measured latency.** Only 5 of 28 published turnaround statements are measured ([queue-batch36-metrics-rails.md](queue-batch36-metrics-rails.md); wave 21).
- **Retained, dated eligibility evidence** [I] ([direction-recipient-compliance-screen.md](direction-recipient-compliance-screen.md); wave 26).
- **Local knowledge.** Community foundations keep 60.0% of US grant dollars in-state, against 7.6% for nationals ([direction-grant-geography.md](direction-grant-geography.md); wave 27).

**G. Top ten (order carried from the wave 22–30 list; evidence refreshed)**
1. **Donor data and automation are contractually closed, and no open standard fills the gap.** Six of the top 20 limit automated access ([queue-batch34-escheat-terms.md](queue-batch34-escheat-terms.md)). DAF Direct is closed, with 3 listed sponsors, one of which (BNY Mellon Charitable Gift Fund) ended its DAF program in December 2024 [V wave 34S] ([direction-data-standards-interoperability.md](direction-data-standards-interoperability.md)). NPT's Excel export is the one top-10 exception ([queue-batch38-vendors-ledgers-portals.md](queue-batch38-vendors-ledgers-portals.md)). Executed contracts remain unseen ([FINAL-STATUS.md](FINAL-STATUS.md) Q-N04).
2. **Most "DAF accounts" are not DAF customers.** DAFRC reports 3.59m accounts. On corrected totals (3.31m, a different basis) the ongoing-advisory count is 2.59m [V wave 33B], or 0.79m without Givinga and PayPal Giving Fund ([queue-batch36-metrics-rails.md](queue-batch36-metrics-rails.md)). The long tail's 46.4% of accounts is 3.49% without Givinga and BNY Mellon CGF ([direction-long-tail-sponsors.md](direction-long-tail-sponsors.md)).
3. **Market payout and growth are overstated by transfers between sponsors.** FY2024: 25.9% aggregate, 22.8% net, 12.7% at the median sponsor ([queue-batch38-data-followups.md](queue-batch38-data-followups.md)). Netted grants are $58.41bn ([queue-batch34-netting-filings.md](queue-batch34-netting-filings.md)). FY2025 covered-filer netting is $3.05–3.80bn so far, a lower bound ([gap19-fy2025-refresh.md](gap19-fy2025-refresh.md)).
4. **Nonprofits bear the cost of fragmented rails and thin donor data.** Any DAF option appears on 41.2% of top donate pages (a lower bound), and DAFpay on 13.7% ([direction-nonprofit-donate-pages.md](direction-nonprofit-donate-pages.md)). Processor complaints describe payout rules: paper-check thresholds, lost checks and EFT vetting ([gap19-complaints-with-denominators.md](gap19-complaints-with-denominators.md); wave 32B). Re-read rules show $100 check thresholds, monthly batches and redirection to employer- or processor-chosen charities, but written rules fully explain only two of seven named complaints, and 11 of 14 grant-delay records predate the rule versions read ([g1913-processor-disbursement-rules.md](g1913-processor-disbursement-rules.md); wave 33A) [V wave 34S]. Congregations are the extreme case. Church-status recipients with no e-filed 990 take 8.3% of top-50 DAF dollars, about half of it congregations, three-quarters of it from national sponsors; none of 14 congregation giving pages read showed a DAF option ([b4026-06-church-daf-recipients.md](b4026-06-church-daf-recipients.md)) [V wave 35C].
5. **Donor cost sits in fund and adviser layers the 990 does not show.** Program-service revenue is $0 at all 15 affiliated sponsors ([direction-sponsor-financial-resilience.md](direction-sponsor-financial-resilience.md)). Fidelity's fee-sized shortfall holds over five years for its active pools [I] ([direction-investment-menus-performance.md](direction-investment-menus-performance.md); wave 31B).
6. **Community foundations carry a structural cost gap.** Admin fees are 1.7–4.7x the nationals', on 37 schedules covering 46% of top-100 CF DAF assets ([direction-cf-consolidation-fees.md](direction-cf-consolidation-fees.md); wave 31B). Staff cost (Part IX lines 5–10, whole organisation) is 78.7 bps of DAF assets at community foundations, against 5.6–15.0 bps at the four self-staffed nationals (CF cost about 3.5–9× theirs) and 2.9 bps at affiliated-commercial sponsors, whose staff sit at the parent firm ([direction-sponsor-executive-compensation.md](direction-sponsor-executive-compensation.md)) [V wave 34S].
7. **Ren's footprint is below its claims.** The confirmed-link floor is 32.2% of assets ([gap19-graph-consolidation.md](gap19-graph-consolidation.md)). Named receipts are a $44.98m floor, 85.8% from three customers ([queue-batch37-vendor-economics-owners.md](queue-batch37-vendor-economics-owners.md)). NPT remains hosting-only evidence ([gap19-second-source-pass.md](gap19-second-source-pass.md) C01).
8. **The platform market is segmented, and Foundant holds the long tail.** Foundant serves 282 of 351 tail community foundations with an identified vendor ([direction-long-tail-sponsors.md](direction-long-tail-sponsors.md)). Give Interactive's first-party-evidenced book is $23.90bn of a $36.74bn DNS-evidenced client set ([crtsh-resourcing-2026-11-19.md](crtsh-resourcing-2026-11-19.md)).
9. **Agentic products are donor-side and unproven.** None of 30 meets the RED test, and only two can move DAF money ([direction-ai-product-census.md](direction-ai-product-census.md)). No re-read entity documents AI in approval ([gap19-ai-coverage-20-entities.md](gap19-ai-coverage-20-entities.md)).
10. **The operations-AI value pool is thin.** The calibrated large-national pool is $445k and break-even falls to 16% of draws [S] ([gap19-ai-calibration.md](gap19-ai-calibration.md)). The review-need proxy (11.0% of top-50 dollars; 10.96% corrected) indicates the scale of recipients that could need review. It is not a bound: it counts lines that sponsors clear by standing rule, and it misses donor-controlled supporting organisations and grants to individuals. No observed review data exist [V wave 34S] ([gap19-exception-load-proxy.md](gap19-exception-load-proxy.md)).

## B. What AI actually changes

**Superficial productivity (speeds work up; does not change who is accountable or what work exists):** drafting donor and adviser communications, grant-status answers, document extraction for complex assets, reconciliation-break triage, and proposal generation. Incumbents already market these. ([Fidelity Charitable virtual-assistant terms](https://www.fidelitycharitable.org/legal/virtual-assistant.html); [TIFIN Give Louise release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/tifin-give-launches-louise-ai-capabilities-for-philanthropy-302595069.html); [Chariot gift processing launch](https://givechariot.com/resources/insights/chariot-launches-first-gift-processing-platform-for-nonprofits))

**Structural changes (alter operating economics only when paired with redesign):**
1. **Policy-as-code.** Sponsor grant policy becomes versioned, executable and back-testable against historical grants, so a final §4966 guidance change can be re-run across the book.
2. **Continuous eligibility monitoring.** Every active payee is checked against BMF/Pub 78/revocation/OFAC refreshes, replacing per-grant lookups. ([IRS EO BMF extract](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/exempt-organizations-business-master-file-extract-eo-bmf); [IRS TEOS bulk data downloads](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/tax-exempt-organization-search-bulk-data-downloads); [IRS Auto-Revocation List](https://www.irs.gov/autorevocationlist); [OFAC Framework for Compliance Commitments](https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/16331/download)) Diligence moves off the grant's critical path.
3. **Exception-only human review.** Each routing decision records which rule or person decided.
4. **Event-sourced ledger.** Grant status becomes one state machine shared by donor, adviser, sponsor and nonprofit, which also makes portability tractable.

**The economic finding [S] (internal-demonstration; the ranking is an artifact of assumptions, the mechanism is reasoned):**
- Applying AI to existing workflows (option C) ranked last of four interventions in all 16 modelled processes under base assumptions. ([Anthropic API pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing); [BLS compliance officers (May 2025)](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/business-and-financial/compliance-officers.htm); [BLS ECEC June 2026](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ecec.nr0.htm); repo [`ai-process-economics.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/ai-process-economics.csv)) **[RT] Wave 2A showed this ranking is fixed by the inputs:** with manual improvement set at 15% and AI-amenability capped at 0.45, C cannot beat A in any process (that would need amenability > 0.50). (repo [`ai-process-economics.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/ai-process-economics.csv); repo [`validation-register-wave2a.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/validation-register-wave2a.csv)) P07 non-standard diligence is effectively a tie with conventional automation ($512 apart). (repo [`ai-process-economics.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/ai-process-economics.csv); repo [`validation-register-wave2a.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/validation-register-wave2a.csv)) The ranking is **not evidence about AI performance**.
- What survives the red team: inference cost (≈$0.45 per heavy document review vs ≈$83 of reviewer time) is immaterial. ([Anthropic API pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing); [BLS compliance officers (May 2025)](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/business-and-financial/compliance-officers.htm); [BLS ECEC June 2026](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ecec.nr0.htm); repo [`ai-process-economics.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/ai-process-economics.csv); repo [`validation-register-wave2a.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/validation-register-wave2a.csv)) **[RT] Correction:** QA/re-review intensity decides whether AI value is *positive* (at 25% re-review it is negative everywhere) (repo [`ai-process-economics.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/ai-process-economics.csv); repo [`red-team-wave2a-ai-and-entrant.md`](../../research/mission-2026-11-14/red-team-wave2a-ai-and-entrant.md)), but the *ranking* is driven by the unsourced amenability and redesign-share assumptions. With zero QA, C still ranks last in 12/16. (repo [`ai-process-economics.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/ai-process-economics.csv); repo [`validation-register-wave2a.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/validation-register-wave2a.csv))
- The directional rule "prevent exceptions before automating their handling" rests on reasoning (redesign removes exception classes; AI only shortens handling), not on the model. [I] inference from [ai-economics-replacement-analysis.md](ai-economics-replacement-analysis.md) (redesign removes the cause of exceptions; AI can only shorten their handling). [gap19-11] AI is best placed after redesign, on the residual unstructured tail: complex assets, non-standard grantee diligence, succession documents.

**[v2, observed costs]** A seeded Monte Carlo on Form 990 cost pools ([ai-economics-v2-observed-costs.md](ai-economics-v2-observed-costs.md)) confirms that redesign or conventional fixes are the most likely winners in all 16 processes, and that AI on the existing workflow ranks first in at most 17% of draws. Below about $1bn of DAF assets, a $120k/yr AI product cannot be funded from process savings, so the economic buyer is an administrator or platform vendor.

- **Taken unconstrained,** AI creates risk in 13 of 16 failure modes and redistributes it in the other 3. (repo [`ai-failure-mode-register.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/ai-failure-mode-register.csv); repo [`validation-register-wave2a.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/validation-register-wave2a.csv))
- **The one rule that turns AI from a risk creator into a risk reducer:** *AI may add a flag, never clear one.* The most dangerous failure is a missed sanctions hit or prohibited benefit, and this rule reduces that risk instead of adding to it. (repo [`ai-governance-failure-modes.md`](../../research/mission-2026-11-14/ai-governance-failure-modes.md))
- **Contract allocation:** public agreements at Daffy, TIFIN Give/GiveClear, Endaoment and Chariot put the loss from erroneous actions on the donor or customer, and NCF, Vanguard and NPT terms restrict third-party credential use or automated access ([queue-agentic-controls-and-sponsor-audits.md](queue-agentic-controls-and-sponsor-audits.md); [queue-sponsor-terms-data-clauses.md](queue-sponsor-terms-data-clauses.md)).
- **Why this is a category error:** DAF law assigns liability to the sponsor and to knowing fund managers (§§4966, 4967, 4958). ([26 U.S.C. §4966](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4966); [26 U.S.C. §4967](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4967); [26 U.S.C. §4958](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4958)) "AI approval" therefore misplaces accountability.

**Bottom line (Clarity posture: Guardrail for any AI touching status determination or money; Experimental for donor-facing assistants with spend limits; Fast for drafting):** AI changes DAF economics mainly by making redesign cheaper to specify, test and audit, not by accelerating the inherited workflow. Its economic case is strongest exactly where governance burden is highest (unstructured, judgment-heavy work). That tension, not model capability, is the binding constraint.

## E. What becomes more valuable as administration becomes cheaper

Administration is becoming cheaper through shared rails, continuous monitoring and AI-assisted servicing. Scarcity then moves to the assets that cannot be copied:

1. **Distribution access (most valuable).** Wealth platforms decide which DAF appears beside client accounts, as the SEI dual-stack, Edward Jones/RCF and TIAA/Charityvest/Foundation Source arrangements show. ([SEI Giving Fund portal](https://sei.donorfirstx.com/); [SEI Charitable Fund release](https://www.seic.com/about-sei/newsroom/sei-launches-sei-charitable-fund-bringing-charitable-giving-advisor-and-client-wealth-experience); [Edward Jones Charitable Gift Fund portal](https://edwardjones.donorfirstx.com/); [TIAA Wealth Management DAF release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/tiaa-wealth-management-makes-charitable-giving-part-of-the-financial-plan-with-new-donor-advised-fund-program-302891438.html)) Adviser AUM on DAF assets (55 bps at OneGive; capped at 1.00% only at MS GIFT, DAFgiving360 and TIFIN Give; no numeric cap at Fidelity CIAP, NPT, AEF or RCF, with observed RIA brochures at 0–2.00% **[V wave 11]**) ([OneAscent Form ADV Part 2A](https://files.adviserinfo.sec.gov/IAPD/Content/Common/crd_iapd_Brochure.aspx?BRCHR_VRSN_ID=1054956); [DAFgiving360 Program Policies, Dec 2024](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/557c669ae4b0e197d11bcb11/t/67c50d53d910296aef195450/1740967255089/DAFgiving360-2024-Program-Policies.pdf); [queue-adviser-fees-pools-npact.md](queue-adviser-fees-pools-npact.md)) is the incentive that keeps distribution loyal. *Medium-High.*
2. **Sponsor trust and legal standing.** Irrevocable gifts make the sponsor's governance the product. Discretion events (2026 SPLC-related restrictions **[RT]**) ([California AG release](https://oag.ca.gov/node/623769)) show the sponsor's judgment is visible and contested. *Medium.*
3. **Custody and affiliated investment economics.** At the affiliate-administered nationals, administration fees flow to the parent and 35 of 37 Vanguard Charitable pools are Vanguard funds. ([Fidelity Charitable FY2025 audited statements](https://www.fidelitycharitable.org/content/dam/fc-public/docs/fc/audited-financial-statements-2025-2024.pdf); [Vanguard Charitable FY2026 financial statements](https://www.vanguardcharitable.org/sites/default/files/2026-11/VCEP_Financial_Statements_2026.pdf)) Cheap administration *raises* the relative importance of investment margin. All-in cost confirms it: pool choice is as large a lever as sponsor choice (Fidelity 60%-equity pool 0.55% vs index 0.015%) **[V wave 10]** ([queue-all-in-cost-dafpay-access.md](queue-all-in-cost-dafpay-access.md)). *Medium-High.*
4. **Cross-sponsor data.** Eligibility outcomes, exception patterns, foreign-grantee equivalency determinations and recipient payment details are duplicated across about 1,500 sponsors. ([DAFRC Annual DAF Report page](https://www.dafresearchcollaborative.org/research/annual-daf-report)) Whoever aggregates them (with consent) holds a compounding asset. No one does today; Chariot comes closest on the payment side. Licences constrain aggregation: Candid's API licence bars data mining to build a database, resell or compete, and requires stopping all use on termination **[V wave 9]** ([queue-sponsor-terms-data-clauses.md](queue-sponsor-terms-data-clauses.md)). ([Chariot llms.txt](https://www.givechariot.com/llms.txt)). "No one does today" is [I] inference from [direction-data-standards-interoperability.md](direction-data-standards-interoperability.md): no open multi-sponsor grant-request standard was found, and DAF Direct is closed, with 3 sponsors (wave 25B). [gap19-11] *Suggestive, Low-Medium.*
5. **Compliance evidence, not compliance labor.** As review labor shrinks, value moves to provable control: event-sourced audit trails, versioned policy, and attested SOC reports that match purchased scope. [I] inference from [queue-assurance-everyorg-givinga-monitoring.md](queue-assurance-everyorg-givinga-monitoring.md) P-QA1-04: none of 13 vendors publishes a SOC report with period and system scope **[V wave 5]**. [gap19-11] *Medium.*
6. **Nonprofit-side access.** Nonprofits bear reconciliation costs across 1,200+ DAF providers. ([Chariot gift processing launch](https://givechariot.com/resources/insights/chariot-launches-first-gift-processing-platform-for-nonprofits)) The rail that nonprofits adopt (Chariot's DAFpay at a 2.9% default fee, or a sponsor-funded rail) ([Chariot nonprofit terms](https://www.givechariot.com/legal-nonprofit)) gains network power. Today sponsors charge nonprofits $0 to receive grants, except Benevity (2.99%, cap $500), DAFpay at default terms and ForGood (1.5%) **[V wave 12]** ([queue-nonprofit-side-panel.md](queue-nonprofit-side-panel.md)). *Medium.*

**Less valuable:** standalone grant-approval software, generic donor portals, and per-grant charity lookups.

## G. The ten most important discoveries (ranked by strategic significance; superseded by §0 G, evidence text kept) [gap19-11]

1. **Donor data and automation are contractually closed, so the entrant gating question has a provisional answer.** (Previously: gating condition in D; part of old #9.)
   - **Evidence:**
     - None of the 10 largest sponsors publishes a donor-side machine-readable export or a portability statement **[V wave 8]**. Fidelity offers PDF only, Vanguard view only, and AEF two years of history; SVCF bars automated collection for non-grantmaking purposes ([queue-donor-export-cf-payments-donorsphere.md](queue-donor-export-cf-payments-donorsphere.md); [`donor-data-export-top10-2026.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/donor-data-export-top10-2026.csv)).

Vanguard treats an outside service using the donor's login as an "Unauthorized Third Party". NPT bans robot harvesting and using its data for any software, model or AI system.    - **Evidence:** all-in annual cost at $250k with each sponsor's typical pool runs from 0.26% (Daffy) and 0.62% (Vanguard) to 1.15% (Fidelity), 1.25% (DAFgiving360), 1.45% (NCF) and 2.13% (San Francisco Foundation); Fidelity's 60%-equity pool costs 0.55% against 0.015% for its index pool **[V wave 10]** ([queue-all-in-cost-dafpay-access.md](queue-all-in-cost-dafpay-access.md); [`all-in-cost-comparison-2026.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/all-in-cost-comparison-2026.csv)). Adviser fees are the largest layer in adviser-led programs. Only MS GIFT, DAFgiving360 and TIFIN Give cap them (1.00%); Fidelity CIAP, NPT, AEF and RCF publish no numeric cap, and observed RIA brochures charge 0–2.00% **[V wave 11]** ([queue-adviser-fees-pools-npact.md](queue-adviser-fees-pools-npact.md); [`adviser-fees-observed-2026.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/adviser-fees-observed-2026.csv)). RCF pays Ren 98% of individual-fundholder admin-fee revenue ($19.23m FY2024) **[V]**; Fidelity's admin fee goes to FMR affiliates; 35 of 37 Vanguard pools are Vanguard funds ([RCF FY2024 audited statements](https://renaissancecharitable.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2024RCFAuditedFinancialStatements.pdf); [RCF FY2023 audited statements](https://renaissancecharitable.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2023RCFAuditedFinancialStatements.pdf); repo [`rcf-ren-audited-admin-payments-2020-2024.csv`](../../data/rcf-ren-audited-admin-payments-2020-2024.csv); [Fidelity Charitable FY2025 audited statements](https://www.fidelitycharitable.org/content/dam/fc-public/docs/fc/audited-financial-statements-2025-2024.pdf); [Vanguard Charitable FY2026 financial statements](https://www.vanguardcharitable.org/sites/default/files/2026-11/VCEP_Financial_Statements_2026.pdf)).
   - **Uncertainty:** the causal effect on payout is unproven; national sponsors (AUM-fee) show about 20% median payout against about 11% for community foundations **[RT]** and about 5.4% for university/hospital sponsors, which runs against a simple "fees cause low payout" reading. ([DAFRC Updated Analysis dataset](https://www.dafresearchcollaborative.org/s/Annual-DAF-Report-2025-Updated-Analysis-Dataset.xlsx); repo [`validation-register-wave2b.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/validation-register-wave2b.csv)) Edward Jones's fee is low confidence (404 pages).
   - **Implication:** price competition on admin fees misses the main cost layers. Adviser economics keep distribution loyal and would be directly exposed if the proposed §4966 rule treating personal advisers as donor-advisors were finalised.
   - **Next:** compare payout at AUM-fee vs flat-fee sponsors; extend all-in cost to adviser-led scenarios at each sponsor.
   - **State:** fee facts High; retention incentive classified **mixed (incentive documented; effect unsupported)**. Clarity posture: Experimental.

7. **Payout is lower net of DAF-to-DAF transfers and bimodal behind the average.** (Old #7, updated.)
   - **Evidence:** pooled FY2021–24 payout excluding processors is 23.9% gross and 21.5%, 20.8% or 19.0% net of sponsor-to-sponsor transfers; nationals fall from 23.8% to 20.9%, and 12.1% of national sponsors' DAF grants go to other nationals **[V wave 11]** ([queue-net-payout-fidelity-outliers.md](queue-net-payout-fidelity-outliers.md); [`net-payout-summary.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/net-payout-summary.csv)). DAFRC's aggregate is 24–28%, but the median sponsor with ≥$1m pays 11–12.5%, and 19–24% of such sponsors pay under 5% ([DAFRC Updated Analysis memo](https://www.dafresearchcollaborative.org/s/Annual-DAF-Report-2025-Updated-Analysis.pdf); [DAFRC Updated Analysis dataset](https://www.dafresearchcollaborative.org/s/Annual-DAF-Report-2025-Updated-Analysis-Dataset.xlsx); repo [`derived-metrics.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/derived-metrics.csv)).
   - **Uncertainty:** sponsor-level, not fund-level; the result depends on the transfer definition; Fidelity's $727m of CY2025 outlier grants and post-SPLC flows are not visible until 2027–28 filings. In FY2025 filings, transfers between DAF sponsors were 10–18% of the four nationals' listed grant dollars ([Fidelity Charitable Form 990 FY2025](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/110303001/202601389349301700/IRS990); [DAFgiving360 Form 990 FY2025](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/311640316/202611199349300601/IRS990ScheduleF); [NPT Form 990 FY2025](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/237825575/202601289349302480/IRS990ScheduleF); [Vanguard Charitable Form 990 FY2025](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/232888152/202621329349306657/IRS990); repo [`breadth-community-faith-infrastructure-emerging.md`](../../research/mission-2026-11-14/breadth-community-faith-infrastructure-emerging.md)).
   - **Implication:** the policy debate concerns a long tail of sponsors and double counting, not the nationals' headline rate. Curricula still teach the aggregate 25.2% framing ([analyst-and-education-landscape.md](analyst-and-education-landscape.md)).
   - **Next:** segment net payout by sponsor type and fee model; re-run when FY2025–26 filings arrive.
   - **State:** internal-demonstration (reproducible script); Medium-High on distribution, Low on cause. Clarity posture: Fast.

8. **Agentic giving products are donor-side, unrecognised by contract and unproven.** (Old #6, extended.)
   - **Evidence:** none of 21 agentic giving products (2024–26) has independent outcome evidence, and none touches sponsor approval **[V wave 8]** ([queue-agentic-products-dafpay-fees.md](queue-agentic-products-dafpay-fees.md); [`agentic-philanthropy-products-2026.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/agentic-philanthropy-products-2026.csv)). No public contract at Daffy, TIFIN Give/GiveClear, Endaoment or Chariot treats an AI agent as a distinct actor, and all four allocate erroneous-action loss to the donor or customer; Louise has 8 of 10 controls undocumented; Daffy's MCP documents no spend limits **[V]** ([queue-agentic-controls-and-sponsor-audits.md](queue-agentic-controls-and-sponsor-audits.md); [TIFIN Give Louise release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/tifin-give-launches-louise-ai-capabilities-for-philanthropy-302595069.html); [Daffy MCP docs](https://docs.daffy.org/agents/mcp)). There is still no public evidence of AI in sponsor approval (repo [`ai-use-case-inventory.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/ai-use-case-inventory.csv); repo [`validation-register-wave2a.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/validation-register-wave2a.csv)).
   - **Uncertainty:** absence of public documentation is not absence of controls; Chariot's hosted confirmation step is the best observed pattern.
   - **Implication:** sponsors need agent-origin tagging and authority separation now, and the flag-only rule (AI may add a flag, never clear one) stays the governing control. Sponsor terms that bar credential sharing (#1) already make many agent integrations non-compliant.
   - **Next:** request TIFIN guardrail specifications; test Daffy MCP documentation; propose a human-vs-agent field to one rail.
   - **State:** suggestive; Medium. Clarity posture: Guardrail for money movement; Experimental for spend-limited donor assistants.

9. **The value pool for operations AI is thin, and the buyer is an administrator or platform, not a sponsor.** (Old #5, updated with v2.)
   - **Evidence:** inference cost is immaterial (≈$0.45 vs ≈$83 per heavy review) **[V wave 2A]** ([Anthropic API pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing); [BLS compliance officers (May 2025)](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/business-and-financial/compliance-officers.htm); repo [`ai-process-economics.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/ai-process-economics.csv)). On observed Form 990 cost pools, redesign or conventional fixes are the most likely winners in all 16 processes, AI on the existing workflow ranks first in at most 17% of draws, a shared platform breaks even in 43% of draws only with the five largest nationals, and a $120k/yr product cannot be funded below about $1bn of DAF assets **[S]** ([ai-economics-v2-observed-costs.md](ai-economics-v2-observed-costs.md); [`ai-economics-v2-results.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/ai-economics-v2-results.csv)). The base labour pool is about 6–8% of observed cost per grant, and the median loaded rate is $38.87/h **[V wave 8]** ([sponsor-cost-structure-and-sensitivities.md](sponsor-cost-structure-and-sensitivities.md); [queue-pe-lenders-labour-rates.md](queue-pe-lenders-labour-rates.md)). Grant-review value is ≈$16k/yr at a 48,744-grant sponsor **[S]**, and an operating-layer product at $120k/yr needs ≈76k–369k grants/yr to break even **[RT]** ([BLS ECEC June 2026](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ecec.nr0.htm); [Charityvest 2025 results release](https://foundationsource.com/newsroom/press-releases/charityvest-reports-record-2025-charitable-giving-more-than-doubling-contributions-and-grants/); [Endaoment institutions page](https://endaoment.org/institutions); repo [`entry-strategy-comparison.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/entry-strategy-comparison.csv); repo [`red-team-wave2a-ai-and-entrant.md`](../../research/mission-2026-11-14/red-team-wave2a-ai-and-entrant.md); repo [`validation-register-wave2a.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/validation-register-wave2a.csv)).
   - **Uncertainty:** amenability, redesign share, QA rate and fixed costs remain assumptions **[RT]**; no measured exception, QA or handling-time data exist anywhere in the market.
   - **Implication:** sponsors should fund continuous monitoring, electronic payee enrollment and policy-as-code first. AI economics work at vendor or network scale.
   - **Next:** one real time-and-motion study at a sponsor, which is the gating evidence for every AI and entrant conclusion here.
   - **State:** internal-demonstration; Medium on direction, Low on magnitudes. Clarity posture: Experimental.

10. **Nonprofits bear the cost of fragmented grant rails.** (New; extends the payments row in C.)
    - **Evidence:** sponsors charge nonprofits $0 to receive grants, except Benevity (2.99%, cap $500), DAFpay at default terms (2.9%) and ForGood (1.5%) **[V wave 12]** ([queue-nonprofit-side-panel.md](queue-nonprofit-side-panel.md); [`nonprofit-side-panel-2026.csv`](../../data/mission-2026-11-14/nonprofit-side-panel-2026.csv)). Cost per $1,000 grant is about $1–2 on free EFT, $5–8 by check and about $32 on DAFpay ([queue-infrastructure-nonprofit-costs-emerging.md](queue-infrastructure-nonprofit-costs-emerging.md)). No sponsor checked requires EFT, none publishes a timing SLA, NPT and Vanguard still use PayPal Grant Payments, and the top 30 community foundations mostly run their own ACH or publish nothing (none uses PayPal or Ren ACH) **[V waves 7, 12]** ([queue-payment-rails-cluster.md](queue-payment-rails-cluster.md); [queue-community-foundation-rails-portals.md](queue-community-foundation-rails-portals.md)). Fidelity reports 67% of grants by EFT ([Fidelity Charitable 2026 Giving Report PDF](https://www.fidelitycharitable.org/content/dam/fc-public/docs/insights/2026-giving-report.pdf)).
    - **Uncertainty:** fees are list prices; platform agreements and donor fee-cover change effective rates.
    - **Implication:** no rail dominates, and the community-foundation channel is an open field. A sponsor-funded rail or nonprofit-side aggregation gains network power.
    - **Next:** measure effective nonprofit fees on a sample of real DAF receipts; track Chariot and Seattle-style ACH mandates.
    - **State:** suggestive; Medium. Clarity posture: Fast.

### (a) What changed in syntheses A–G

*Superseded by §0 for current figures; kept as the wave 14–21 record.* [gap19-11] **A. Beliefs that may be wrong.** Two beliefs are now weaker at market level. On accounts (A1): counting only accounts with ongoing advisory privileges, and applying only AOGF's own reclassification, the market held 2.59m accounts in FY2024, not 3.31m. Without Givinga and PayPal Giving Fund it held 0.79m. Account growth over FY2021–24 is 9.7% a year on that basis, against 20.6% on corrected totals ([queue-batch36-metrics-rails.md](queue-batch36-metrics-rails.md); [queue-batch35-panel-screens.md](queue-batch35-panel-screens.md); waves 20–21). On payout (A2): netting sponsor-to-sponsor transfers lowers FY2024 market payout from 25.9% to 22.8% ([queue-batch34-netting-filings.md](queue-batch34-netting-filings.md); wave 19). Three new beliefs fail. First, "complaint rates show service quality": public complaint rates per 10,000 accounts measure venue coverage, not service (wave 14). Second, "the 990 shows what a wealth-captive DAF costs": Part IX understates all-in cost at GS DAF and MS GIFT, and AEF's line 11f is an average fund-borne cost, not the rate a fee-paying account pays (waves 16–17). Third, "a vendor that does not hold funds is unregulated": California's platform law reaches DAF technology vendors on its text ([queue-batch30-legal-circulars.md](queue-batch30-legal-circulars.md); wave 15). On portability (A8), six top-40 community foundations publicly document a donor grant-history export ([queue-batch33-voids-exports-rails.md](queue-batch33-voids-exports-rails.md); wave 18). So the "no export" finding holds for the top 10 nationals, not for all sponsors.

**B. What AI changes.** The flag-only rule still governs. Two observations add detail. Benevity's Match Assurance AI pre-checks and submits match requests, while administrators approve by default. Its overlay moves from RED to AMBER (provisional). It returns to RED if auto-approval keyed to the AI check is documented ([queue-batch36-agent-terms-controls.md](queue-batch36-agent-terms-controls.md); wave 21). Daffy is the only DAF API with third-party agent wrappers. Three wrappers document donation creation; two expose only gift creation, and Daffy keys carry no documented scope ([queue-batch35-agents-analysts-rails.md](queue-batch35-agents-analysts-rails.md); wave 20). For eligibility evidence, Rev. Proc. 2018-32 §8.01 (not the superseded 2011-33) requires the grantor to keep a copy of the report. Candid's API licence does not allow retention after termination; only its subscriber licence does ([queue-batch29-regulation-litigation-crypto.md](queue-batch29-regulation-litigation-crypto.md); waves 14–15). A monitoring product must store its own evidence under terms that allow it.

**C. Where incumbents are vulnerable.** Hidden cost layers are an exposure. Fidelity's all-in floor is about 50–52 bps per invested dollar. MS GIFT's 1.00% advisory cap and 0.07% MSSB fee do not appear in Part IX. NPT pays Hollencrest 5–25 bps of assets introduced ([queue-batch31-fees-costs.md](queue-batch31-fees-costs.md); [queue-batch32-infund-costs.md](queue-batch32-infund-costs.md); waves 16–17). NPT's "program fees" stayed at $3.7–4.3m since FY2021 while DAF assets rose 3.5×, and the payee is undisclosed (wave 19). Channel sponsors keep consolidating or exiting: Ayco merged into GS DAPF on 2025-07-01; BNY Mellon CGF voted to wind down on 2024-12-24; Amicus is merged into TIFIN Give; Philanthropi's site is dark ([queue-batch34-pass-through-channels.md](queue-batch34-pass-through-channels.md); [queue-batch32-feeds-owners-codebases.md](queue-batch32-feeds-owners-codebases.md)). Give Interactive's claim needs a discount: its known clients hold $36.7bn against a "$40B+" claim, and its only demonstrated live community-foundation loss is East Bay ([queue-batch30-cf-coverage-give.md](queue-batch30-cf-coverage-give.md); wave 15). On the not-vulnerable side, Foundant keeps consolidating: it merged with SmartSimple (2024-08-13) and took in GivingData (May 2025) (wave 18).

**D. What entrants should avoid building.** Credential-based integration is now ruled out by more contracts. No donor contract read admits a software service as a delegate. Vanguard interested parties "must be individuals". DAFpay bars use on behalf of third parties. Endaoment's terms bar sharing API credentials and tokens. Schwab's Online Services Agreement treats shared credentials as authorising the recipient and caps Schwab's liability at $100 ([queue-batch36-agent-terms-controls.md](queue-batch36-agent-terms-controls.md); wave 21). Six of the top 20 sponsors (about $84bn of assets) contractually limit automated or third-party access (wave 19). Sanctioned routes do exist: Endaoment's registered OAuth clients and keyed Partner API, and Daffy's corporate or trust Financial Advisor or Organizer. Build on those. Two "avoid" items are new. Avoid crypto dependence: The Giving Block earnout settled at $10.4m of a $246m maximum, and Fidelity Charitable's crypto inflow fell 88% in 2022 and 54% in 2025 (wave 14). And do not assume no-custody means no regulation: a white-label portal is a California Type E platform with light duties, and not holding funds removes the fundraiser bond in only some states (waves 14–15).

**E. What becomes more valuable.** Distribution gains detail. Fraternal insurers run their own sponsors (Thrivent, Knights of Columbus), Northwestern Mutual uses RCF, and Alera white-labels TIFIN Give (wave 19). Custodial and adviser data feeds are a distribution asset. Fidelity Charitable is the only sponsor named among Addepar's feeds, and Schwab's custodial feed carries DAF registration codes that Fidelity's and Pershing's, as documented by BridgeFT, do not (waves 16, 17, 21). Nonprofit-side access gains cost data. With fees not donor-covered, DAFpay costs nonprofits $5–$29 more per $1,000 than card on all five platforms with published comparisons ([queue-batch36-anonymity-feeds-fees.md](queue-batch36-anonymity-feeds-fees.md); wave 21). Grant latency is published as policy, not measurement, so measured latency is an open data asset (wave 21).

Voluntary account samples put the median account at about 9% (DAFRC 2024; 22% of accounts inactive in 2020–22). Sponsors under $50m pay out a median 11.7%, against 19.6% in the top 50, and 26.8% of them pay out under 5% ([direction-academic-literature.md](direction-academic-literature.md); [direction-long-tail-sponsors.md](direction-long-tail-sponsors.md); waves 24B, 30). Much of the spread in published payout rates is definitional. One study links donor-benefit website language to lower payout among nationals; the validator kept this row at Mixed (Low), because the measure is marketing language, not fees.
- **Concentration (A3).** Asset concentration is rising: CR5 went from 51.0% to 56.9% and HHI from 734 to 853 over FY2021–24. Grant concentration is falling: CR5 went from 53.6% to 46.6% and HHI from 803 to 653. Both are "unconcentrated" by HHI [I]. NPT passed DAFgiving360 for #2 in FY2024 ($42.12bn against $40.94bn) ([direction-league-table-concentration.md](direction-league-table-concentration.md); wave 29).
- **Portability (A8).** NPT now documents a donor export. Its fund-activity tutorial (created 2026-03-23) says donors can "export the data in any table to an Excel file" ([queue-batch38-vendors-ledgers-portals.md](queue-batch38-vendors-ledgers-portals.md); wave 23). So "no top-10 sponsor offers a donor export" no longer holds for NPT. No outbound transfer terms were found.
- **Regulation (A9).** The 2023 proposed regulations are still proposed. The docket holds 249 submissions, 13 of them new in May–June 2026. No state DAF payout, reporting or donor-intent mandate was found, but this was not a 50-state search ([direction-policy-lobbying-obbba.md](direction-policy-lobbying-obbba.md); [direction-state-daf-law-tracker.md](direction-state-daf-law-tracker.md); waves 24A, 25A).
- **Three new beliefs fail.** First, "a DAF is more tax-efficient than giving directly." For itemizers the tax is identical in every scenario. For non-itemizers a DAF loses from 2026, because DAF gifts are excluded from the new deduction: $720 against $0 over three years in profile H1 ([direction-donor-tax-scenarios-2026.md](direction-donor-tax-scenarios-2026.md); wave 27; illustrative, not tax advice). Second, "international giving is about 5.6% of DAF grants." Adding direct foreign grants gives 7.8% ([direction-foreign-grants-schedule-f.md](direction-foreign-grants-schedule-f.md); wave 30). Third, "community foundations send the largest share of grants to other DAFs." That holds only through SVCF. Excluding SVCF, community foundations send 5.1% of FY2023–24 Schedule I dollars to other sponsors, against 13.7% for nationals (wave 24B).

**B. What AI changes.** The flag-only rule still governs, and the new evidence supports it.
- **No product meets the RED test.** The census covers 30 products from 22 vendors. Only TIFIN Louise and Daffy's MCP server can move DAF money. Both are donor-side, with sponsor review downstream. Daffy documents no per-donation confirmation or cap ([direction-ai-product-census.md](direction-ai-product-census.md); wave 28).
- **Grantmaking tools set the control norm.** SmartSimple, Fluxx Finn and YourCause document opt-in, no training on customer data, and a human decision (provisional GREEN). Givebutter's stretch goals and suggested amounts change without per-action approval, so it is a GREEN/AMBER boundary case. Ren, Givinga and Foundation Source ship no generative AI. Foundation Source's CEO describes a roadmap tool that would flag scholarship applicants for review.
- **The AI work is at vendors.** AI is named in 29.1% of vendor job postings and 13.3% of sponsor postings. None of Fidelity Charitable's 11 roles mentions it. Chariot is hiring for agentic infrastructure ([direction-news-hiring-signals.md](direction-news-hiring-signals.md); wave 24B).
- **Eligibility errors are rare, so evidence is the product.** Grants to EINs under an IRS revocation were 0.082% of $91.02bn of top-50 Schedule I dollars ($74.8m). The core review list is $10.5m. Reliance turns on what the IRS had published at grant time, which Schedule I does not show ([direction-recipient-compliance-screen.md](direction-recipient-compliance-screen.md); wave 26). These are screening hits, not findings of improper grants.
- **Ratings licences constrain AI rankers.** Charity Navigator's API licence is non-sublicensable and bars using ratings "in any other calculation". It rates about 17% of Pub 78 records ([direction-charity-ratings-integrations.md](direction-charity-ratings-integrations.md); wave 28).
- **IP and security.** No 2021+ giving patent by a named DAF incumbent was found; this was a citation crawl, not a clearance search. The AI claims found are narrow. No DAF-linked issuer except Coinbase filed an Item 1.05 cyber 8-K ([direction-patents-security.md](direction-patents-security.md); wave 24A).

**C. Where incumbents are vulnerable.**
- **Community foundations carry a cost gap.** Their median published admin fee is 100 / 100 / 67 bps at $100k / $1m / $10m. That is about 1.7x, 2.2x and 3.6–4.7x the nationals' 60 / 45 / 14.3–18.8 bps, before investment costs ([direction-cf-consolidation-fees.md](direction-cf-consolidation-fees.md); wave 25A). Pooled staff cost is 78.7 bps of DAF assets at community foundations against 2.9 bps at affiliated sponsors. Median CF top-officer pay is $725,134 and barely scales with size (elasticity 0.23) ([direction-sponsor-executive-compensation.md](direction-sponsor-executive-compensation.md); wave 28). Advertising costs community foundations $7,981 per net new account, and more advertising does not rank with faster account growth (ρ −0.25, n=55) ([direction-sponsor-marketing-spend.md](direction-sponsor-marketing-spend.md); wave 28). Consolidation has not closed the gap: no two top-100 CFs combined in 2021–26, and only 5 of 803 CF filers reported a large disposition or final return.
- **Nationals' costs sit off the 990.** Fidelity's asset-allocation pools trail benchmarks by 0.58–0.76 pp over five years. Each trails its own underlying fund by 0.59–0.64 pp, about the size of Fidelity's 0.60% first-tier admin fee [I] ([direction-investment-menus-performance.md](direction-investment-menus-performance.md); wave 27). Ten of 12 affiliated-commercial sponsors carry no paid executive on their own 990. Program-service revenue is $0 at all 15 affiliated sponsors, so the DAF fee is an internal transfer ([direction-sponsor-financial-resilience.md](direction-sponsor-financial-resilience.md); wave 29).
- **Independent nationals have thin own reserves on the 990.** Median non-DAF net assets cover 2.2 months of operating cost, against 29.4 months at affiliated nationals. This is a bound, not a measure (wave 29).
- **Service is unpublished.** No readable top-20 sponsor publishes a response-time promise, a public chat channel or a WCAG conformance claim. Automated checks found critical failures on 7 of 37 pages, at NPT, Vanguard, SVCF and NCF ([direction-sponsor-accessibility-service.md](direction-sponsor-accessibility-service.md); wave 25A). These are partial automated checks, not a ranking.
- **Vendor concentration.** Ren's named receipts in sponsor 990s were at least $44.98m in TY2024, from 18 sponsors. RCF and the two Morgan Stanley programs supplied 85.8% (wave 22). Give Interactive's "$40B+" matches its known clients at 2025 asset levels ($41.80bn), but $9.21bn (corrected from $9.81bn; [crtsh-resourcing-2026-11-19.md](crtsh-resourcing-2026-11-19.md)) of that rests on certificate-log hosts; without them it is $32.59bn (wave 23), of which $27.01bn is Vanguard Charitable, which rests on Give's own release; no current client names Give in a 990 contractor line.
- **Not vulnerable.** Fidelity held 20.5–21.7% of assets every year. Foundant serves 282 of the 351 tail community foundations with an identified vendor ([direction-long-tail-sponsors.md](direction-long-tail-sponsors.md); wave 30).

**D. What entrants should avoid building.**
- **Do not wait for an open rail.** No open, multi-sponsor DAF grant-request standard was found. DAF Direct is closed and has 3 sponsors. Chariot's guide is the nearest spec, but Chariot certifies each integration and does not require "an exact API specification" ([direction-data-standards-interoperability.md](direction-data-standards-interoperability.md); wave 25B). Build on sanctioned sponsor routes (section above).
- **Do not blend licensed ratings into an AI score without written clearance** (Charity Navigator terms, wave 28). Do not rely on Candid API data after termination (waves 14–15).
- **Do not sell a DAF as tax-superior** to a direct gift (wave 27).
- **Do not enter the long tail as a standalone business** [I]. The 1,240 sponsors under $50m hold $9.18bn, 2.84% of assets. Their whole admin-fee pool is about $138m a year if every tail sponsor charged like the median of the published tail-CF schedules (150 bps at $100k), or about $90m if only CFs charge, within about $76m–$184m. That rests on 6–7 published schedules: only 11.7% of 60 sampled tail sponsors publish a rate. It replaces wave 30's $92m at the top-100 CF median of 100 bps ([b4501-04-tail-fee-pool-revision.md](b4501-04-tail-fee-pool-revision.md)) [C on those assumptions] [b4501-04] [V wave 35A]
- **Do not build a corporate-DAF product without equity-gift operations** [I]. The segment is small: Fidelity's 556 corporate DAFs granted $539.8m in 2025, 2.9% of its grants. 15 of 49 programs fund the DAF with shares or warrants ([direction-corporate-dafs.md](direction-corporate-dafs.md); wave 26).
- **Do not rebuild a complex-asset desk to compete on price** [I]. Fidelity charges no extra fee for non-publicly traded assets and averages 65 days to liquidity. Dechomai Asset Trust took in $525m–$986m a year in 2021–23 ([direction-noncash-gift-infrastructure.md](direction-noncash-gift-infrastructure.md); wave 25B).

### (c) What an AI-native entrant should do differently

Facts come from validated files. Each "so" clause is inference [I].

1. **Integrate sponsor by sponsor, through sanctioned routes.** There is no open grant-request standard, DAF Direct is closed, and Chariot certifies each integration (wave 25B). Donor contracts bar credential sharing (section above). So budget for per-sponsor integration and Chariot certification, not a single API [I].
2. **Ship agent access with scoped keys, per-action confirmation and flag-only review.** Daffy's MCP server moves money with no documented confirmation or cap; grantmaking tools already document opt-in, no-training and human-decision controls (wave 28). So match the grantmaking norm and exceed Daffy's [I].
3. **Treat nonprofit identity data as the product, not another checkout button.** DAFpay is on 13.7% of top donate pages and any DAF option on 41.2% (a lower bound). 44 of the 75 pages with an option give text instructions only (waves 24B, 26). Defaults, not donor choice, withhold contact data at most sponsors in the CF-weighted survey (wave 29). At the top 20, Fidelity and four others preselect full contact, and Vanguard, MS GIFT and BofA default to fund name only (wave 34A) [V wave 34A]. Nonprofits object to connectors that turn fee-free DAF grants into fee-bearing ones (practitioner reports, wave 29). So lead with remittance and identity data, and price it so nonprofits do not pay for gifts that now arrive free [I].
4. **Sell community foundations lower fixed cost, not lower headline fees.** CF fees are 1.7–4.7x the nationals', but CF pay barely scales with size (wave 28). So the offer is a shared administration layer that cuts per-account cost [I].
5. **Size the long tail honestly.** 1,240 sponsors hold 2.84% of assets, the median runs 24 DAFs, none has a published fee in the repo, and Foundant holds most mapped tail CFs (wave 30). So serve the tail only as part of a wider product, or through Foundant's installed base [I].
6. **Use IRS files for eligibility and keep your own evidence.** IRS lists are free and monthly; Candid's API bars retention after termination; Charity Navigator bars use of ratings "in any other calculation" (waves 14–15, 28). Revocation hits are 0.082% of dollars (wave 26). So the value is a dated, retained check log, and any ratings shown should be displayed, not blended [I].
7. **Market on in-kind stock and timing, not on DAF tax superiority.** Giving long-held stock in kind is the largest tax lever in every profile ($4,541, $28,770 and $191,157); bunching is not DAF-specific (wave 27). So position the DAF as an administration tool [I].
8. **Build payout and cross-border tracking early.** Peer markets set sponsor-level floors (wave 24A); a quarter of tail sponsors pay out under 5% (wave 30); foreign grants need equivalency or expenditure-responsibility files (wave 30). So these are low-cost features with regulatory option value [I].
9. **Grow through channels.** The fastest-growing sponsors are channel- or platform-led, and national sponsors' marketing is largely paid off the 990 (wave 28). So partner for distribution rather than buy ads [I].
