# Public and elite opinion on donor-advised funds, 2021–2026 (P-NEW-OPN-01)

**Prepared 2026-11-15. Desk research only. No pollster, sponsor, campaign or author was contacted. Nothing was logged into or submitted.** Recency rule: 2021–2026. One 2020 poll and the December 2020 launch of the Initiative to Accelerate Charitable Giving are kept only as baselines and are marked as such.

**Labels.** **[F]** means the source states it directly. **[C]** is our own count or arithmetic. **[I]** is an inference or judgment.

**Evidence grades.**
- **P:** primary document read (poll deck, sponsor page, op-ed text).
- **S:** secondary coverage read.
- **SS:** search-engine summary or headline only; the page was not opened, usually because robots.txt forbids it.

**Poll-quality grades** (our own scale):
- **A:** probability-based sample, with method published.
- **B:** large opt-in or online panel, with method and wording published and neutral wording.
- **C:** opt-in sample commissioned by an interested party, or leading or "informed" wording, or partial toplines.
- **D:** a sponsor's own customer survey with no method published, or a figure seen only in a search summary.

**Bottom line [I].** Public opinion is **not a constraint on DAF growth today**, because salience is too low. It is a **latent risk**: when told the facts in advocate-framed polls, about 70–80% of adults across ideologies back time limits or payout rules, and trust in wealthy donors' philanthropy is falling. Elite and legislative opinion, which decides policy, has so far run the other way. This matches the repo's policy finding that reform-side voices are nearly absent from lobbying and the docket.

## 1. Method and coverage

- **Searches.** Web search, standard and extended modes, for:
  - polls: Data for Progress, Morning Consult, YouGov, Ipsos, Harris, GSG, TargetPoint, Edelman/Independent Sector, Lilly School, Philanthropy Roundtable;
  - donor surveys: Fidelity Charitable, DAFgiving360/Schwab, Vanguard Charitable, BofA, DAFRC;
  - elite views: editorials, op-eds, think tanks (IPS, Roundtable, Urban/TPC, AEI, Brookings), campaigns (IACG, Patriotic Millionaires/Donor Revolt) and congressional letters.
- **robots.txt first.** A script read robots.txt on every host before any fetch, applying the repo convention:
  - 2xx is parsed for `*`, `Claude-User`, `ClaudeBot` and `anthropic-ai`;
  - 404 means no rules;
  - 401, 403 or an HTML or login page means **unreadable, so not fetched**;
  - other failures mean disallow.

  The log is in the scratchpad (`robots-log.json`).
  - **Not fetched because robots.txt was unreadable (403 or HTML):** dafgiving360.org, schwabcharitable.org, aboutschwab.com, businesswire.com, insidephilanthropy.com, candid.org, counterpunch.org, devex.com, taxnotes.com, city-journal.org, and api/downloads.regulations.gov.
  - **Not fetched because robots.txt disallows our agents:** philanthropy.com (the Chronicle), washingtonpost.com, bloomberg.com, bostonglobe.com, wealthmanagement.com, theconversation.com and clickorlando.com.
  - **Unreachable:** acceleratecharitablegiving.org and daflive.org (robots.txt request failed), so not fetched.
  - **Not searchable:** nytimes.com, wsj.com and ft.com are excluded by the search tool itself.
  - **Fetch refused (403) although robots.txt allowed it:** philanthropyroundtable.org and bakerinstitute.org. We did not retry or work around the refusal.
- **PDF handling.** PDFs were downloaded into a separate scratch directory and converted with `pdftotext`: the IPS 2024 deck, IS Trust 2025, the IS/TargetPoint Sept 2023 deck, the DAFRC donor executive summary and the Fidelity 2026 Giving Report. The Fidelity report contains no attitude items.
- **No results invented.** Where a page did not show a percentage, the CSV says so ("no percentage visible", "not seen").

**Verbatim key items** (each ≤40 words; from [IPS24](https://inequality.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Summary-of-American-Opinions-on-Charitable-Reform-Measures_Final-3-6-24.pdf)):
- Payout: *"Congress should raise required foundation payout from 5 percent of assets to 10 percent annually -and require that donor-advised funds have a 10 percent payout to active charities - even if this reduces the overall amount…"*
- Timeline: *"Donors who get up front tax breaks when they put money into their donor advised charity accounts (DAFs) should have to grant that money to working charities within…?"*
- Deduction cap, with a leading preamble: *"Billionaires are opting out of paying their fair share of taxes, thanks to the charitable deduction."*

**Quality and sponsorship notes [I].**
1. **All DAF-specific policy items come from reform advocates.** IPS and Inequality.org are on the left. The Giving Review co-sponsored the 2024 poll and is a conservative-leaning reform voice. Both polls ran an "awareness" battery of advocacy-framed facts first, for example "as much as 74 cents of each dollar… subsidized". Their payout item bundles foundations and DAFs into one question, so DAF-only support cannot be isolated.
2. **Ipsos opt-in omnibus, n ≈ 1,000.** The stated ±3.5 is a credibility interval for a non-probability sample. The 2022→2024 within-5-years shift (+7) is larger than a single wave's interval, but no formal significance test is published.
3. **The only probability-based survey (Lilly/Hewlett) does not ask about DAF rules.** On foundations it points the other way: among those with a view, ~80% prefer perpetuity. Opinion on charitable vehicles is therefore highly sensitive to framing.
4. **Sector-association polls** (Independent Sector/TargetPoint) also use preambles that argue for the policy ("About 90% of Americans cannot claim a deduction…"). They are as leading as the IPS items, in the opposite policy direction.
5. **Sponsor-side or neutral polling on DAF payout was not found.** The Philanthropy Roundtable's 2025 polls were not readable: the site returns 403 to scripted fetches. No pollster's own omnibus (YouGov, Morning Consult, Data for Progress, Gallup) asks about DAFs.

**Movement 2021–2026 [C/I].**
- **DAF time limits:** firmer. Within 5 years went 72% → 79%; "take as long as want" went 25% → 18% (2022 → 2024, same sponsor and stem).
- **Payout:** flat at 69% → 71%.
- **Universal deduction:** flat-high at 85–88%; support for a higher cap rose from 74% to 83%.
- **Trust in wealthy donors' philanthropy:** rose to 34% in 2022, then fell steadily to 29% in 2025. Overall philanthropy trust fell to 29% in 2026.
- **Awareness of DAFs:** flat-low at 17–19% (Ipsos) and 11% familiar (Lilly).

**Reading [I].**
- DAF holders report **high satisfaction and tax-led reasons**: tax and growth reasons score 96–98% at BofA, and 62% at DAFRC on a stricter "a lot" scale.
- Sponsors' "gives more because of my DAF" figures of **67–77%** are self-reported counterfactuals from customer samples. They cannot test crowd-out (see the repo academic file, gap 1).
- **No donor survey found asks donors about payout rules, time limits or the 2023 proposed regulations.**
- Privacy matters to 80% of wealthy users (BofA). That aligns with the Roundtable's donor-privacy campaign and cuts against the 83% public support for disclosing large gifts in the IPS poll [I].

## 5. Synthesis: where opinion is and how it has moved

1. **Mass public: latent, framing-dependent support for time limits.**
   - When told DAFs have no payout rule, about 70% back a 10% payout and about 79% want grants within five years. Majorities hold on the right as well: 69% on payout, 74% on five years.
   - But only 11–19% know what a DAF is or how it works. And the only probability survey shows a preference for foundation perpetuity among those with a view.
   - So **public opinion is a resource advocates can mobilise, not a standing demand** [I].
2. **Distrust of wealthy donors is the moving variable.**
   - Independent Sector trust in HNW philanthropy fell from 34% to 29% (2022–2025), and overall philanthropy trust fell to 29% in 2026.
   - Harris and Give.org show majority feeling that the rich are not doing enough.
   - If DAFs become identified in public with billionaire giving, this trend is a headwind for sponsors [I].
3. **Donors are satisfied and tax-motivated.**
   - Holders cite tax efficiency (96–98%) and privacy (80%).
   - Nobody has asked them in public about payout rules.
   - Sponsors' "give more" claims of 67–77% are marketing-grade evidence.
4. **Elite opinion runs against reform at the decision points.** The anti side is institutional: the Roundtable, CFs, sponsors, Republican AGs, and bipartisan Ways and Means letters in 2022 and 2024.
   - The pro side has moved from a legislative push (ACE Act, 2021) to campaigns and research: the Donor Revolt (2024) and the IPS DAF report (2025).
   - The reform coalition has a notable left–right element (Giving Review, Grassley) but little organised money.
   - This mirrors the repo's lobbying and docket findings.
5. **Major-outlet editorial pages are largely silent on DAFs, as far as this pass could see.** The visible coverage is critical **news** framing (Bloomberg 2022, Boston Globe 2024) and, in 2025–26, **service journalism** on OBBBA bunching (WSJ). Editorial-board positions remain a documented gap (§6) [I].
6. **Implications for the repo's policy-risk view [I].**
   - Near-term federal DAF reform remains unlikely. OBBBA left DAFs untouched, and Ways and Means sentiment is protective.
   - The risk is **event-driven**: a scandal, Israel–Hamas-era scrutiny of DAF grantees, or a DAF-to-DAF recycling story could activate the ~70% latent public majority.
   - Trust trends make such an event more potent over time.

## 6. Limitations

- **Major outlets not reachable.**
  - NYT, WSJ and FT are excluded from the search tool.
  - WaPo, Bloomberg, the Boston Globe and the Chronicle of Philanthropy forbid our agents in robots.txt.
  - So we cannot say that editorial boards took no position, only that none was found.
  - The one NYT opinion item surfaced (Taparia and Buchanan, 2025-02-03) was seen as a headline only. Its DAF content is not assessed.
- **No Data for Progress, Morning Consult or Gallup DAF item found.** YouGov has only the Patagonia proxy.
- **Philanthropy Roundtable materials** (2025 McLaughlin poll, Nov 2025 privacy poll, payout report, comment letter) return 403 to scripted fetches, so they are graded SS.
- **Lilly (2023) full PDF** returned HTML to a scripted download, so only the release page was read.
- **IS trust series** values were read from chart text extraction. The HNW year ordering (28/34/33/31/29) is inferred from layout and agrees with the report's stated "−2" change and its 29% 2025 figure [C].
- **Coverage confusion.** Some outlets attribute the 2020 Giv3 poll's 72% to the 2022 IPS poll. The NonProfit Times reports 22% "within five years" without making clear that it is in addition to the 50% within two years. Both are noted in the CSV.
- **DS06.** Vanguard/Harris fieldwork dates, sample and average-gift figures come from search summaries. Two survey waves may be conflated.
