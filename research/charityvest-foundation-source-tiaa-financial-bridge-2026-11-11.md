# Charityvest / Vennfi / Foundation Source: sponsor-to-vendor financial bridge and TIAA wealth distribution
**Evidence reviewed: November 11, 2026 | U.S. DAF sector | Public documents only**

## Executive finding

**We found documented evidence that Charityvest has historically paid or agreed to pay a software-and-services provider using a percentage-based contract, but have not verified any exact FY2024 Charityvest payment to Foundation Source or Vennfi.**

Charityvest's [FY2019 financial statement PDF](https://www.charityvest.org/documents/Charityvest%202019%20Audited%20Financials.pdf), visibly marked **DRAFT**, states that affiliated for-profit **Vennfi, Inc.** supplied technology, services, people, resources and systems and that a comprehensive services agreement called for a monthly percentage-based payment. The percentage was not specified, and the note says no significant costs were incurred for those services as of year-end 2019 [V01]. We do **not** regard that 2019 document as an audited confirmation of the **2024** contract fee rate or payee.

Charityvest's [FY2022 Form 990 Schedule O](https://www.charityvest.org/documents/Charityvest%2C%20Inc.%20-%202022%20Form%20990%20-%20Public%20Inspection%20Copy.pdf) confirms management functions delegated to Vennfi from January 2020, including development, flexible products and support to DAF programs. Schedule M says Vennfi supported liquidation of publicly traded stock and cryptocurrency gifts. Schedule O states that **Vennfi paid $125,290 in 2022 compensation to an individual who chaired Charityvest**. That is *individual remuneration by Vennfi*, **not a dollar amount Charityvest paid to Vennfi**, and is not evidence of an improper related-party transaction by itself [V02]. These historic filings do establish a meaningful external service-provider arrangement.

Foundation Source acquired Vennfi on **October 17, 2024**. Charityvest remained an independent legal charitable sponsor, while the Foundation Source business acquired the technology and team [V06,V07]. This means **FY2024 spans both the Vennfi era and the post-acquisition Foundation Source era**; legal billing details for each period are not established.

**Separate and important new proof of distribution:** TIAA Wealth Management launched a donor-advised fund program on **September 28, 2026**. It identifies **Charityvest as the sponsor**, **Foundation Source as its technology infrastructure**, and **TIAA-CREF Individual & Institutional Services as the investment adviser**. TIAA's dated March 2026 Form ADV and May 2026 Reg BI disclosure identify investment advice fees paid from Charityvest's DAF and affiliated-fund incentives [V08,V09,V10]. This proves a real 2026 wealth-channel product and a fee path, but not the dollar amount, actual donor take-up, or Foundation Source's received share.

## 1. Financial evidence and the missing exact payee

| FY2024 Charityvest Form 990 | USD | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Total charity revenue | **$113,380,722** | Includes **$111,895,516 donated contributions**, which become sponsor charitable assets; **not software vendor revenue** [V03] |
| Total charity expenses | **$52,603,302** | Includes grants to other organizations, plus management and operations [V03] |
| Grants paid to other charities | **$51,791,362** | Flow-through charitable gifts, not Foundation Source technology fees [V04] |
| **Arithmetic total expenses other than grants** | **$811,940** | Difference of the two above totals. **NOT a named-payee vendor fee**, profit, subscription ARR, exact maximum cash paid, or an audited software cost [V03,V04] |
| Year-end charity net assets | **$103,172,624** | Sponsor's net charitable assets, not enterprise technology assets or revenue [V03] |

**Important:** The $811,940 is an expense-line accounting residual. It would be an upper bound on *fees expensed as non-grant operating costs within these same expense lines* if all such payments were classified that way. It is **not a reliable upper bound on all vendor cash payments**, on amounts paid by other contracting entities, on capitalized technology costs, or on revenues earned by Foundation Source in other channels. There is no defensible software gross-margin or DAF cost-per-fund calculation from these values.

The [Charityvest official financial reporting page](https://guide.charityvest.org/governance-and-legal/financial-reporting) links a **2024/2023 Smith & Howard audit PDF** and 2024 Form 990 [V05]. **The audit attachment could not be fetched for full content inspection through the available web environment**. The ProPublica PDF/XML links for the latest 2024 Form 990 also returned 403, but Form 990 summary numbers were visible via IRS-derived third-party extracts [V03,V04]. Therefore **no 2024 audited fee note was read**, and a Foundation Source 2024 invoice **must remain unknown**.

### Compare against RCF/Ren without false equivalence

Our earlier investigation established **$19,225,546 of FY2024 payments from Renaissance Charitable Foundation to Ren**, a named service-provider relationship in RCF audited financial statements [V14]. We have not established a comparable **Charityvest→Foundation Source** amount. RCF's fee structure, account base, assets, and operating obligations differ from Charityvest. Comparing the RCF named-provider payment to Charityvest's $811,940 **aggregate non-grant expenses** and declaring one company more profitable, expensive, or efficient would be **invalid**.

A valid parallel requires:
- Exact sponsor legal entity and recipient/payee legal entity for each billed service;
- Effective dates and pre/post-acquisition assignments;
- Administrative service fee base and percentage or flat fee;
- Accrual-paid reconciliation and vendor-side revenue recognition, including any intercompany entries;
- Grant volume, average rather than year-end assets, donated assets vs grant flows, cash vs invested funds, and category of service;
- Sponsor/technology separation and full actual service cost to the vendor.

## 2. Fee waterfall for Charityvest for Advisors and TIAA

**Public Charityvest for Advisors standard program fee structure** [V11,V12]:

1. **Donor irrevocably contributes** to a fund legally owned by **Charityvest Inc., EIN 81-2771871**.
2. **Cash account:** no account-level annual sponsor administration fee under published general terms. This is a donor product choice, **not a proof the Foundation Source software is free to serve**.
3. **Invested account:** charity sponsor fee begins at **60 basis points** on the first $500k, subject to **$100 annual minimum**, with blended lower tiers at larger balances. The sponsor receives the fee.
4. **Optional wealth-advisor fee:** an advisory firm may elect to assess a separate adviser fee on invested balances. Charityvest's general guide says this fee is paid through to the adviser. The firm's exact rate is its own choice.
5. **Technology/admin fee received by Foundation Source:** Charityvest openly states it pays Foundation Source for technology and administration, **but the rate and amount are unpublished in the materials obtained** [V06].
6. **Investment underlying expenses and affiliated-fund fees:** depend on actual portfolio, and are distinct from sponsor and adviser fees.

A general advisor partner guide says **no firm-level setup/platform fee in most configurations** and possible minimums for fully white-labeled programs [V12]. This is not a signed TIAA fee schedule.

**What TIAA specifically adds:** TIAA's [March 31, 2026 Form ADV Part 2A](https://www.tiaa.org/public/pdf/tc_adv_program.pdf) states Charityvest **appointed TIAA-CREF Individual & Institutional Services (TC Services) as investment adviser**, that TIAA's WIM builds the model portfolios, and that **TC Services receives an asset-based advisory fee from Charityvest**. The advisor recommendation to the donor is *point-in-time*, while TC Services' advisory duty to Charityvest is ongoing [V09]. TIAA's Sept. 28 launch names **seven TIAA portfolios** [V08]. No exact fee amount or investor fee stack for this branded program was verified.

## 3. Explicit TIAA conflict disclosures

This is stronger than a general possibility of misaligned incentives: TIAA's official **May 1, 2026 Regulation Best Interest disclosure, page 16**, explicitly describes an economic incentive to recommend its Charityvest-sponsored DAF because TIAA receives asset-based adviser compensation and affiliated investment funds can generate additional fees [V10].

TIAA's own [March 31, 2026 Form ADV, printed page 14](https://www.tiaa.org/public/pdf/tc_adv_program.pdf) additionally states that TIAA predominantly uses affiliated funds for the DAF model portfolios, and recognizes a corresponding conflict in recommending a DAF and in choosing affiliated investments [V09].

These are **disclosed economic conflicts**, **not proof of actual unsuitable advice, fraud, poor investment choices, or regulatory breach**. The correct product test is whether **advisor recommendations prioritize donor intent and the best eligible giving path**, especially direct charity donations and no-DAF decisions, compared with recommending a DAF that creates advisor fees.

### Standardized conflict/fitness controls to add across all advisor-led DAFs

| Control | What an evidence-based test would ask |
|---|---|
| **Direct versus DAF** | Would the advisor recommend a qualifying direct cash gift to a standard-deduction donor when 2026 nonitemizer rules favor direct giving? |
| **Gift amount and timing** | Is the donor already committed to give, and does the model compare same-year in-kind direct gifts and sponsor fees? |
| **Investment versus cash account** | Is the zero-admin cash account shown appropriately, rather than steering a donor to invested funds solely because those generate fees? |
| **Affiliated fund selection** | Are proprietary funds ranked using documented suitability/cost criteria compared with available alternatives, with disclosures? |
| **Incentive independence** | Are adviser compensation and fund sponsorship fees separated from automated or human charitable-vehicle recommendations? |
| **Consent and irrevocability** | Can a donor receive advice without automated, irreversible transfer or undisclosed additional authorization? |
| **Public conflict transparency** | Can the user distinguish sponsor administration fees, advice fees, underlying fund expenses, and technology vendor income? |
| **Independent review** | Are negative cases, exception overrides, and conflicts logged and reviewed independently? |

A disclosed conflict is a **measurable control requirement**, not a basis to condemn a particular provider. Comparable questions apply to TIFIN's model-sponsorship disclosures and other wealth-firm DAF arrangements already in the repository.

## 4. 2026 TIAA program changes the competitive picture

TIAA's announcement [V08] describes:
- TIAA digital-client integration for DAF opening and advice.
- **Charityvest** as legal sponsoring charity.
- **Foundation Source** as technology platform, not legal holder of DAF funds.
- **Seven TIAA investment model portfolios**, with investment advisory compensation accruing to the TIAA group.
- Electronic grant delivery and automated transaction processing (announced functionality, not independently tested).
- No public DAF-specific assets, donor activation rates, vendor fees or churn as of the launch announcement.

That relationship should be added to the **expanded market's institution/channel layer**, not as a second separate DAF system. **TIAA total managed assets around $1.6 trillion** are group-wide wealth/investment assets and **cannot** be assigned to the newly launched Charityvest DAF program [V08].

The existence of the Wealth Channel is real. What is **unverified** is whether Foundation Source keeps a large service fee per TIAA DAF account, whether TIAA drives sustained donor adoption, and how sponsor/wealth firm/technology expenses compare with Ren or TIFIN.

## 6. Highest value verification requests

**Charityvest**: The actual **2024/2023 audit Note on Service Arrangements/Related Parties**, full Form 990 Part IX (management, investment and information technology), 2025 audited notes, service payment register with actual payee, executed original Vennfi agreement and post-October-2024 amendment or novation.

**Foundation Source**: Reconcile any received fees from Charityvest to Vennfi acquisition closing date, consolidated revenue, future TIAA program license/share and cost to support cash versus invested fundholders; distinguish acquisition-based growth from organic vendor revenue.

**TIAA**: Current program-specific all-in donor fee schedule, which legal charity account is charged each service fee, fund-model investment expenses, affiliated-fund alternatives, conflict testing and TIAA DAF adoption metrics, contracts and revenue rights.

**Ren/RCF**: Use the already validated 2024 audited $19.23m service payment as a **named payee source**, not as a winner claim. Match service scope and actual customer cohort metrics before comparing.

**Cross-platform**: Request vendor-neutral `donor_intent -> correct_vehicle -> acceptance -> fund_and_accounting -> investment -> grant_approval -> payout` test evidence, with a neutral no-DAF/direct-donation control and all fee recipients reconciled.

## 7. Boundaries

- **Not established:** Charityvest's exact 2024 or 2025 payments to Foundation Source; current signed service terms; Foundation Source profit and ARR; TIAA program DAF assets, advisor rate, customer retention or actual observed misconduct.
- **Verified via public filings:** Charityvest 2022 named Vennfi service delegation, sponsor financial aggregates FY2024, TIAA 2026 DAF advisor duties and conflict disclosure, independent charity vs technology company relationship.
- **No assumption:** The 2019 Vennfi percentage agreement remained identical in 2024 or transferred without change upon acquisition.
- **No false margin claim:** Low visible charity overhead is not proof of a high-margin technology business or lower total donor cost.
- **No NDA and private data** from employer, clients, expert network participants or donor accounts used.
