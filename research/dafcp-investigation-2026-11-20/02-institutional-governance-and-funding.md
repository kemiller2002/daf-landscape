# The American College of Financial Services: governance, accreditation and funding

**Date:** 2026-11-20. **Evidence:** [DE001–DE015, DE036] in [`evidence-register.csv`](../../data/dafcp-investigation-2026-11-20/evidence-register.csv). Financial figures come from the College's IRS e-file XML (public mirror `gt990datalake-rawdata`). Object IDs are in the register.

## 1. Identity and history

- **Legal entity.** "The American College", EIN 23-1352008, a 501(c)(3) classified as a school (foundation code 11), IRS ruling 1938 [DE001].
- **Origins.** Founded in 1927 as The American College of Life Underwriters. The trade name "The American College of Financial Services" is used in marketing and in the 990 mission text [DE002]. No primary document dating either rename was found.
- **Location.** Headquartered at 630 Allendale Rd, King of Prussia, PA. It moved from the former Bryn Mawr campus around 2019 [R].
- **Merged foundation.** Its supporting organisation, The American College Foundation, merged into the College on 2020-09-16, transferring $49.7m [F, agent A07].

**Reading [I]:** institutional accreditation means the College is a legitimate degree-granting institution. It does not certify the psychometric quality of an individual designation. The 2022–23 probation concerned educational-effectiveness assessment and planning and resources. That is relevant context for a new product line, but it was resolved, and it is not evidence about DAFCP quality.

## 3. Governance

- **Board.** 20 voting members in FY2024, 18 of them independent [F]. Chair: Salene Hitchcock-Gear (Prudential). Trustees come mainly from life insurers and distribution firms: Prudential, Nationwide, State Farm, Securian, MassMutual, New York Life, Northwestern Mutual and NFP [DE010].
- **Trustees with links near the DAF business:**
  - Ellen Rinaldi (Vanguard, retired);
  - Cheri Lytle (JPMorgan Chase);
  - Joe Bergan (Edward Jones), listed in the Dec 2025 report but not on the current page.

  **No current trustee was found from Fidelity, Schwab/DAFgiving360, NPT, Vanguard Charitable, Ren, TIFIN or AEF** [DE010].
- **Disclosed business relationship (Schedule O).** A trustee sits on the OneAmerica board, whose CEO was also a trustee, and OneAmerica committed $1m to a College programme [F, agent A22].
- **Related-party transactions.** No Schedule L was filed for FY2020–FY2024 [DE008]. The conflict-of-interest policy and governing documents are available only on request [F].
- **Executive pay.** The President and CEO, George Nichols III (since 2018; formerly New York Life and NAIC president), received $1,096,751 in reportable compensation in FY2024 [DE009].
- **Center for Philanthropy and Social Impact (CPSI).**
  - Managing Director: Paul M. Caspersen, appointed June 2025. He holds the "Wallace Chair" and is also CAP and DAFCP programme director [DE027].
  - The funder of the Wallace Chair was not found.
  - CPSI's named partners are insurers: MassMutual, Penn Mutual, Securian, Guardian, Nationwide, Northwestern Mutual, OneAmerica and Pacific Life Foundation [DE011].
  - The CPSI team page lists only Caspersen and no advisory board [F, agent B].
- **Outside role of the programme director.** The College's own welcome release says Caspersen "is an advisor with Charitable Solutions, LLC" [DE027]. That firm's 2026 team page lists him as "Vice President, Charitable Trusts & Life Income Strategies" [DE026]. The DAFCP page does not show this. See [04](04-industry-relationships-and-influence-map.md).

- **The deficits are persistent.** Net assets fell about 29% from FY2020 to FY2024 [S].
- **Revenue depends on designations.** In FY2024 the Huebner School (designations) brought in $19.41m, and a new "Non Credit Designation" line brought in $2.48m [DE007].
- **Endowment.** The FY2024 Schedule D reports a year-end endowment of $4.54m against $43.9m at the start. The arithmetic does not reconcile, so this is probably a data-entry error in the filing [I]. It is not used in any conclusion.
- **The College is not a DAF sponsor.** `DonorAdvisedFundInd` is 0 in every year. As a gift recipient, it accepts DAF grants through Chariot's DAFpay [DE036].
- **Donor identities.** Schedule B is filed but is not public. The donor honor roll lists Fidelity Charitable Gift Fund ($100,000+) and Schwab Charitable and Vanguard Charitable ($10,000+). These undated listings are most likely donor-recommended DAF grants, not institutional sponsorship [I] [DE012].

**Reading [I]:** a credential that costs little to deliver online, charges $1,495 per student and $240 a year in recertification, and has an annual sponsor-funded forum attached is a rational product for an institution running deficits. That is a background incentive. It is not evidence about the course's quality or the College's intent.

## 5. Designations and credential maintenance

- **Recertification programme.** The Professional Recertification Program (PRP) covers 11 designations, DAFCP included. Requirements:
  - client-facing holders: 30 CE hours every two years, including 1 ethics hour, and $240 a year [DE013];
  - non-client-facing holders: 1 ethics hour and $155 a year [DE013].

  Holders who do not comply lose the right to use the marks after 31 March [DE013].
- **Code of Ethics.** Adopted in 1984 and enforced by a Certification Committee. Sanctions range from censure to revocation, and revocation requires a unanimous vote. **No public sanctions list was found.** FINRA records "None" for disciplined CAP designees [DE014, DE066].
- **Holder counts.** The College publishes no current holder counts. The most recent CAP figure is "over 2,500" (2022), which conflicts with "over 1,900 since 2003" [DE015]. ChFC: "more than 50,000" [F, agent D].
- **Designation launch years found:** CLU 1927; CAP 2003 (per the GCF sheet); RICP about 2013 [R]; TPCP 2024; DAFCP 2026.

## 6. Negative and unresolved findings

- Who funds the Wallace Chair; sponsorship dollar amounts for the 2025 certificate and the 2027 Forum. Only tier floors are public: Silver from $15,000, Exhibitor from $5,000 [DE037].
- Whether the PhD programme is active.
- A current DAFCP or CAP holder count.
- Any written content-governance policy for designation curricula.

## 7. Implications

1. The College's governance is **insurer-centric, not DAF-sponsor-centric**. Sponsor influence, if any, would enter through programme-level channels: co-development, the Forum, faculty. It would not come through the board.
2. The College runs designations as a revenue line under financial pressure. A cheap, short, prerequisite-free designation with recurring fees is consistent with that. Monitor whether DAFCP becomes a feeder into CAP: the Forum release advertises "pathways to" both DAFCP and CAP.
3. Credential quality rests on the College's own controls, not on third-party accreditation. The College's institutional accreditation history [DE003] is worth stating plainly, but it should not be overstated.
