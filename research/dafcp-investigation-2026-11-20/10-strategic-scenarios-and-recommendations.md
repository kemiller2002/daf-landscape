# DAFCP: strategic scenarios and recommendations for our DAF strategy

**Date:** 2026-11-20. **Data:** [`scenarios.csv`](../../data/dafcp-investigation-2026-11-20/scenarios.csv).

**No numerical probabilities are given.** There is no adoption time series, and the course is 11 weeks old, so a probability would be false precision under the EDF claim policy and the Clarity Constitution. Qualitative confidence labels are used instead.

## 1. Four futures

### Scenario A: industry standard

- **Description:** DAFCP becomes the recognised credential for DAF administrators and philanthropic advisers.
- **Leading indicators:**
  - CFP Board approval for CE;
  - a FINRA listing;
  - sponsors or broker-dealers reimbursing or requiring it;
  - published holder counts in the hundreds and rising;
  - "DAFCP preferred" appearing in postings;
  - DAF technology vendors joining the Forum.
- **Supporting evidence:**
  - it is the only DAF-specific designation [DE068];
  - the issuer is established;
  - it has an aligned forum funded by the four largest sponsors [DE038];
  - recertification creates a recurring holder base [DE013].
- **Counterarguments:**
  - no entry gate or third-party accreditation [DE005, DE017];
  - after 20 years CAP is only "preferred" [DE063];
  - reception has been indifferent [DE070].
- **Likely beneficiaries:** the College; the sponsors whose framing becomes the professional baseline; early holders.
- **Risks:** the sponsors' view of DAFs (aggregate payout, no portability or fee comparison) hardens into professional orthodoxy.
- **Confidence:** **Low**.

### Scenario B: an adviser marketing designation

- **Description:** visible on bios, with little effect on competence or on how sponsors and vendors are chosen.
- **Leading indicators:**
  - holders are mostly advisers using it on LinkedIn;
  - no change in sponsor or vendor selection;
  - coverage stays release-driven;
  - no exam transparency.
- **Supporting evidence:**
  - the launch framing ("market-proof their practices", DE016);
  - low cost and no prerequisite;
  - the College's revenue incentive [DE006].
- **Counterargument:** the early visible holders are sponsor staff, not advisers [DE062].
- **Likely beneficiaries:** the College; advisers looking for differentiation.
- **Risks:** credential inflation; clients may read a designation as competence.
- **Confidence:** **Medium**.

### Scenario C: a specialised credential for complex-gift, sponsor-staff and gift-planning roles

- **Description:** valuable mainly inside sponsors, community foundations and complex-gift practices.
- **Leading indicators:**
  - cohorts of sponsor and community-foundation staff;
  - uptake of the nonprofit discount;
  - complex-asset sessions dominating the Forum;
  - sponsors citing DAFCP in staff development.
- **Supporting evidence:**
  - the curriculum's emphasis on complex assets [DE044];
  - a noncash-administrator faculty cluster [DE026];
  - early holders at sponsors [DE062];
  - the $1,195 nonprofit price [DE018].
- **Counterargument:** the market is small, and CAP already serves gift planners.
- **Likely beneficiaries:** noncash specialists; sponsors; community foundations.
- **Risks:** a small market may not sustain investment in the curriculum.
- **Confidence:** **Medium**.

### Scenario D: limited adoption

- **Description:** fails to differentiate from CAP, CFP and sponsor CE.
- **Leading indicators:**
  - no holder counts by 2027–28;
  - weak attendance at the Forum;
  - DAFCP repositioned as a module inside CAP or as CE.
- **Supporting evidence:**
  - indifference signals [DE070];
  - free substitutes [DE068, DE071];
  - the issuer's financial pressure [DE006].
- **Counterarguments:**
  - first-mover position;
  - sponsor-funded distribution through the Forum.
- **Likely beneficiaries:** sponsors, whose own CE stays the default.
- **Risks:** early holders' investment is wasted.
- **Confidence:** **Medium**.

**Reading [I].** B, C and D are not mutually exclusive in the near term. The most consistent near-term picture is **C with elements of B**: a sponsor-staff and complex-gift credential that advisers also use for marketing. A only becomes plausible if CE-body and employer indicators appear.

**Reconsider if** any two Scenario A indicators appear within 12 months. If so, raise A to Low-Medium and revisit §2.

## 2. What DAFCP's emergence tells us about the DAF industry

1. **Professional norms are being set by incumbents.** The four largest national sponsors co-developed the precursor and fund the forum aligned with the curriculum. Noncash administrators and sponsor executives are among the named experts. Whoever writes the curriculum defines what a "competent" DAF professional knows [DE020, DE026–DE038].
2. **The canon is legal-financial, not operational.** Tax, assets and estate planning are in. Operations, data, payments and AI are out [DE057]. That fits the repository's finding that incentives tilt toward accumulation and away from technology that speeds money out the door ([industry-incentives-structural-analysis.md](../mission-2026-11-14/industry-incentives-structural-analysis.md) §1), though it is not proof of that finding.
3. **Complex assets are the professional frontier.** That is where judgement is scarce and fees are material: Dechomai paid $4.43m to its administrator in TY2024 [DE029].
4. **Sponsor-staff professionalisation is under way.** Early adopters are on the sponsor and community-foundation side [DE062]. As sponsor operations grow, staff training becomes a market of its own.
5. **Comparative transparency has no professional home.** No credential teaches advisers to compare sponsors on fees, payout distribution, portability or technology, and batch 37C found the same gap across 11 outlines. That gap is both a public-interest gap and an opening for us.

**Opportunities that connect professional expertise to measurable operational improvement** (prioritised):
1. **The platform-capability comparison**, because it directly informs sponsor and vendor selection.
2. **The operations module, piloted with one operations team.** Measure whether it changes a control, for example adding a call-back step before bank-detail changes.
3. **The complex-asset decision aid.** Measure time to gift acceptance and the number of escalations.

## 4. Risks to us

- **Being seen to attack an educational institution.** Mitigation: Guardrail posture; documented relationships only, with status labels; no influence claims.
- **Overweighting a small signal.** Mitigation: the scenarios carry qualitative confidence, and the monitoring plan is explicit.
- **Contamination of independence through the College channel.** Mitigation: contribute research rather than paying for sponsorship.
