# DAFCP: EDF assessment

**Date:** 2026-11-20.

**Framework.** EDF specification v0.3 and complexity levels, from `kemiller2002/echelon-diagnostic-framework` at `710d54c`. Claim-and-confidence policy v1.0.

**Repository rules applied** ([clarity-edf-application-guide.md](../mission-2026-11-14/clarity-edf-application-guide.md)):
- EDF is applied **only to observed outcomes**, never to hypotheses.
- Every analysis follows the nine-step sequence and includes all required output fields.
- Control-point ranking is a defensible ordering. It is not a score: the policy states that the dimensions are heuristics.
- The structural-versus-habit test is the repository's own.

**The eight evaluation dimensions in the brief are not EDF concepts.** They are institutional governance, credential quality, operational competence, industry incentives, technological readiness, organisational resilience, evidence quality and strategic consequences. §4 maps each dimension to where it is diagnosed. Where no outcome is observed, the dimension is marked "not diagnosable under EDF" and goes to Clarity instead.

## 1. Outcome O1 (EDF-1): the form in which DAFCP emerged

- **Primary system:** the College's DAF professional-education programme.
- **Context:** the US DAF market, which has $328bn in assets (DAF Research Collaborative, as cited by the College) [DE016], the national sponsors, the College's finances, the unfinished DAF regulations and OBBBA.
- **Focus:** why the credential took its observed form.
- **Outcome (factual).** In 2026 the College launched a prerequisite-free, about-30-hour online DAF designation. It followed a 2025 certificate that the College developed with four national sponsors. The DAFCP names no partner. 10 of its 22 named experts currently work for a DAF sponsor or a sponsor's administrator. A sponsor-funded forum is organised around its curriculum. Sources: [DE016–DE020, DE026–DE038].
- **Manifestations:**
  - the "neutral" self-description [DE048];
  - no employer disclosure on the programme page [DE019];
  - the curriculum's emphasis on complex assets [DE044];
  - omission of sponsor-comparative topics [DE046];
  - early holders at sponsors [DE062];
  - the same four Premier Partners at the Forum [DE037].
- **Origin network:**

  | Type | Origin | Evidence |
  |---|---|---|
  | Financial | Five consecutive operating deficits. Designations are the main revenue line. Recertification fees recur | [DE006, DE007, DE013] |
  | Financial | National sponsors fund adviser education: the 2025 certificate and the 2027 Forum | [DE020, DE037] |
  | Organizational | Existing CAP programme and Center for Philanthropy infrastructure. A new director hired in June 2025 | [DE027] |
  | Human | Long-standing practitioner networks. A Charitable Solutions founder has sat on the CAP Advisory Board since 2001; the director holds a concurrent Charitable Solutions role | [DE026, DE030] |
  | Environmental | Rapid DAF growth. Adviser demand claimed by the College. OBBBA changes create a need for tax advice | [DE016] |
  | Regulatory | No regulator requires a DAF credential. FINRA lists designations without regulating them. The proposed regulations are unfinished | [DE050, DE064, DE070] |

- **Propagation:**
  - *Financial:* sponsor sponsorship → event aligned with the curriculum → the curriculum's commercial reach.
  - *Human and organisational:* practitioner networks → named experts → topic emphasis on noncash assets, international grants and succession.
  - *Informational:* sponsor-funded research (DAFRC statistics) → course marketing and framing [DE047].
- **Evidence:**
  - Direct observation: every relationship and programme fact, re-read by the lead for the decisive items.
  - Inference: the motive weights (E1–E5) and any influence on content.
  - Gaps: who authored which module; sponsorship amounts; the governance policy.
- **Control points:**
  - CP1: the College's content-governance and disclosure policy, covering expert employers, review roles and partner funding.
  - CP2: third-party credential accreditation or exam transparency (blueprint and pass rates).
  - CP3: recognition decisions by employers and CE bodies (CFP Board, FINRA, broker-dealers).
  - CP4: independent comparative evidence available to learners, a role we can play.
  - CP5: sponsor decisions on Forum sponsorship.
- **Control-point ranking:**
  1. CP1 ranks first: high influence on trust, fully within the College's control, low cost.
  2. CP4 ranks second: within our control and low cost. Its influence is moderate, since it informs learners without changing the credential.
  3. CP2 ranks third: high influence on credibility, but costly for the College.
  4. CP3 ranks fourth: decisive for adoption, but outside both our and the College's control.
  5. CP5 ranks last: the sponsors control it, and changing it would reduce the funding the programme depends on.
- **Classification (structural or habit):** **mixed**.
  - *Structural:* the financial origins, meaning the College's revenue need and sponsor funding of education.
  - *Habit:* the organisational and informational origins, meaning the non-disclosure of expert employers and the omission of comparative topics shared across adviser curricula.

  The habit components have feasible control points within the College's authority, chiefly CP1.
- **Confidence:**
  - Outcome facts: High (claim state internal-demonstration).
  - Origin weights: Medium (suggestive).
- **Diagnostic sufficiency:** sufficient for our next responsible actions: interviews, one enrollment, monitoring and publishing complementary evidence. **Not sufficient** for any public claim that sponsors shape the content.
- **Unknowns:** OQ01, OQ02, OQ08 and OQ10.
- **Next responsible action:** interview the programme director and the provost about content governance (OQ01). Enrol one team member to read authorship attributions (OQ02).

## 2. Outcome O2 (EDF-0): no visible employer recognition 11 weeks after course access opened

- **Primary system:** the DAFCP credential in the labour market. **Context:** adviser and sponsor hiring. **Focus:** recognition.
- **Outcome:** as of 2026-11-20 there is no published holder count, no job posting, no FINRA listing and no sponsor requirement or reimbursement [DE061–DE064].
- **Manifestations:**
  - two self-declared holders;
  - omission from a roundup of 261 designations [DE062, DE070].
- **Origin network:**
  - Time: the course is about 11 weeks old.
  - Organizational: no experience gate or third-party accreditation that employers could rely on.
  - Environmental: free substitutes such as sponsor CE and DAFopedia; the CAP precedent of "preferred, never required".
- **Propagation:** informational. Employers do not yet see a signal.
- **Evidence:** direct observation of absences from searches. Job boards were blocked.
- **Control points:** College disclosure of holder counts; sponsor and employer HR policy; CE-body listings.
- **Ranking:** College disclosure first (cheap and controllable), then CE listings, then employer policy.
- **Confidence:** Medium that recognition is currently absent, given the blocked job boards.
- **Diagnostic sufficiency:** yes, for the decision not to treat DAFCP as a market standard now.
- **Unknowns:** enrolment and the mix of holders.
- **Next responsible action:** quarterly monitoring ([07 §5](07-market-adoption-evidence.md)).

## 3. Outcome O3 (EDF-1): public curriculum covers law, tax and complex assets but omits sponsor economics and operational technology

- **Primary system:** the DAFCP curriculum. **Context:** DAF adviser education generally, across 11 outlines in batch 37C. **Focus:** topic selection.
- **Outcome:** the public module text names prohibited transactions, OBBBA, complex assets, Notice 2017-73, international grants and succession. It does not name payout, fees, portability, inactivity, anonymity or technology architecture. AI is absent [DE042–[DE046], DE057].
- **Manifestations:**
  - the programme director's article treats payout and inactivity as "policy debates" outside the outline [DE047];
  - the only technology item is "DAF technology tools".
- **Origin network:**
  - Human: the faculty's expertise is concentrated in noncash gifts and planned giving [DE026].
  - Organizational: a course length of about 30 hours forces selection [DE018]. The College's philanthropy tradition (CAP) is planning-centred.
  - Financial: the sponsors who fund the companion Forum compete on fees, payout and portability [I].
  - Informational: adviser curricula across the field share the same omissions (batch 37C).
  - Environmental: technology education happens in vendor-led venues [DE060].
- **Propagation:** organisational and informational. The selection of topics passes to holders' beliefs and from there to client advice.
- **Evidence:**
  - Direct observation: the public outline.
  - Inference: that paid content mirrors the outline.
  - Gap: Module 6 content.
- **Control points:**
  - CP-a: the College's curriculum review, adding comparative economics and operations modules.
  - CP-b: an independent supplementary resource (ours).
  - CP-c: Forum agenda choices.
  - CP-d: CE-body topic requirements.
- **Ranking:**
  1. CP-b ranks first for us: fully controllable, low cost, moderate influence.
  2. CP-a ranks first for the College: high influence and controllable.
  3. CP-c ranks third.
  4. CP-d ranks fourth: slow and outside direct control.
- **Classification:** **mixed, leaning habit.**
  - Leaning habit: the shared field-wide omission, and the lack of course time spent on operations.
  - Structural element: the incentive of the funding sponsors not to emphasise fee comparison.
- **Confidence:**
  - Outcome on public text: High.
  - The origin weights: Low-Medium.
- **Diagnostic sufficiency:** sufficient to pilot complementary education. Not sufficient to say the paid content lacks these topics.
- **Unknowns:** OQ04 and OQ05.
- **Next responsible action:** obtain Module 6 content through authorised enrollment, then decide the scope of a complementary module.
