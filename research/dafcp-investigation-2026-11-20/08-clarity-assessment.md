**What Clarity does here.** Clarity does not diagnose. For each claim it asks three things:
- What do I know?
- What is currently justified?
- What would make me reconsider?

**Labels used:**
- Claim states and confidence labels come from the EDF claim-and-confidence policy, applied to repository findings as the repository guide specifies:
  - **internal-demonstration**: a fact observed by this research process and re-checked by its lead; this is not independent replication;
  - **suggestive**;
  - **unsupported**;
  - **contradicted**.
- **Posture** applies to *our decision* that depends on the claim. It is set by the Decision Posture Model: impact if wrong × correctability.

## 0. Admissibility screen (Constitution)

The following claims were screened out or reworded before analysis:
- **"The sponsors control DAFCP."** Rejected as an unbounded claim that substitutes motivation for incentives. It is replaced by C2, which is bounded to documented channels.
- **"DAFCP is marketing."** Rejected as moral or character framing. It is replaced by C5 and Scenario B, which name observable indicators.
- **"DAFCP will become the standard."** Rejected as prescription without a signal. It is replaced by Scenario A, with leading indicators.
- **Hero narrative.** Explanations that turn on the programme director's personal intent are excluded. Only incentives and structures are analysed (*Systems over heroes*; *Incentives over intent*).

## C1. Does a professional designation demonstrate professional competence?

- **Proposition:** holding DAFCP demonstrates competence to advise on, or administer, DAFs.
- **Observations:**
  - no entry requirement;
  - about 30 hours of coursework;
  - quizzes plus a proctored exam at 70%, with no published blueprint or pass rate [DE017];
  - one case study;
  - Code of Ethics, recertification CE [DE013, DE017, DE018].

  The 2025 predecessor required three years' experience [DE022].
- **Interpretation:** DAFCP evidences *tested knowledge* of DAF law and planning. It does not evidence *applied* or *operational* competence.
- **Assumptions:**
  - a proctored knowledge exam measures knowledge reliably, which is plausible but unverified without item statistics;
  - competence requires judgement under real cases, which the course tests only through a case study.
- **Supporting evidence:**
  - proctoring;
  - the 70% pass bar [DE017];
  - topic coverage of the core statutes [DE042–DE045].
- **Contradicting evidence:**
  - no experience gate;
  - no practicum (compare CSPG, DE067);
  - no published exam statistics;
  - visible omissions on fees, payout, portability and technology [DE046, DE057].
- **Alternative explanation:** the designation may be designed as an awareness credential for sponsor and nonprofit staff, not as a competence certification. The $1,195 nonprofit price and the early holders fit this reading.
- **Claim state and confidence:**
  - "DAFCP certifies knowledge": internal-demonstration, High.
  - "DAFCP demonstrates operational competence": unsupported, Medium-High that it does not.
- **What would change this:**
  - publication of the exam blueprint and pass rates;
  - addition of an experience requirement;
  - an assessed practicum;
  - paid content showing applied operational exercises.
- **Practical implication:** do not use DAFCP status as a proxy for operational capability when assessing sponsor staff, vendors or partners.
- **Our decision and posture:** using DAFCP as a hiring or partner filter is low impact and correctable, so the posture is **Fast**. The rule is: do not rely on it.

## C2. Does curriculum sponsorship imply curriculum influence?

- **Proposition:** DAF sponsors influenced DAFCP content.
- **Observations:**
  - the 2025 predecessor was co-developed with Fidelity Charitable, NPT, Vanguard Charitable and DAFgiving360 [DE020];
  - the same four are the 2027 Forum's Premier Partners, and the Forum's sessions map to the DAFCP curriculum [DE037, DE038];
  - among the 22 named experts are current NPT and DAFgiving360 executives [DE031, DE032], and six people at Dechomai's administrator, including the programme director [DE026, DE027];
  - the curriculum omits sponsor-comparative topics [DE046];
  - the DAFCP release names no partner [DE016];
  - no content-governance policy is published.
- **Interpretation:**
  - *Influence is structurally enabled* through lineage, money and people.
  - *Influence over DAFCP content is not demonstrated.*
- **Assumptions:**
  - the absence of a partner credit means no sponsor review; this is untested;
  - topic omissions reflect sponsor preference; the alternative is the field-wide norm, since batch 37C found the same gaps in 11 adviser-education outlines.
- **Supporting evidence:** [DE020, DE026–DE038, DE046].
- **Contradicting evidence:**
  - the director publicly names payout, transparency and inactivity as policy debates [DE047];
  - the 2025 agenda included a session on critiques [DE023];
  - Fidelity Charitable and Vanguard Charitable have no current staff on the faculty [DE034];
  - the same gaps appear in non-sponsor outlines such as CFP Board's.
- **Alternatives:**
  1. Expertise sourcing (the null hypothesis).
  2. Field-wide teaching norms.
  3. Sponsor channel strategy.

  All three fit the evidence. Only (3) implies influence.
- **Claim state and confidence:**
  - Relationship facts: internal-demonstration, High.
  - "Influence on content": suggestive, Low-Medium.
  - "No influence": unsupported.
- **What would change this:**
  - a written content-governance policy showing independent review, which would lower the claim;
  - evidence that sponsors reviewed or funded DAFCP modules, which would raise it;
  - the paid content teaching fees, payout distributions and portability evenhandedly, which would lower it.
- **Practical implication:** treat DAFCP's framing of contested topics as **interested until shown otherwise**. Do not describe it publicly as sponsor-controlled.
- **Our decision and posture:** what we say publicly about the credential's independence is high impact, because it carries reputational and relationship risk, and only partly correctable. The posture is **Guardrail**: state only documented relationships, with their status labels.

## C3. Does adoption by a major sponsor establish industry legitimacy?

- **Proposition:** sponsor involvement makes DAFCP a legitimate industry credential.
- **Observations:**
  - the major sponsors' documented involvement is institutional (the certificate and the Forum), not adoption: no sponsor has announced a requirement, reimbursement or staff completion [DE040, DE063];
  - the early self-declared holders are at CAF-family sponsors [DE062].
- **Interpretation:** endorsement by participation is not adoption, and adoption by employees is not legitimacy among the clients the credential serves (advisers and donors).
- **Assumption under test:** sponsor participation predicts industry uptake. The CAP precedent argues against strong uptake: after 20 years it is "preferred" only [DE063].
- **Claim state and confidence:** "major-sponsor adoption" is unsupported (no evidence), High confidence that it is not yet shown.
- **What would change this:**
  - announced sponsor reimbursement or requirement;
  - DAFCP in sponsor job postings;
  - CFP Board CE approval;
  - a FINRA listing.
- **Our decision and posture:** whether to treat DAFCP as a market standard in our materials is moderate impact and easily corrected, so the posture is **Experimental**: monitor the indicators quarterly.

## C4. Does the designation indicate maturation of the DAF market?

- **Proposition:** DAFCP's creation signals that the DAF market is maturing.
- **Observations:**
  - the College cites $328bn in assets and 19% growth [DE016];
  - a DAF-specific designation now exists for the first time [DE068];
  - the curriculum and faculty centre on complex assets and sponsor-side practice [DE026, DE044];
  - the early holders are sponsor staff [DE062];
  - technology is absent [DE057].
- **Interpretation:** the evidence supports *professionalisation of sponsor-side and complex-gift practice*. It does not support maturation of operations or technology.
- **Assumption:** credential supply follows demand. The competing explanation is that the issuer's revenue need and the sponsors' channel strategy create supply ahead of demand (E2 and E3 in [01 §6](01-executive-investigation-report.md)).
- **Claim state and confidence:**
  - "professionalisation of sponsor-side practice": suggestive, Medium;
  - "market maturation" in general: suggestive, Low-Medium.
- **What would change this:**
  - adviser-side uptake data, which would broaden the claim;
  - DAFCP stalling or folding into CAP, which would narrow it.
- **Implication:** the professional canon is consolidating around the incumbent sponsors' model of the DAF: tax and asset mechanics inside sponsor rules. It is not consolidating around comparative or operational evaluation.

## C5. Does its technology curriculum reflect the actual future of DAF administration?

- **Proposition:** DAFCP's technology content reflects where DAF administration is going.
- **Observations:**
  - one generic bullet, "DAF technology tools"; no AI [DE057];
  - in the market, the repository documents:
    - agentic donor AI (TIFIN Give's Louise; Daffy's MCP);
    - nonprofit-side AI (Chariot);
    - open-schema and portability problems;
    - payment-redirection controls ([06 §4](06-ai-and-technology-gap-analysis.md)).
- **Interpretation:** the public curriculum does not reflect the technology trajectory documented in the repository.
- **Assumption:** public outlines represent paid content. The test is OQ04.
- **Claim state and confidence:** "DAFCP does not reflect the future of DAF administration in public materials": internal-demonstration, High. "Paid content also omits it": suggestive, Medium.
- **What would change this:**
  - Module 6 demonstrating platforms or AI controls;
  - the College extending its AI tools to DAFCP [DE059];
  - technology vendors joining the Forum.
- **Implication:** a professional-education gap exists that the repository's evidence base could fill.
- **Our decision and posture:** building complementary technology education is moderate impact and reversible, so the posture is **Experimental** (pilot one module).

## C6. Is DAFCP independent because its issuer is an accredited nonprofit?

- **Observations:**
  - MSCHE accreditation is institutional, and the College was on probation in 2022–23 [DE003, DE004];
  - the designation has no third-party credential accreditation [DE005];
  - the board is insurer-dominated, with no DAF-sponsor trustee found [DE010];
  - no Schedule L filed [DE008].
- **Interpretation:** institutional status does not establish programme-level independence. The evidence relevant to independence sits at programme level (C2).
- **Claim state and confidence:** "issuer accreditation establishes credential independence" is unsupported, High confidence.
- **Implication:** cite the College's accreditation accurately, with its history, and do not use it as evidence about DAFCP.

## C7. Will DAFCP become an influential industry credential?

This is a forecast, so it is handled in [10](10-strategic-scenarios-and-recommendations.md).
- **Current justification:** low visible adoption and an indifferent reception [DE061–[DE064], DE070], against first-mover status and an aligned sponsor-funded event [DE038].
- **Justified posture:** monitor; do not invest in alignment.
- **Reconsider if** any two Scenario A indicators appear within 12 months.

## Clarity result (template sections)

- **Executive understanding.** DAFCP is a new, low-barrier knowledge designation. Its lineage, companion event and named experts are closely tied to sponsor-side practice. Its public curriculum is strong on law, tax and complex assets, and silent on sponsor economics and technology.
- **Clarity snapshot.** Ties are documented; influence is not. Adoption is not measurable yet.
- **Evidence landscape.** Primary pages for every relationship claim. No paid-content access. Adoption evidence is limited to snippets.
- **Assumption landscape.**
  - Public outlines represent paid content.
  - Absence of partner credit means no sponsor review.
  - Early holders represent the audience.
- **Calibration review.** Confidence is high on structure and relationships, low on content and influence. Effort is matched: no further desk research would close the content questions, so interviews and enrollment are the next step (Progressive Commitment).
- **Correctability review.** Our recommended actions (one enrollment, interviews, monitoring) are cheap and reversible. Public statements about independence are not, and are kept at Guardrail posture.
- **Monitoring plan.** The quarterly indicators in [07 §5](07-market-adoption-evidence.md) and [scenarios.csv](../../data/dafcp-investigation-2026-11-20/scenarios.csv).
- **Reassessment plan.** Re-run C2 after the OQ01/OQ02 interviews. Re-run C5 after the Module 6 review.
- **Learning priorities.** OQ01 (content governance), then OQ04 (Module 6), then OQ03 (adoption data).
- **Framework concepts most active:**
  - *Incentives over intent* (C2);
  - *Bounded truth* (C1, C3);
  - *Signals before moves* (C3, C7);
  - *Progressive commitment* (enrol before partnering).
- **Recommended next posture.** **Experimental** for engagement (enrol, interview, pilot complementary content). **Guardrail** for public characterisation.
- **Current best understanding.** DAFCP tells us more about who shapes DAF professional norms than about who is competent. It is worth studying and worth complementing with independent evidence, but not yet worth aligning to.

## Reconsideration criteria (whole assessment)

I will reconsider the assessment if any of these occurs:
- the College publishes a content-governance policy, or names DAFCP partners or reviewers;
- paid Module 6 or Module 4 content differs materially from the public outline;
- the College or a sponsor publishes holder counts above about 500, or any employer requires DAFCP;
- CFP Board approves DAFCP for CE, or FINRA lists it;
- DAF technology vendors become Forum sponsors or DAFCP contributors.
