# DAF Market Research Agenda: Beyond Ren

Status: proposed research backlog (2026-11-12). This document records questions and hypotheses, not validated competitive findings.

## Research objective
Map the donor-advised fund ecosystem independently of Ren. Distinguish sponsors, donor/advisor relationship owners, custodians, software providers, grant-disbursement infrastructure, and nonprofit verification services. Test whether administrative simplification and relationship continuity matter more than automating inherited workflows.

## Initial company universe (classification to verify)
- National sponsors: Fidelity Charitable; DAFgiving360 (Schwab); Vanguard Charitable; National Philanthropic Trust.
- Wealth channels: Goldman Sachs; Morgan Stanley GIFT; Bank of America; Raymond James; Edward Jones.
- Software and administration: Ren; TIFIN Give; Givinga; Foundation Source.
- Digital entrants: Daffy; Charityvest.
- Infrastructure: Chariot; DAF Direct; nonprofit-verification and payment providers.
- Independent/specialized sponsors: American Endowment Foundation; Renaissance Charitable Foundation; National Christian Foundation; ImpactAssets; Jewish Communal Fund; The Signatry; DonorsTrust.
- Community foundations: Silicon Valley Community Foundation; Chicago Community Trust; Greater Kansas City Community Foundation.
Do not treat every named organization as a direct Ren competitor; validate current names, status, roles, and affiliations.

## Workstreams
- [ ] Company registry: ownership, sponsors, customers, products, history, distribution, partnerships.
- [ ] Technology audit: architecture evidence, legacy dependencies, APIs, integrations, manual work, security, modernization.
- [ ] Grant approval: intake, eligibility, diligence, decision rights, exceptions, turnaround, audit trail.
- [ ] Charity vetting: duplicated checks, policy ambiguity, risk, marginal value of automation.
- [ ] Custody and investment: assets, options, liquidity, advisor retention, noncash gifts, fees.
- [ ] Unit economics: revenues, operating costs, scale, pricing, cross-subsidy, acquisition costs.
- [ ] Distribution: advisors, broker-dealers, custodians, platform and sponsor dependencies; map Edward Jones–Ren relationship with primary evidence.
- [ ] User experience: donor onboarding, grant request design, nonprofits, accessibility, support, data portability.
- [ ] Failures and consolidation: shutdowns, acquisitions, discontinued products, failed implementations; establish causes from evidence.
- [ ] Regulation: sponsor control, IRS obligations, payout definitions, reform scenarios, compliance costs.
- [ ] AI: identify use cases, baseline labor and error rates, measurable benefit, review requirements.
- [ ] Future structure: wealth transfer, generational retention, commoditization, new entrants.
- [ ] Public feedback: identify appropriate forums and communities, design neutral questions, document selection bias.
- [ ] Reporting methodology: normalize periods, sponsor-vs-fund payout, DAF-to-DAF transfers, and asset categories.

## Cross-company hypotheses to test
1. Is modern-looking software actually associated with lower operational cost and fewer handoffs?
2. Who owns and retains the donor relationship after advisor or generational transitions?
3. Does better intake/form design outperform more automated charity vetting?
4. Which administrative functions are commodities, and which genuinely differentiate?
5. Do switching costs and restricted portability protect incumbents?
6. Why have particular companies or implementations failed?
7. Do aggregate payout statistics mask fund-level differences?
8. Is the next advantage simpler operations and preserved relationships, not automation of legacy processes?

## Evidence protocol: Clarity + Echelon Diagnostic Framework
For each claim record: exact claim; primary source/date; independent corroboration; assumptions; contradictions; causal mechanism; alternative explanations; confidence (supported/partial/unknown/contradicted); implication; unresolved evidence requests. Separate facts from inference. Avoid converting absence of public evidence into a negative finding.

## First investigation sequence
1. Vanguard Charitable: administration, technology, grants, advisor continuity, costs.
2. Fidelity Charitable: scale and unit-economics benchmark.
3. TIFIN Give: white-label structure, sponsorship, integrations and custody.
4. National Philanthropic Trust: institutional partnerships and sponsor/service roles.
5. Compare against Ren and investigate Edward Jones distribution linkage.

## Deliverables
Company registry; market relationship map; standardized company scorecards; technology and grant-workflow audit; economics and payout methodology; failure-case library; sourced Clarity/EDF findings; research gaps and follow-up queue.

Every finding must be dated and sourced before it is treated as established.
