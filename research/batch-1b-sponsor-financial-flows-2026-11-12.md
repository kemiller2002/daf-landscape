### Accounting controls
1. Do **not** divide annual expenses by **year-end** net assets and label the quotient the actual fee rate; the appropriate denominator is average assets subject to that fee and eligible pools.
2. IRS Form 990 functional classifications and audited natural expense categories are not necessarily equivalent.
3. A sponsor's management or investment expense does not identify the payee. Need related-party notes, Schedule L/R, vendor contracts, advisor billing and sponsor investment pools to trace cash beneficiaries.
4. Underlying fund expense ratios may be netted in investment returns rather than appear as a separate sponsor investment-management expense. Do not call reported expense lines the total fee burden.
5. Fidelity audited financials explicitly state advisor-managed accounts are sponsor subaccounts, not separate entities, and donors have no legal or beneficial interest in them.

## Explicit $500k DAF account price scenario
**DAFgiving360:** sponsor administrative fee = 0.60% × $500k = **$3,000 annually** at constant balance; independent adviser hypothetical 0.50% = **$2,500**; total $5,500 before underlying investment expenses, transaction costs and special fees. Advisor maximum 1% is a cap, not typical fee. At $1m sponsor fee = $3,000 + 0.30% × $500k = **$4,500**; at 0.50% adviser = $5,000; combined $9,500 before underlying costs.
**Fidelity Charitable:** individual standard account schedule begins 0.60% or $100 minimum; CIAP may have distinct policies, eligibility and fee terms. Do not apply standard account schedule mechanically to CIAP without contract confirmation.

## Relationship retention: recipient and payer distinctions
- **Donor** transfers legal ownership; relationship may continue, but donor no longer owns charitable assets.
- **Sponsor** owns assets and pays/assesses sponsor program costs; has authority to approve investment manager.
- **RIA** may earn permitted sponsor-owned account advisory fees and/or household planning fees, but can lose fees on donated taxable AUM.
- **Custodian and affiliated asset managers** may earn administration, recordkeeping, custody and underlying investment fees; allocation remains unverified from totals.
- **Pave Finance** could sell SaaS to the RIA or sponsor; no evidence its fee is directly charged to DAF assets or that it has sponsor integration.
- **Nonprofit recipient** receives eventual grants; fees reduce the charitable assets available over time.

## Clarity falsification
**Claim:** DAF sponsorship is a wealth-management retention engine. **Evidence:** adviser-managed programs exist; no causal retention cohort evidence.
**Claim:** AEF $35.95m investment fees represent an external technology market. **Unsupported:** these are investment-management expenses, not software TAM or proof of any particular payee.
**Claim:** Fidelity $179.9m administration is Fidelity parent revenue. **Unsupported:** audited Gift Fund expense classification alone cannot identify affiliated recipients.
**Claim:** Pave can monetize all adviser-managed DAF assets. **Unsupported:** contractual mandate, software purchasing and existing vendor competition must be shown.
**Claim:** Lower sponsor minimum means high profitability. **Unsupported:** sponsor/admin fees, adviser servicing, technology license and small-account economics can make accounts uneconomic.

## EDF causal map
Donor contribution→sponsor ownership→investment allocation/approved RIA→custodian/fund manager→sponsor and advisory fee debits→grant recommendations→grant settlement. Key failure mechanisms: wrong legal-entity attribution, duplicated fee pools, inconsistent periods, year-end denominator, fee cap mistaken for realized fee, conflicts between adviser revenue and charitable mission, donor confusion over asset ownership.
