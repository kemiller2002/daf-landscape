# Pave Finance and donor-advised funds: strategic market-entry study
**Date:** 2026-11-11
**Decision status:** Investigate partnership-led entry; **do not** recommend launching a sponsor without economic and regulatory validation. Independent industry research, not commissioned by or affiliated with Pave.

Company says 170+ custodian integrations, 30,000+ managed accounts and '$130B assets from managers using Pave'. This is **not** Pave-owned AUM, DAF assets or contractually addressable assets. Source: https://www.pavefinance.com/

## 3. Mechanisms and conflicts
- **Tax-aware contribution selection:** Pave's portfolio intelligence could help advisers identify highly appreciated lots for donor discussion, subject to tax/legal review. Donating appreciated securities is not the same as tax-loss harvesting; gifting low-basis assets may remove potential capital gains but reduces investable client assets. The right recommendation depends on itemization, AGI limits, holding period, liquidity and donor goals. Do not promise universal tax advantage.
- **Managed DAF investment accounts:** Sponsor legally owns donated assets; donor advisers can generally recommend, not command, investments. Investment adviser authority and fees require sponsor contracts, not merely donor consent. Some sponsors permit outside advisers above thresholds; investigate individually.
- **Cross-business economics:** Pave may monetize software or adviser services while sponsors collect administration fees and investment managers collect portfolio fees. Avoid double counting managed assets and affiliated fee flows. Donation may reduce the RIA's ordinary taxable AUM, so philanthropy can cannibalize existing fees unless the firm manages the sponsor-held assets or retains household relationship.
- **Custodian versus sponsor:** Fidelity custody API and Schwab RIA integrations do not grant access to Fidelity Charitable or DAFgiving360 administration and grant APIs.
- **Conflict of interest:** An adviser recommending a charitable contribution while retaining investment fees requires transparent conflict disclosure, best-interest/fiduciary analysis and appropriate sponsor oversight. No assumption that adviser-managed DAF fees are permissible for all sponsors.

## 4. Candidate partner categories
1. Independent DAF sponsors with adviser-managed accounts and willingness to outsource investment operations.
2. Community foundations offering externally advised investment accounts.
3. Wealth managers already using Pave, seeking philanthropic planning tools.
4. Custodians supporting sponsor-owned investment accounts, with documented investment authority.
5. Tax/estate planning software and adviser CRM partners.

Vanguard Charitable, Fidelity Charitable, DAFgiving360 and NPT are comparison cases, **not verified Pave partners**. Large sponsors may have existing affiliated or contracted investment solutions and limited incentive to replace them.

## 5. Economics: illustrative, not forecast
Assume Pave charges $X per RIA firm per month or Y basis points on *contractually eligible* sponsor-managed assets. Validate actual pricing, minimums, advisory fee permissions and marginal service costs.

Scenario only: 10 sponsor partners × $50m each **eligible** invested DAF assets × 5 bps = $250,000 annual gross revenue, **before** acquisition, servicing, compliance and partner costs. At 2 bps = $100,000; at 10 bps = $500,000. These are arbitrary hypothetical prices and assets, not market evidence. Software licensing per adviser may be preferable to asset-based fee where sponsor contracts disallow advisory billing or where assets vary.

More useful model: gross contribution → tax-aware lot selection → transfer accepted by sponsor → sponsor investment mandate → net eligible assets managed → retained fees → compliance/operating costs → contribution margin. Separately quantify **AUM cannibalization** in taxable client accounts and any adviser relationship retention; don't count same assets twice.

## 6. Clarity hypotheses to falsify
H1: Pave's direct indexing capabilities create incremental value for philanthropic planning. **Test:** advisers actually need and pay for lot-selection workflow; competing tools may already do it.
H2: DAF sponsors need personalized investment optimization. **Test:** sponsor portfolios may be mostly pooled, passive, low-cost and standardized; customization may conflict with grant liquidity and committee mandates.
H3: Custodian integrations confer DAF distribution advantage. **Not established:** custodian accounts ≠ sponsor systems.
H4: DAF assets are addressable adviser AUM. **False as blanket claim:** sponsor investment authority, account eligibility and mandate determine access.
H5: Philanthropic advice improves retention. **Plausible but unproven:** need household-level controlled outcomes and conflict checks.
H6: Pave should own a DAF sponsor to capture economics. **Not established:** heavy operational and regulatory burden, potential conflict with neutral software positioning.
H7: Tax optimization and charitable gifting are naturally additive. **Sometimes:** gifting can reduce harvesting opportunities and create opportunity costs; optimize household objectives, not a single tax metric.

## 7. EDF diagnostic
**Inputs:** client holdings, lot basis, unrealized gain, donation budget, restrictions, charity/sponsor eligibility, donor privacy.
**Decisions:** suitability, donation candidate selection, funding timing, sponsor choice, investment policy, advisor authority, grants.
**Controls:** sponsor legal ownership, written advisory authority, privacy/consent, fee disclosure, audit trail, tax/legal review.
**Handoffs:** RIA→custodian→sponsor acceptance→investment manager→grant administration→payment processor→recipient.
**Failures:** duplicate asset counting, sale before in-kind gift, unavailable lots, sponsor restrictions, unintended fee conflicts, incomplete grant status, lost donor attribution.
**Outcomes:** donation completion, advisor time saved, avoided operational errors, donor value, eligible assets serviced, net revenue, charitable recipient outcomes.

## 8. 60-day evidence program (proposed, not executed)
**Days 1–15:** Verify 15–20 sponsor adviser-managed account policies and thresholds; Pave actual software/API fee structure and advisory authority; interview 5 RIAs with charitable clients; examine SEC ADV, regulatory roles and conflicts.
**Days 16–30:** Interview 5 sponsor CIO/operations leaders, 5 adviser teams, 5 donors or donor representatives; test willingness to pay and sponsor acceptance.
**Days 31–45:** Paper prototype lot-selection and DAF sponsor workflow; use synthetic data; evaluate integration without sponsor credentials; legal counsel review.
**Days 46–60:** 2 sponsor or RIA design partners, documented business case, gate decision.

## 9. Decision gates
**Proceed with RIA planning pilot** only if at least 3 independently sourced RIA teams identify recurrent workflow pain and a credible budget owner, and existing Pave functionality can address it without prohibited tax/legal claims.
**Proceed with sponsor investment pilot** only if at least 2 sponsors confirm eligible mandates and adviser authority, actual fee economics, data integration path and documented investment restrictions.
**Do not launch a sponsor** absent independent board/governance, compliance cost, charitable operations capacity, conflict controls and evidence of differentiated demand.
