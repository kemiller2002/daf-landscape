# Agent mission: DAF servicing economics and commercial entry
## Objective
Determine the minimum viable, resilient DAF servicing business at an assumed 20 basis points annually on average billable assets under administration (AUA). Established demand is not proof of our profitability. Work backward from price, service obligations and attainable customers. Complete research autonomously; no paid consultations, outreach, production implementation or invented evidence.

## 1. Price and scope
Treat 20 bps as owner-supplied assumption, not verified market rate. Test 5/10/15/20/25/30/35 bps, tiers, minimum annual sponsor commitments, implementation fees and exceptional work charges. Separate software licensing, staffed administration, legal sponsorship, custody and investment management. Normalize Ren/Stellar, TIFIN Give, Chariot and other comparables by scope, payer, retained sponsor work, billable average assets, pass-throughs, minimums and period. Trace actual payees from ADV, audited statements and IRS 990 contractor records. Do not infer contract prices from donor-facing fees or year-end assets. Evaluate pricing from sponsor and donor perspectives, including charitable outcomes and conflicts of interest.

## 2. Entire sponsor dataset
Process every available sponsor record, deduplicate EINs and mergers, preserve year and data quality. Extract DAF assets, beginning/end balance, counts, contributions, grants, grant counts if available, other fund types, vendor, technology, staffing, outsourcing, renewal/migration signals and buying constraints. Use average eligible assets where known; beginning/end proxy and year-end run rate explicitly labeled. Compute revenue at each fee, workload proxies, service cost ranges, contribution margin, onboarding cost, concentration, downturn exposure and realistic addressable share. Do not treat recipient rows as grant counts. Identify captive providers, recent migrations, multi-fund dependencies and unavailable mandates. Rank by attainable risk-adjusted contribution and evidence strength, not AUA alone. Produce full CSV, segment summaries, top 25 hypotheses and exclusions; no invented sales leads or probabilities.

## 3. Bottom-up organization
Challenge the existing five-FTE $1,177,750 fixed budget and $75 incremental cost per fund. Model founder-led 3, lean 5, established 8+ core employees, shared Echelon services, fractional specialists and outsourced coverage. For each role document salary, employer burden, productive hours, duties, authority, segregation, backup, peak coverage and hiring triggers. Research BLS OEWS/ECEC, public job postings, cloud/payment/identity data pricing, insurance and assurance evidence. Include engineering, security, audit, compliance, accounting, sales, implementation, migration, support, disaster recovery, legal, insurance and management. Separate startup, fixed, step-fixed, incremental, pass-through and sponsor-retained costs. Reconcile platform and staffed P&Ls; no double counting, no free founder labor (show cash and economic cost), no unsupported staffing minimum. Allocate shared costs explicitly. Model technology-only, staffed-first and combined strategies.

## 4. Activity-based cost and capacity
Map account opening, contributions, recurring payments, grants, charity verification including 180-day and event-driven checks, reconciliation, tax/reporting, donor service, investment interfaces, exceptions, complex assets, check handling, fraud, security, sponsor configuration and migration. Estimate volumes, minutes, loaded rates, vendor charges and capacity; label evidence quality. Compare conventional, AI-enhanced and redesigned minimum-touch workflows. Public replay is recipient-level, not production grant automation or handling-time evidence. Preserve safety, accessibility, sponsor control, human judgment and financial integrity. Test the $40 incremental labor allowance (48 minutes at $50/hour), distinguish core staff from additional capacity and add step-fixed hires. Identify workload tails and seasonal bottlenecks; do not count displaced work as savings.

## 5. Model and stress test
Build reproducible F# scenario model (research only, no production components) and tests, or use existing approved research tooling if required by repository governance; document choice. Revenue = average billable AUA * bps / 10000 plus separately identified fees. Costs = fixed + step-fixed + activity-driven variable + sponsor-specific costs. Calculate break-even AUA, minimum price, minimum sponsor commitment, 10/20/25/30% target margins, per-sponsor contribution, cash flow and capital. Test account balance distribution rather than mean alone, 1/2/5/10/20 sponsors, 100m–10bn AUA, 1k–100k accounts, asset shocks 10–50%, outflows, churn, concentration, December peaks, vendor price rises, security incidents, delayed onboarding, reduced automation and combined shocks. Model 6/12/18/24/36-month runway and staged hiring, cash collection and working capital. Show normal/adverse/severe cases, sensitivity and explicit failure conditions. Validate arithmetic, units, edge cases and monotonicity where appropriate; do not claim probabilistic forecasts without calibrated distributions.

## 6. Commercial strategy
Compare anchor sponsor, multiple smaller sponsors, advisor-distributed programs, white-label partnerships, software-first, administration-first and combined entry. Evaluate migration risk, procurement, switching costs, implementation effort, time to revenue, sales cycle, competitive response and customer concentration. Quantify sponsor savings only for equivalent scope. Investigate whether a minimum annual fee or hybrid asset/activity pricing is fair and protects resilience. Avoid incentives to delay grants, retain assets unnecessarily or reject legitimate complex cases. Identify the smallest credible launch and expansion path; distinguish market opportunity from signed business.
