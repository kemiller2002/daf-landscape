# DAF Commercial Opportunity and DAFCP Research Agenda

Date: 2026-11-20
Status: Research hypotheses, not validated demand
Repository: kemiller2002/daf-landscape

## Objective
Identify who will pay for independent DAF expertise, market intelligence, technology evaluation, operating-model redesign, and specialized software. Evaluate whether DAFCP certification materially improves access, credibility, referrals, or conversion compared with practical expertise alone.

## Buyer segments
1. Mid-sized DAF sponsors and community foundations.
2. Wealth managers, RIAs, broker-dealers, family offices.
3. DAF technology providers and infrastructure vendors.
4. Banks, trust companies, and new market entrants.
5. Private equity, venture capital, and strategic acquirers.
6. Nonprofits, universities, and institutional advancement teams.
7. Professional education and credentialing organizations.
8. Unexpected adjacent buyers discovered by research.

## Offers to validate
- Independent DAF technology audits and vendor comparisons (Ren, TIFIN Give, Chariot, proprietary platforms, other entrants).
- Operating model and workflow redesign, including AI governance and practical automation.
- Market intelligence and recurring competitive research.
- Investor commercial/technical due diligence.
- Advisor-centric DAF workflows and integrations.
- Narrowly scoped operational software where repeated customer evidence supports build.
- Training and professional education partnerships.

## Mandatory research questions
For every segment: who experiences pain, who holds budget, triggers, current alternatives, measurable economics, procurement constraints, credible buyer evidence, willingness to pay, accessible named prospects, and disconfirming evidence. Separate source facts, estimates, assumptions, and inference. Identify conflicts and missing evidence.

For DAFCP: curriculum, issuer, costs, maintenance, adoption, employer sponsorship, buyer preferences, differentiation versus CAP/CFP and hands-on experience, network/referral benefits, and credential-use constraints.

## Deliverables
- 50 named organization profiles with evidence, buyer roles, and source URLs.
- Competitive map including consultants, software providers, internal teams, and failed entrants.
- Opportunity scorecard with explicit weights, uncertainty, and falsification criteria.
- 10-20 interview hypotheses and a research-backed interview guide.
- 3-5 concrete service offers with buyer, deliverable, ROI hypothesis, pricing hypothesis, and pilot plan.
- Product opportunities only where repeatable demand is substantiated.
- Executive synthesis using Clarity and EDF as analytical methods, without treating their conclusions as verified facts.
- Evidence ledger and research gaps, plus prioritized next actions.

## Guardrails
Do not fabricate contacts, pricing, customer demand, adoption figures, or technical capabilities. Public research is not proof of willingness to pay. Respect privacy, terms of service, and licensing. Avoid unsolicited outreach without approval. Preserve and cite existing repository research; do not overwrite prior work. Use PR and required tests/checks for changes, do not merge incomplete work.
