## 2. How DAFs affect retention and AUM economics (evidence, not a causal claim)

**Adviser-managed DAF assets are large, and fees are paid out of sponsor assets** [F]
- **Fidelity CIAP** adviser-managed investments were $18.1bn (FY2022), $22.4bn (FY2023), $27.5bn (FY2024) and $37.1bn (FY2025). FY2025 was 46.9% of investments and 43.5% of DAF value.
- Audit investment-management fees were $84.3m in FY2025. That implies about 26 bps of average CIAP assets, if the line is adviser fees only [I].
- **DAFgiving360**: 77% of assets were "associated with an advisor relationship" in CY2025 [R]. The adviser fee is capped at 1% on professionally managed accounts of $100k or more [F].

**Observed adviser fee policies** [F, ADV/program documents]
| Firm or program | Fee policy |
|---|---|
| IEQ Capital | Up to 1% on charitable platform assets |
| Winthrop Wealth (AEF) | 30–200 bps general schedule |
| Waverly | DAF fee may be "lower or higher" than the client schedule |
| FC Advisory | 0 bps on non-discretionary DAF assets |
| OneAscent OneGive | 55 bps, waivable |
| MS GIFT | 1.00% advisory cap plus 0.07% MSSB fee (repo synthesis) |
| AEF, sponsor-wide | 0.46% investment-management cost proxy (TY2024 990) |

**Bank white-label economics, from the Regions Charitable Fund guide (May 2026)** [F]
- Regions earns 0.60% on pooled accounts.
- RCF earns 0.65% admin on the first $500k.
- The guide's $1m example costs $11,000 a year (1.10%) before fund expenses.
- **Contrast:** Altruist/Endaoment from 50 bps with no minimums [R]; Charityvest retail 60 bps; RCF→Ren receives 98% of fundholder admin fees ($19.23m FY2024, repo).

**Stated-preference evidence** (TPI 2026 study, DAFgiving360-sponsored, via InvestmentNews) [R]
- 75% of HNW clients say they are more likely to choose a philanthropy-knowledgeable adviser, up from 40% in 2018.
- 90% of advisers say philanthropic conversations are good for business.

These are survey attitudes, not retention outcomes.

**Counter-evidence and caveats**
- No longitudinal or causal retention study was found.
- Contributions remove assets from taxable AUM, so cannibalization is real.
- Vanguard Charitable has no adviser-managed option [F].
- SEI's own launch coverage says giving should not be viewed mainly as a retention tool [R].
- The repository continuity study keeps "DAFs improve retention" as an untested hypothesis.

**Commercial implication** [I]: the sellable question is net economics per household:
- the adviser fee on sponsor assets;
- plus planning value;
- minus cannibalized taxable-AUM fees;
- compared across three routes: an open-architecture sponsor (CIAP / DAFgiving360 / AEF), a white-label, and an affiliated sponsor.

## 3. Entry economics and barriers for a new bank or entrant

**Routes, in rising order of commitment**
1. **Refer out / open architecture.**
   - Fidelity CIAP needs $100k per account to nominate an adviser.
   - DAFgiving360 needs $100k for adviser management.
   - NPT segregated accounts need $500k (discretionary) or $2m.
   - AEF requires an adviser on every fund and imposes no build.
   - Cost: near zero. Control: limited branding.
2. **White-label on a sponsor of record.**

   | Sponsor / vendor | Example channels | Published firm-level terms |
   |---|---|---|
   | RCF/Ren | Regions, First Citizens, EJ, LPL, Ameriprise | Admin about 60–65 bps first tier; channel-specific circulars |
   | GiveClear/TIFIN Give | SEI, AssetMark, Confluence, Alera, Ronald Blue (BlueGive) | Not published |
   | Charityvest/Foundation Source | TIAA, OneGive | "No firm-level setup/platform fee in most configurations", but possible minimums for fully white-labelled programs [F]; custom-program fees not published |
   | Endaoment | Altruist | Not published |
   | Foundation Source advisor-branded multi-custodial DAF (Sept 2025, with Charityvest, BridgeFT API) [R] | — | Not published |

   - Vendor platform payments seen in 990s run $118k–$498k a year per sponsor (median $218k, 18 sponsors) [F repo]. Stifel paid Ren $801k in CY2025 [F]. GiveClear paid TIFIN Give $185,921 [F].
3. **Form an affiliated sponsor.**
   - Requires a 501(c)(3), a board, and legal control and liability.
   - Trust accumulates slowly.
   - The repo's entrant analysis says to avoid it.
   - Examples are scale players only: MS GIFT, GS DAPF, BofA trust, RJ, Stifel, Bessemer.

**Minimum economics** [I, illustrative only, not market evidence]
- At the Regions 60 bps bank investment fee, a $100m pooled book yields $600k a year before staff and compliance.
- At the 990-observed median platform cost of about $218k a year, a stand-alone affiliated sponsor needs tens of millions in DAF assets just to cover technology.
- That is why sub-scale banks white-label.
- Price pressure (Altruist 50 bps all-in; Fidelity pools) squeezes bank programs that charge about 110 bps.

**Barriers observed**
- Sponsor legal control.
- Custody versus sponsor-system separation: custodian integrations do not grant sponsor API access [F].
- No public API rights in incumbent vendor templates (repo).
- Conflict disclosure burden (TIAA Reg BI; OneAscent ADV) [F].
- Legacy-portal risk.
- Exits happen. The repo records BNY voting on 2024-12-24 to stop operating its DAF program [R repo; not re-verified]. Its FY2024 row shows $4.7m left after $726m of grants.
