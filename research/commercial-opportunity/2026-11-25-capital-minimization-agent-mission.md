Agent Mission: Capital Minimization and AI-Native DAF Enterprise Design

1. Mission objective

Fundamentally reconsider the economic and organizational assumptions underlying the proposed DAF administration business.

Our previous research established that outsourced DAF administration is an existing commercial market and that 20 basis points is a credible working pricing assumption under certain conditions.

However, much of our economic modeling assumes conventional software development, conventional staffing structures, conventional departmental responsibilities, and conventional business formation.

We are not attempting to establish a conventional company that uses AI.

We are attempting to establish a company whose operating model is fundamentally different because AI, deterministic automation, reusable technology, and integrated business processes are available from its inception.

Do not begin with an organizational chart, staffing ratios, or an established company’s operating budget.

Begin with the outcomes the company must deliver.

Determine the minimum resources necessary to deliver those outcomes reliably, securely, profitably, and in compliance with applicable obligations.

Challenge the entire business model, including our own previous conclusions.

3. Challenge conventional thinking

For every major assumption, ask:

* Why does this assumption exist?
* Is it based on a legal obligation, economic necessity, historical convention, or organizational habit?
* Would this requirement exist if the business were designed today?
* Does it assume humans must perform work that technology could perform?
* Does it assume separate departments must exist?
* Does it assume information must move between people or systems?
* Does it assume software development must follow conventional staffing models?
* Does it assume a company must own capabilities it could obtain externally?
* Can the underlying problem be prevented?
* Can the activity be eliminated entirely?

Do not assume unconventional approaches are automatically superior.

Compare alternatives using cost, reliability, control effectiveness, complexity, customer experience, resilience, and scalability.

Maintain an explicit register of conventional assumptions and their proposed replacements.

5. Eliminate work before automating it

Investigate every recurring administrative activity.

Apply the following order of preference:

1. Prevent the problem that creates the work.
2. Eliminate the activity.
3. Consolidate overlapping activities.
4. Simplify the activity.
5. Automate deterministically.
6. Apply AI where ambiguity or judgment makes it valuable.
7. Escalate to humans when necessary.

Investigate whether:

* Reconciliation can occur continuously rather than periodically.
* Audit evidence can be generated automatically during transactions.
* Financial reports can be produced directly from authoritative records.
* Customer questions can be prevented through better information presentation.
* Errors can be prevented through input design.
* Charity verification can become continuous monitoring.
* Sponsor configuration can replace custom engineering.
* Operational problems can be detected before customers encounter them.
* Management reporting can be generated without employees preparing reports.
* Customer onboarding can become a repeatable, largely automated process.

Measure the work eliminated, not merely the number of automated tasks.

Do not count work transferred to sponsors, donors, charities, or other employees as eliminated.

* Incremental costs.

15. Minimize startup capital through sequencing

Do not assume the company must be fully established before revenue generation begins.

Investigate alternative sequences:

Sequence A: Conventional formation

Raise capital, hire employees, develop the product, acquire customers, and begin operations.

Sequence B: Product-first development

Develop Unbound using shared Echelon capabilities before establishing a staffed servicing operation.

Sequence C: Customer-funded development

Secure a design partner that funds part of the required development and implementation.

Sequence D: Narrow commercial entry

Begin with a limited, commercially valuable capability and expand the service offering incrementally.

Sequence E: Existing provider partnership

Provide selected technology or administration capabilities to an existing sponsor or servicer.

Sequence F: Founder-led development

Maintain minimal fixed cash expenses until product and customer evidence justify expansion.

Sequence G: Hybrid formation

Combine the strongest elements of the other approaches.

For each sequence, calculate:

* Initial capital.
* Monthly cash burn.
* Time to first revenue.
* Time to recurring revenue.
* Time to profitability.
* Customer dependencies.
* Product dependencies.
* Commercial risks.
* Operational risks.
* Reversibility.

Identify which expenditures can be deferred until a specific milestone is reached.

Do not confuse deferred costs with eliminated costs.
