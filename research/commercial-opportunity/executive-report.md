# Echelon Foundry and the DAF market: executive report

**Date:** 2026-11-20. **Status:** a public-source investigation.
- **No buyer has been interviewed and no prospect contacted.**
- Every opportunity below is a hypothesis with evidence of **ability** to pay. None has evidence of **willingness** to pay.
- Package index: [README.md](README.md).

**Evidence references:**
- INV### refers to [the Phase 0 inventory](evidence/phase0-repository-inventory.md).
- DS## refers to [demand signals and process economics](market-validation/demand-signals-and-process-economics.md).
- CP## refers to [competition and pricing](competitive-landscape/competition-and-pricing.md).
- PS, PW and PV refer to prospect rows; DE### refers to the [DAFCP register](../../data/dafcp-investigation-2026-11-20/evidence-register.csv).

## 1. Answer in one paragraph

Build an **independent, evidence-led DAF advisory and intelligence practice**, not a software company, at least initially.

**The first market** is roughly 40–60 mid-size community foundations and faith or single-issue sponsors with $250m–$5bn of DAF assets, plus about 10 broker-dealer and bank programmes facing dated platform decisions. Sell them:
1. vendor-neutral platform assessment and selection;
2. migration assurance.

These are the needs with the most visible cost:
- 3–5 week grant freezes during migrations;
- lost donor history;
- vendor fees of $118k–416k a year;
- attrition from older platforms.

**Turn the existing evidence base into a market-intelligence subscription** for vendors, investors and wealth platforms. No analyst covers DAF platforms.

**Defer software** until at least three paying engagements reveal the same repeated workflow pain. The repository's own economics say redesign beats AI in most processes, and that sponsors under $250m cannot fund technology projects.

**Treat DAFCP as a research instrument, not a sales asset.**

## 3. Business-model options

| Model | Assessment | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Specialist consulting | Visible budgets at mid-size sponsors; trigger-driven, lumpy demand; competes with generalist consultancies | **Start here** (OF1 + OF6) |
| Independent industry research | Empty analyst slot; credibility asset; little direct revenue | **Start alongside**: free briefing, then paid tiers |
| Recurring intelligence subscription | Small buyer universe (fewer than 100 institutions) [I]; independence rules needed | **Second step** (OF3) |
| Technology advisory (AI readiness) | Crowded with generic AI consultancies; thin value pools | **Module of OF1**, not standalone |
| Product development | Fails the R8 repeat-demand test today | **Defer** |
| Hybrid advisory and software | Consulting can reveal repeated pain: complex-asset intake, eligibility evidence, contract benchmarks | **Destination option**, gated on evidence |
| Strategic partnerships | Generalist due-diligence firms and expert networks (OF4); community-foundation associations; education providers (OF10) | **Use for distribution**; avoid vendor partnerships that compromise independence |

**Sequencing.** Consulting engagements produce proprietary, consented operating data (turnaround, exceptions, contract terms). That data can seed:
- the benchmark cooperative (OF7);
- a product, if the same pain recurs.

This is the main route by which consulting could reveal repeatable product opportunities [I].

**Conflicts of interest.** Independent vendor assessment and paid vendor relationships are incompatible unless there are:
1. no paid ratings or placements;
2. vendor subscriptions limited to the standard intelligence product, with subscribers disclosed by category;
3. deal-level conflict checks for investor work (no diligence on a vendor that Echelon is assessing for a sponsor at the same time);
4. an information barrier and published funding disclosure.

The existing research is critical of several vendors (Ren, Benevity, YourCause), which may limit vendor-side revenue (PV notes).

| **OF6 Migration assurance and data continuity** | 3–5 week freezes; AEF two-year history limit; runbook exists; dated go-lives (Give Interactive, NPact sunset) | Short, lumpy engagements; vendors resist outside testing | No interviewee reports migration losses (Q7/Q10) |

**Close fourth: OF5 wealth-manager programme strategy.** It has dated triggers and strong economics but access is hard. It moves up if interviews show broker-dealers buy outside help.

## 6. 30-, 60- and 90-day plan

Actions marked † need owner authorisation, because they involve outward contact or representing Echelon Foundry.

**Days 1–30: evidence before selling**
- † Approve the interview programme and the outreach wording. Book 15–20 discovery conversations from the prospect list ([interview-program.md](market-validation/interview-program.md)).
- Publish one free, vendor-neutral artefact: a "DAF platform and vendor-footprint brief" from the 990 data and the migration watchlist. Include the independence policy. † Publication needs approval.
- Enrol one principal in DAFCP for research access.
- Write the conflict-of-interest and independence policy.
- Pre-draft OF1 and OF6 statements of work, priced in the hypothesis ranges.
- Refresh the FY2025 990 contractor lines for the 23 sponsor prospects.

**Days 31–60: test pricing with one pilot**
- Code the interviews against the rejection criteria. Kill or keep each offering.
- † Offer one discounted OF1 or OF6 pilot to a sponsor with a dated trigger, in exchange for a reference and anonymised publication rights.
- † Offer the first quarterly intelligence briefing to about 10 institutional prospects (vendors, investors, wealth platforms). Record willingness to pay.
- † Register with 1–2 expert networks. Approach one generalist due-diligence firm about a subcontract bench (OF4).

**Days 61–90: decide the business**
- Deliver the pilot. Measure hours against price (rejection criterion R7) and outcome metrics.
- Decide go or no-go for each offering, using the evidence thresholds in [offerings.md](service-offerings/offerings.md).
- If at least 3 institutions commit to intelligence at $5k or more, launch OF3 paid tiers.
- Re-score opportunities with interview data. Rerun the sensitivity analysis.
- Review software only if R8 is met: 3 or more buyers with the same pain and budget.

## 7. Clarity posture

- **Discovery and publication:** Fast.
- **Pilots:** Experimental.
- **Vendor-paid work:** Guardrail.
- **Software build:** Strategic, deferred.

**Reconsider the whole strategy if any of these occurs:**
- interviews show budgets sit with parent firms or generalist consultancies at 3 or more of the 4 mid-size sponsors interviewed;
- a vendor-neutral analyst enters the DAF platform space;
- a final §4966 rule creates a compliance-assessment wave that changes priorities.
