# 18: Executive findings

**Date:** 2026-11-25. **Status:** theoretical research from public sources and the owner's repositories. It is not a forecast, an investment recommendation or a launch authorization. Every number is conditional on labelled assumptions. The research passes, the adversarial reviewer and the integrator are the same model family, so **nothing here is independently validated**.

## Answer to the mission question

**Does an entirely different type of company emerge? No, not on present evidence.**
- Designing from outcomes, with AI, deterministic automation and Echelon from inception, produces **a somewhat cheaper, better-instrumented version of the same business**.
- Like for like at $1.5B, the redesigned company costs about **8% less** than the previous fully loaded case ($3.58M against $3.88M a year). That saving comes mainly from a cheaper *build assumption*, not from the organization.

The economics are set by things AI does not remove:
- legal and assurance obligations: sponsor reserved authority, OFAC, IRC 4966/4967, SOC 1 and SOC 2, three-person release before gate acceptance;
- a non-personnel floor of about $0.65M a year;
- platform engineering;
- buyer price at high balances;
- December release capacity.

Handling minutes, the variable AI moves most, are about 6% of revenue.

**A genuinely different economic structure becomes possible only under conditions that can be tested cheaply:**
1. measured agent-assisted development of 2x or better on *accepted* capabilities;
2. auditor and board acceptance of a deterministic release gate and generated evidence, which unlocks 30-minute operation;
3. the platform finding customers beyond the servicer, about 19-21k external funds, so it stops being a one-customer cost;
4. per-account pricing accepted by mid-balance sponsors.

## Does the AI-native organization hide costs?

**Partly, and the research makes them visible.** At $1B, 5 sponsors and 4,000 accounts, the adversarial hidden-cost ledger finds **$0.75-3.06M a year** (base $1.66M) not counted in the servicer model, plus $0.76M partly counted. The largest items:
- **Sponsors:** reserved decisions, appointees, migration, the payment rails passed through ($84.5k measured), the third release person and audit evidence. This sponsor-side subset is $0.17-1.30M.
- **Deferred scope.**
- **Echelon reliance.**

Agent compute is small. A sponsor CFO will price these costs even where the servicer's P&L does not carry them ([`hidden-costs.csv`](../../../data/commercial-opportunity/ai-native-capital-minimization/hidden-costs.csv)).

## Clarity and EDF

- **Clarity** was applied to five decisions: organisation size at go-live, build investment, entry sequence, pricing structure, and reliance on Echelon. All five are **not ready** under Clarity's readiness rule, because the owner has not yet accepted ownership, decision dates, review cadence and outcome measures. This report gives the options and evidence, not the decisions.
- **EDF** was applied only to observed outcomes: the Echelon development history, the Rehearsal 4 trial and the ordo cost trial. They show high output, but no demonstrated reduction in the cost of an accepted capability. EDF was **not** executed on the hypothetical company (report 16).
