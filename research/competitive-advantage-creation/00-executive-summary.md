# 00 - Executive summary

Status: research (2026-11-26). DAF Landscape Phase III: competitive advantage creation and capital minimization.

This package builds on the owner-approved Phase II decisions (DEC-001..012, approved 2026-11-26; [01](01-approved-strategic-baseline.md)).
- **No outreach, spending, contracts or production work was done.**
- **Figures are model outputs on labelled assumption ranges**, given as favourable / base / adverse. Nothing is measured.
- **Same-family review.** Research agents, the fifteen adversarial perspectives and the integrator are the same model family, so this is not independent assurance.

## The primary question

> How can Echelon Foundry create a commercially viable DAF technology business without requiring conventional capital, staffing, distribution or organizational structures?

**Answer:** only as a niche licensor whose customer is a channel partner's whole book of sponsors, and only if the 180-day charity deep review is performed by the sponsor or automated renewal is allowed. Even then, viability is unproven until a named partner, a net price and the review allocation are observed. On present evidence the right move is to **validate before spending**. The validation is cheap; the spending it would justify is not ([26](26-final-recommendations.md)).

## Key results (model revision 3, after two review rounds)

- **The decisive variable is who performs the 180-day deep review** (P3-DEC-MOD005, Unbound OD-CDD-02).
  - If Echelon performs it for every sponsor, no sequence pays back within 120 months.
  - If the sponsor performs it, or automated renewal is allowed, the partner-book sequence pays back at month 76-80 on a peak of about $2.4-2.6M.
- **Break-even size,** when the sponsor reviews or automated renewal is allowed: about 3,100-3,200 accounts for a single sponsor, or about 12,500-13,300 across a partner's book.
- **The kill rule.** With the cost center open from month 0, every sequence pauses at month 24, after about $1.3-1.4M. No sequence beats doing nothing on the median simulated outcome. If the cost center opens later, after the gate, A3 can pass.
- **Outside investment** is not justified before the gate. After the gate, the surviving route's peak must come from owner capacity (unknown) or customer billing terms.
- **The narrow products** (components, charity intelligence, migration assessments, compliance evidence) cannot finance Unbound.
- **Corrections.** Two adversarial rounds produced 150 challenges, each with a recorded disposition. They corrected the economics twice: an early lean result that was optimistic, then an over-corrected pessimistic one ([20](20-adversarial-review.md)).

## Recommendations

1. **Decide who performs the deep review, and OD-CDD-02, first.** Every other gate depends on them.
2. **Approve founder-led, no-cost outreach** for partner conversations about licensing a whole book (V-6), win/loss interviews (V-1) and price tests (V-2), with pre-registered pass rules. Run the $0 validations now: V-3 replay, IRS and sanctions archiving, and V-9 preparation.
3. **Open the DAF cost center only after the gate passes.** The gate needs a named partner with a whole-book term sheet, a net price at or above the walk-away floor, and a decided review allocation (P3-DEC-MOD003).
4. **Do not build:** a custodial administrator, a payment network, standalone intelligence or compliance products, vendor-specific packages before an authorised sample, adjacent-industry modules before a customer asks, or further DAF-specific build before the gate.
5. **Next initiative:** "Phase IV: gate evidence". Run the validation ladder and record results against the pass rules ([23](23-commercial-validation-roadmap.md)).
