## Answer in brief

0. **Revision 3 (CFO-corrected) drives this report; revision 2 is in the bases table of [16](16-capital-efficient-market-entry.md) section 1b.** The review allocation (P3-DEC-MOD005) is the largest single switch; the kill-rule result is timing-sensitive (section 7).
1. **Under the governing registry configuration, registry volume dominates.** For a completed Unbound (sequence A3), charities in use per account, variable cost per account, the registry mode itself (M1 versus M5, conditional on OD-CDD-02) and the partner share move peak cash and ten-year economics more than any other variable; build cost is minor (P3-CLM-MOD010).
2. **No base-case draw pays back with the kill rule applied, and almost all pause.** Most pauses come from the pilot test (one whole live customer by cost-center month + 24) or, at later named-partner months, from the named-partner test; timing assumptions without evidence drive them (P3-ASM-MOD034, P3-GAP-MOD002). Uninterrupted, the platform sequences have wide tails but no positive median.
3. **Correlation matters little at the median.** Demand and cost variables are correlated within groups; medians barely move between rho 0 and 0.5.
4. **Break-even conditions**: the break-even sponsor size (in [14](14-commercial-sequencing.md) section 5) is met by few FY2024 sponsors; a programme breaks even at a few dozen accounts when hosted; the lean licensor's walk-away price stays below Phase II's after funded control operations; a partner is cheaper than a direct sale only below a share that falls with sponsor size.
5. **The stresses that matter most are conventional run rates and partner terms.** A 100% partner share or a Phase II MD-6 run rate more than doubles the uninterrupted loss; SOC 1, conventional maintenance and paying the founder in cash each add to an already negative base.

## 2. Correlated Monte Carlo

2,000 triangular draws per sequence (seed 20261126, the Phase II seed), run with and without the kill rule and at rho 0 and 0.5. Partner-delivered sequences also draw a partner termination time from an exponential hazard (P3-ASM-FGJ016), so the paused column counts terminations even without the kill rule. Correlation uses Phase II's common-adverse-quantile method, applied within two groups only:
- **Demand** (month of first signing, customers per year, revenue per customer, churn, go-live lag, named-partner month): all depend on whether a partner or buyer exists and how much it values the product, so they plausibly fail together.
- **Cost** (development, one-time, pre-revenue and live run, CAC, implementation): buyer assurance requirements raise security, compliance, insurance and implementation at once (agent AB, report 02 section 8).

Other variables are independent; no correlation between demand and cost is assumed, because none is evidenced.
