## Answer in brief

1. **Sharing infrastructure lowers Unbound's cash cost only when another product earns revenue and the owner adopts a rule that charges it.** Under a revenue rule Unbound bears almost all of the pooled security, compliance, infrastructure, insurance and administration cost today, because no other Echelon product has revenue. Agent AB's S7 factor of 0.6 (P3-ASM-AB029) is one point inside a rule-dependent range (P3-CLM-MOD009).
2. **The five economic entities are kept apart.** Every allocation carries an entity; the model refuses to combine entities without a recorded justification (`SharedInfra.combineEntities`), following echelon-organization-administration `strategy/daf-entry/02`. The allocation rule is an explicit input; the owner has not chosen one (P3-DEC-MOD002).
3. **The charity registry is the clearest shared asset, but its governing cost is set by a requirement, not by architecture.** Under today's ratified requirements the 180-day deep review stays human (mode M1) until owner decision OD-CDD-02 allows automated renewal (REV3010, REV3020); M1 costs several times the hybrid mode M5 and grows with the union of charities as sponsors are added. M5's small marginal cost per sponsor (agent HI, P3-CLM-HI013) is conditional on OD-CDD-02, V-3 and V-9 (P3-CLM-MOD014, P3-DEC-MOD004).
4. **Do not add the registry to Phase II operating modes; do add it to the lean licensor.** Phase II modes already carry registry engineering in fixed cost and vetting labour in EV-E08, so adding the registry double counts; agent AB's lean run carries no registry at all (P3-CLM-MOD004, resolving P3-GAP-HI010).
5. **Transferred work is a cost to someone else, not shared infrastructure.** Rail partners, advisor firms and customers carry it; the event model reports it by party and never as a saving (P3-CLM-MOD008).

### GAP-HI010: is registry engineering already inside Phase II's mode fixed costs?

Traced from the code and sources:
- Phase II `Enterprise.fs` and `Analysis.fs` never add `Registry.fs` cost to a mode; the registry is a separate model.

- Phase II event EV-E08 prices vetting labour per account; agent HI shows that the registry stewardship hours price the same work (report 10 section 9, items 1 and 9).

## 4. What sharing can and cannot do

- **Can:** spread fixed assurance and infrastructure across products once each has revenue; reuse the registry's public layer across sponsors at a falling marginal cost; reuse onboarding packages (AB's S6) across customers.
- **Cannot:** remove the registry's accountable-owner floor, the sponsor's segregated decisions, buyer-driven assurance (SOC 2 at contract) or the founder's time; make a product with no revenue pay its share.
- **Decision needed (P3-DEC-MOD002):** an allocation rule recorded before any combined Echelon figure is presented, as `strategy/daf-entry/02` requires.

## 6. Revision 3 (round-2 review)

**Who performs the deep review.** Under licensing (DEC-006) the deep review is a sponsor control: RR-REQ-X-007-r1 makes the interval a sponsor policy and writes reliance to the sponsor's partition, and CNV-GRT-003 allows cross-sponsor evidence only as input (UNB-CDD-CIV-010). The model therefore treats the human deep review as per sponsor-charity, never shared across sponsors, and allocates it by a performer parameter: Echelon (vendor cost) or the sponsor (sponsor labour, reported as transferred work, not Echelon cost). Choosing between them is a new owner decision, **P3-DEC-MOD005** (proposed). With Echelon performing M1 reviews, no customer size breaks even (P3-CLM-MOD017). Reviews follow demand (P3-ASM-MOD050), and a risk-tiered attested review (P3-ASM-MOD055) is shown as a configuration **subject to counsel and V-9**, not as established.

Vendor-borne registry cost by configuration, through the lean licensor's first invoice and sequences, is in the bases table of [16](16-capital-efficient-market-entry.md) section 1b; the gate threshold per configuration is in the same section.
