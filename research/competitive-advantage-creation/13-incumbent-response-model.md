## Answer

**Incumbents are not static, and their cheapest rational responses (renewal price concessions, contract extension, bundling, acquiring the partner) defeat every Unbound advantage that is not already measured and sold through a channel they do not control.** Unbound stays viable under a rational response only as a **niche channel-licensing business**: a net price to Unbound at or above the walk-away price **for the cost base actually achieved**, a partner share of about **30% or less on a $100-150 headline**, and non-exclusive, change-of-control-protected partner terms. On the Phase II MD-6 cost base ($1.81M fixed) the floor is roughly **$73 or more per account-year** (vendor break-even ≤ ~43k accounts). On the Phase III lean licensor base, the model's walk-away price is about **$27.86 at five years and $21.56 at ten**. That base is $1.03M fixed, including TPSP obligations, control operations, a $60k founder draw and the registry, plus $12.15 variable per account. With the founder paid at market in cash, the walk-away is $30.35 ([16](16-capital-efficient-market-entry.md) response walk-away table, model revision 3; revision 2 gave $31.30, $23.61 and $33.78 on a $1.25M base). The variable cost is still unmeasured (P3-GAP-MOD004). An earlier Revision 2 text gave $21.51 on the revision-1 lean base ($612k fixed).

**Revision 2 (2026-11-26; P3-CHL-REV1004, P3-CHL-REV3005, P3-CHL-REV3009).** This answer previously gave "$73 or more per account-year (vendor break-even ≤ ~43k accounts)" as the single viability threshold. That figure is correct only on the MD-6 base. The lean figure is mostly founder subsidy plus unmeasured variable cost; the topology itself contributes about $45k a year of infrastructure. It is not an architectural advantage. Model revision 2 provides the walk-away with funded control operations and with the founder at market (`response-walk-away.md`). Use that one table across 02, 13, 16, 19 and 21. A **full-administration** entry does not survive a fee response of the size national sponsors have made before (P3-CLM-EK016). The Phase II operating advantage over a well-automated incumbent, **≈$11-71 per account-year**, is 2-14% of a 20 bps fee on a $250k account: smaller than one historical sponsor fee move (P3-CLM-EK008).

## 3. Scenarios

Inputs are ranges with basis in [assumptions.csv](data/fragments/EK/assumptions.csv). Economics use the Phase II technology-only licensing mode (MD-6), the mode least sensitive to productivity assumptions: vendor fixed cost ≈ **$1.81M a year** and vendor variable cost **$30.89 per account-year**, reconstructed exactly from results.md §2b (36,049 accounts at $81; reproduces 20,272 at $120) (P3-ASM-EK009/010). **Break-even accounts = $1.81M ÷ (net price − $30.89)** (P3-CLM-EK007). These are vendor costs only; build, CAC and capital recovery are excluded, so real thresholds are higher. **Every threshold in this section depends on the cost base.** On the Phase III lean licensor base (model revision 3: about $1.03M fixed and $12.15 variable per account, [16](16-capital-efficient-market-entry.md)), the same net prices break even at well under half of these account counts. For example, $84 net gives **14,305** accounts against 34,013 on MD-6, and $56.70 gives 23,071 (revision 2: 17,431 and 28,113) (an earlier Revision 2 note quoted about 8.5k on the revision-1 lean base). ### 3.2 The administration mode under a fee response

At 20 bps on a $250k account a sponsor earns $500 per account-year. The ≈$11-71 per account-year operating advantage over MD-4/MD-3 is **2.2-14.2%** of that. A fee response of the size seen at national sponsors (1.00% → 0.60%, a 40% cut; Vanguard "up to 45%") would take 20 bps to about 12 bps. Phase II break-even for AI-native administration (MD-5) rises from **$1.92B at 20 bps to $2.98B at 15 bps and $6.58B at 10 bps** (SRC-063). DEC-002 already defers administration; this confirms it is the mode least able to survive a price response.

## 4. Is Unbound viable when incumbents respond rationally?

A rational incumbent does **not** start a price war against a sub-scale entrant across its whole book. It concedes price **at the contested renewal**, extends contracts, bundles, matches promises, and, if the entrant gains traction, **buys the channel or the entrant** (precedent: CLM-025, P3-CLM-EK009). Under that response:

- **Administration-led entry: no.** A 40% fee move erases the $11-71 advantage several times over (§3.2).
- **Direct licensing against incumbents' installed base: no.** Win rates fall and the incumbent's renewal concession costs it little.
- **Channel licensing where Unbound fills a gap the partner lacks: yes, narrowly**, if four conditions hold: (1) net price at or above the walk-away price for the cost base actually achieved, or a committed minimum volume (P3-DEC-EK001; ~$73 on MD-6, ~$27.86 on the revision-3 lean base, which includes control operations, or $30.35 with the founder at market; previously ~$31.30 (revision 2) and ~$21.51 (revision 1)); (2) partner share ≤ ~30% on a $100-150 headline; (3) non-exclusive terms with change-of-control continuity (P3-DEC-EK002); (4) at least one differentiator (EK017 replay or EK010 reconciliation-proved migration) measured and valued by the partner's customers (V-3, V-5). Even then the outcome is a sustainable niche (~$2.6M revenue at month 60), not a venture-scale business (CLM-045).

The economics are robust to **no** and **moderate** responses only when the partner holds the customer relationship and the incumbent is not competing for that partner's slot. They are not robust to aggressive pricing at the same accounts or to acquisition of the partner.

## 5. Response playbook

| Trigger | Response | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Incumbent offers a renewal discount at a target account | Do not match on price below the walk-away floor (P3-DEC-EK001); compete on measured replay and reconciliation evidence; accept the loss | The incumbent's marginal concession is cheap; matching destroys Unbound's break-even |
| Incumbent publishes migration commitments or exit symmetry | Publish measured outcomes from real rehearsals rather than promises; require the incumbent's export in the customer's contract | Promises are matched in weeks (CHL-004); measured history cannot be bought |
| Incumbent bundles sponsorship, payments or charity data | Do not build a competing bundle (DEC-009: use existing rails); position as the neutral module the partner bundles | A bundle war needs balance sheet Unbound lacks |
| Distributor signs an exclusive with an incumbent | Move to the next unserved channel (CF software vendors without DAF depth, administrators, bank/trust platforms; DEC-001); do not chase exclusivity | Few distributor slots; exclusivity is costly to buy |
| Incumbent ships comparable replay or evidence features | Shift messaging to cost and neutrality; keep price at parity with a lower fixed cost | EK014/EK015 survive product parity |
| Partner is approached for acquisition | Rely on change-of-control continuity; consider whether a licence-plus-sale outcome is acceptable | Consolidation is the dominant precedent |
| Unbound itself receives an acquisition approach | Evaluate against the owner's goals, not as failure; it is a plausible exit (2-6x revenue, Low confidence) | Phase II found no venture-scale outcome (CLM-045) |
| No response for 12+ months | Use the window to measure EK017/EK010 on live pilots; do not over-build differentiators before evidence | P3-HYP-EK002; P3-DEC-EK003 |

Kill and change signals (feed report 21 (commercial thesis invalidation) and the Phase II kill rule, CHL-022): net price below the walk-away price for the achieved cost base with no volume commitment (Revision 2: previously "below ~$73"; see TI-10 in [21](21-commercial-thesis-invalidation.md)); partner share above ~40%; an incumbent matching replay and published outcomes before the first pilot is live; a target partner acquired by an incumbent during G1.

## 6. Main evidence gaps

No public technology-licence price or renewal-discount data (P3-GAP-EK003); exclusivity and change-of-control terms in distributor deals not public (P3-GAP-EK004); dates of the Fidelity and Vanguard fee cuts unconfirmed (P3-GAP-EK005, P3-GAP-EK011); no DAF evidence on win-rate elasticity to incumbent response (P3-GAP-EK009). All scenario parameters are therefore ASSUMPTION or INFERENCE ranges; every number in §3 should be recomputed by the Phase III model team rather than treated as a forecast. Most of these gaps can be narrowed for $0 through stayer interviews (V-1) and partner conversations (V-6).
