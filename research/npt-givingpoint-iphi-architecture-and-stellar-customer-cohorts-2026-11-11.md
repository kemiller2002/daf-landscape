# NPT GivingPoint versus Stellar iPhi: platform lineage and inherited Ren footprint
**Research date:** 2026-11-11
**Status:** Evidence-based architecture hypotheses; current NPT backend and supplier contract remain unverified.

## Finding 1: NPT historical Stellar technology evidence
NPT-branded 2020 donor application and account-management materials are hosted under npt.stellartechsol.com. This demonstrates a historical Stellar-hosted NPT donor experience or materials, not definitive iPhi ledger deployment. See [direct documentary evidence](npt-stellar-ren-primary-source-platform-link-2026-11-11.md). NPT publicly describes GivingPoint as launched in 2021 after a two-year development and implementation project; the developer and ledger backend are not named.

## Finding 2: iPhi is more than a donor-facing portal
Ren's post-acquisition technical description calls iPhi a philanthropic subaccounting platform that ingests daily market data, allocates it to accounts and subaccounts, and supports donor/endowment/foundation views. The vendor's public demo at https://demo.stellartechsol.com/iphidemo/ displays an example foundation, not a real customer environment. This makes the **GivingPoint frontend over iPhi backend** hypothesis architecturally plausible, not verified.

## Finding 3: 2023 acquisition inherited an existing customer base
Ren acquired Stellar January 20, 2023. Ren's announcement and Stellar's sale adviser say iPhi already served major DAF sponsors, community foundations, religious philanthropies and financial institutions. Therefore Ren's post-2023 customer footprint cannot be interpreted as all organically won. However, there is no public cohort bridge, acquisition price, inherited ARR, or verified current retained client count.

## Architecture hypotheses to falsify
- **H1 Frontend refresh:** GivingPoint replaced Stellar donor UX but retained iPhi CoreEnterprise/subaccounting. Test: authenticated/authorized vendor architecture statement, contract or release documentation showing fund ledger integration.
- **H2 Full migration:** GivingPoint introduced a replacement ledger and donor UX, and Stellar became legacy. Test: migration/retirement announcement, old account portal closure, new system-of-record attribution.
- **H3 Partial coexistence:** GivingPoint is a frontend with multiple underlying systems by product, channel or period. Test: NPT integration map and transaction routing.
- **H4 Limited Stellar scope:** Stellar only hosted selected web forms/documents or a subset of NPT services. Test: original scope of work, archived site and records of system usage.
None currently proven.

## Clarity: avoid four inference errors
1. A 2020 Stellar domain does **not** prove a 2026 iPhi backend.
2. An NPT-branded GivingPoint interface does **not** prove NPT owns its source or accounting stack.
3. Ren's 2023 acquisition does **not** prove it won every historical Stellar account organically or retained them through 2026.
4. Stellar client case-study efficiency claims, such as a 75% grant-processing reduction at Minneapolis, are vendor-authored and not independently audited causal outcomes.

## EDF control-point audit
Trace contribution → NPT legal acceptance → GivingPoint donor event → authoritative fund ledger → custodian investment positions → grant approval → bank settlement → nonprofit receipt. At each boundary identify source of truth, event ID, timestamp, reconciliation owner, exportability and supplier dependency. A branded interface can mask a third-party ledger; the diagnostic must test **operational control and reversibility**, not design appearance.
