# Expanded DAF ecosystem: institutions, technology vendors, sponsors and infrastructure
**Evidence cut-off: November 11, 2026 | U.S.-focused; some providers advertise global capabilities**

## Executive conclusion

The research is **no longer restricted to Ren, TIFIN Give, and Chariot**. It now covers **23 separately categorized entities**, **15 documented relationships**, **46 public sources**, and **9 dated sponsor/vendor scale observations**. The major addition is **Foundation Source**, which belongs near Ren on the shortlist for core philanthropy administration and platform services rather than being treated as an incidental adjacent company. **National Philanthropic Trust (NPT)** is also a real alternative for institutions that want a private-label sponsor-operated program without necessarily licensing a standalone administrative platform. **GivingTech DAFtech** merits a focused emerging-vendor check but requires much more independent verification.

A 2026 revision of the DAF Research Collaborative Annual DAF Report identified **1,512 FY2024 U.S. DAF sponsors**: 103 national, 803 community foundations and 606 single-issue charities [U01]. This is a *sponsor census*, **not a count of competing software vendors or current 2026 active firms**. The report also separates national donation-processing platforms from traditional DAF sponsor models.

## 1. Five distinguishable markets, not one competitor list

| Market | Buyers / participants | Named providers | Competitive question |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Core institutional DAF technology and administration** | Financial institutions, sponsoring charities, wealth managers | **Ren**, **Foundation Source**, **TIFIN Give**, **GivingTech DAFtech** | Which platform operates donor/advisor workflows, fund accounting, compliance and controls with the least total cost and strongest migration rights? |
| **DAF sponsorship/private-label institutional model** | Banks, RIA networks, wealth managers, families | **NPT**, **RCF**, **Charityvest**, **GiveClear**, **AEF**, **United Charitable**, **Greater Horizons** | Who is legal sponsor and who supplies / operates technology and retains distribution rights? |
| **Large national and community DAF sponsors** | Donors, families, financial advisors, nonprofits | **Fidelity Charitable**, **DAFgiving360**, **Vanguard Charitable**, **SVCF**, **NPT** | Donor economics, advice/custody relationships, access, charitable activity and service, not platform ARR |
| **Digital and workplace/transaction giving** | Retail DAF donors, employers, nonprofit checkout, employee givers | **Daffy**, **Charityvest**, **Benevity/AOGF**, **Blackbaud Giving Fund**, **Givinga**, **Chariot** | Are we measuring true donor-accepted DAF assets, pass-through grants, workplace pooling, API transactions or a distinct charitable savings product? |
| **Underlying infrastructure and neighboring products** | Platforms and wealth integrators | **BridgeFT**, **Chariot**, **PG Calc** | Custody data integration, grant payment rails and planned-giving tooling are potentially inputs to a platform, not substitutes for legal DAF sponsorship |

**Analytical rule:** A sponsor's assets under management are **not** software company assets/revenue. A product held by an acquired company or an affiliate is **not another independent competitor**. A grant that passes through a payment processor is not necessarily a traditional ongoing donor-advised account. Never sum across vendor + sponsor records without deduplicating legal and commercial entities.

## 2. Foundation Source is the most important addition

**What is verified by official company and charity material:**
- **September 2023:** GTCR acquired Foundation Source and said it would fund technology, acquisitions and organic growth [U10].
- **February 2024:** Foundation Source acquired **PG Calc**, adding planned-giving software and services [U32].
- **October 2024:** Foundation Source acquired **Vennfi**, the technology company powering Charityvest's DAF program [U04,U31]. **Charityvest, Inc. remained an independent 501(c)(3) sponsor**. This is explicitly documented by both the charity and vendor [U07,U31].
- **April 2025:** Foundation Source acquired **Giving Place**, TIFIN's family-office/private-foundation giving software, while TIFIN said it was concentrating on DAF products [U05]. Do **not** misclassify it as the sale of TIFIN Give's entire DAF platform.
- **September 2025:** Foundation Source announced multi-custody DAF connectivity using **BridgeFT's WealthTech API**, supporting an advisor-branded approach [U06,U34].
- **November 2025:** Foundation Source and Charityvest announced an institutionally branded **Temple University** DAF program, a concrete deployment reference [U09].
- **As of December 31, 2025**, a Foundation Source product brief marketed **over 5,600 private foundations, 20,000 DAF accounts, 1,700 nonprofits, more than $55bn charitable assets, over 190,000 grants/planned gifts annually** [U08]. **These are cross-product company-reported operating figures**, not $55bn pure DAF assets, not recognized vendor revenue, not recurring-contract evidence, and not independently audited in this study.

**Competitive inference:** Foundation Source has assembled DAF technology, private-foundation administration and planned-giving capabilities under common PE ownership. It can potentially offer an integrated philanthropic suite and multiple distribution channels. That is a **material competitive threat to both Ren and TIFIN**, especially at wealth managers and sponsors requiring combined products. This is still not proof that its acquired software is technically integrated, more reliable or profitable.

**A very useful integration boundary:** BridgeFT documentation says custodial source data, including tax lots, may arrive on overnight schedules [U35]. A multi-custody connection does **not** automatically imply same-day or real-time cost basis. Test explicit data freshness, source IDs, reconciliation lag and permissions before comparing Foundation Source with TIFIN's marketed real-time Tax Intelligence.

**Security scope caveat:** PG Calc states it passed an *expanded SOC 2 Type II audit in 2021* [U33]. That is a **historical statement by an acquired planned-giving product company**, **not a current SOC 2 Type II report for Foundation Source's DAF stack**. Likewise, Ren's old Type I/II public discrepancy remains unresolved by independent audit evidence. We need exact **legal entity, system, criteria, observation period and current audit report** for every platform.

## 3. NPT: a sponsor that is also a genuine institutional choice

NPT's public institutional services explain that it configures **private-label** DAF programs for financial institutions, including investment options, fee structures, digital workflows, SSO and branded advisor/client experience [U11,U29]. Its FAQ indicates a **typical 14–16-week implementation**, an official planning estimate rather than a guaranteed contractual deadline.

NPT reported **20,561 DAF accounts and $6.44bn grants for FY2025 ended June 30, 2025**, alongside 159,543 grants [U02].

**Competition:** A bank deciding whether to launch a DAF doesn't necessarily buy Ren software or Foundation Source software directly. It could instead contract for NPT's branded sponsorship and operations. This is **indirect competition at the institution's buying decision**, even if NPT's actual technology stack or third-party suppliers are not publicly known.

What needs verification: pricing structure and profit/cost allocation, technology ownership, underlying system reports, custodial integration, exceptions and data portability, outsourcing permissions and donor account transition rules.

## 4. GivingTech DAFtech: an emerging operational-platform candidate

GivingTech markets DAFtech as a white-label DAF platform offering **technology-only** and **fully managed** models with portal, onboarding, deposits, grants, fees, portfolio connectivity and administration, along with optional sponsorship and AI support [U17,U18]. It claims high cost savings and faster operations.

**What is *not* verified:** Actual U.S. 501(c)(3) sponsoring charity name/EIN for “Your Brand, Our Charity,” financial-reporting assurance, audited SOC scope, company operating volume specific to DAFs, exact pricing, demonstrated ledger correctness, audited processing speeds or verified customer reference deployments. Company marketing elsewhere describes DAFtech as a sponsor, which is not enough to establish the *legal charitable entity*.

**Priority:** High-potential vendor to investigate, but not place above incumbents based on unverified 40–60% savings claims or marketing screenshots. Demand a sponsor/EIN mapping, current audit reports, exact ledger/accounting demo and independent institutional references before assigning capability scores.

## 6. Digital and workplace giving: another economic market

- **Charityvest** publishes a **cash DAF account with no annual administration fee**, and a separate invested account whose administrative fee begins at **0.60% on the first $500k**, subject to a **$100 annual minimum** [U46]. That is an important lower-balance alternative to previously assessed $250-minimum DAF offerings. Additional investments and contribution methods can still carry costs; eligibility for the new 2026 non-itemizer direct-gift deduction is **not** established for a DAF deposit.
- **Daffy** makes public API documentation available, including charitable account, contribution and donation operations and agent/assistant tooling [U15]. It is a digital-first donor product, not an established independent sponsor-admin SaaS comparable to Ren without additional evidence.
- **Givinga** markets a corporate **Charitable Savings Account (CSA)** and explicitly distinguishes it from being locked into a third-party DAF [U16]. The legal/tax characterization needs verification. It belongs in the *corporate-giving adjacent* category, not automatically in a Ren/TIFIN DAF core-systems contest.
- **Benevity** is a corporate workplace giving platform, while **American Online Giving Foundation** is the U.S. charity named in Benevity's disbursement model [U36,U37]. Two legal/commercial entities may contribute to one donation processing model.
- **The Blackbaud Giving Fund** underpins nonprofit and employer giving workflows in the Blackbaud/YourCause ecosystem [U20]. This has distinct donation-processor economics from permanent investment-advised DAF accounts.
- **Chariot** is the transaction/disbursement API and payment infrastructure layer, already evaluated separately. The TIFIN partnership provides real evidence of its role, but not universal exclusivity [U23,U39,U40].

The new map prevents **workplace pass-through giving** from inflating a comparison of endowment-like DAF accounts, sponsor operating profits or software contracts. It also avoids recording an **acquired company as a separate surviving competitive platform** after the acquisition.

## 8. Strategic synthesis

The market is consolidating at the **software-and-services layer** while charitable sponsorship remains legally separate in several prominent partnerships:

- **Ren ↔ RCF** is a disclosed related-party administration relationship.
- **Foundation Source ↔ Charityvest** combines a for-profit technology platform (acquired Vennfi) with a legally independent sponsor.
- **TIFIN Give ↔ GiveClear ↔ Chariot** separates platform, legal DAF sponsorship and payment.
- **Benevity ↔ AOGF** similarly separates corporate giving software and U.S. charitable sponsor, but in a different kind of giving market.
- **NPT** competes by supplying an institutional private-label sponsor + operating model.

This helps explain why a competitive study limited to a list of *sponsors* can be misleading: the sponsors can share infrastructure or simply offer a distinct bundled buying route. It also explains why buyer-side selection needs separate controls for **legal asset owner, platform, outsourced administrator, custodian, advisor and disbursement provider**.

**Hypothesis:** Foundation Source's acquisitions could expand cross-selling across private foundations, DAFs and planned giving, but could also create integration complexity. Its public company asset/support claims do not prove cross-selling success, completed technology integration or margin improvement. Test conversion, actual multivehicle client adoption, operational hours per completed grant, overlap of identities, account ledger integration, and signed retention contracts.

## 9. Verification boundaries

- **No 2026 comparable company revenue, enterprise ARR, NRR, gross margins or signed contract fees** established for Foundation Source, NPT, GivingTech, Daffy or digital/corporate providers here.
- **No SOC 2 audit report** for newly added DAF platforms inspected. PG Calc's historical Type II is not assurance for Foundation Source's DAF system.
- **No live functionality test** or third-party accounting validation performed; official product pages establish marketing, not measured performance.
- **No legal tax opinion** that Givinga's CSA, Foundation Source's Charityvest-sponsor offering, or GivingTech's sponsor option is universally eligible for a given donor's tax situation.
- **No private data** from former employers, expert networks, customers, investors or signed nonpublic agreements added.
- FY2024/FY2025/FY2026 fiscal periods are not assumed the same, and any vendor-reported portfolio size is checked for cross-program scope.
