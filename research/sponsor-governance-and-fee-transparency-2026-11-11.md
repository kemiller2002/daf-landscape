# DAF sponsor governance, provider conflicts, and verifiable operating expenses
**Public-source research: November 11, 2026 | Extends the 24-entity DAF market map**

## Executive synthesis

We started with a narrow question: **Does Charityvest's FY2024 audit identify actual payments to Foundation Source?** The charity indexes the FY2024/23 Smith & Howard report, but the attached file could not be retrieved from its GitBook document portal in the available research environment [G11,G16]. We separately attempted GiveClear's FY2024/23 audited PDF; its current website lists the report but the linked CDN returned HTTP 403 [G12]. **Neither audit note was read, and neither report can justify an exact technology-provider payment.**

A more meaningful, directly documented distinction emerged: **legally independent charitable sponsorship does not imply that the service vendor is absent from the sponsor's board.** Charityvest's official current board roster lists Stephen Kump, identified there as **President of DAF Technology at Foundation Source**, as a charity director [G01]. The charity separately says that **Foundation Source is a fee-paid software and administrative-services vendor that has no legal claim to DAF assets** [G03].

This overlap is an **observable governance relationship, not proof of conflicted decisions**. Charityvest publishes a conflicts-of-interest policy directing conflicted directors to disclose, leave deliberation and vote, and document the transaction in minutes [G02]. We have **not** obtained those minutes, actual fee approvals, 2024–26 service-agreement amendments or a contemporaneous record of the director's participation. Its public policy PDF states that it has been adopted, but the printed approval date at the end is blank; thus **the adoption date and whether this is the latest version cannot be established from the published copy**.

RCF/Ren presents a related, but more financially observable case: the **FY2024 RCF audited financials** identify **$19,225,546 paid to Ren** and a 98%-based fee-sharing formula for a specified *individual fundholder* income base; the notes also disclose overlapping Ren personnel on the RCF board [G05]. Even here, exact independent board committee review, contract negotiation and vendor costs have not been reviewed. **Do not equate board overlap with illegality**, and do not label Foundation Source and Ren relationships legally identical without comparing the actual contracts and governing rules.

The due-diligence question has changed from simply "Is this charity independent?" to **"Can its disinterested decision-makers negotiate, supervise, reprice and terminate the technology supplier, while preserving fund and grant integrity?"**

## 1. Different kinds of independence

| Dimension | Meaning | Publicly observed | Proof still required |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Legal sponsorship and asset control** | A 501(c)(3) charitable entity has legal ownership/control over accepted DAF assets | Charityvest independently owns its DAF assets; Foundation Source doesn't [G03] | Governing agreements and actual sponsor approval authority |
| **Corporate/entity independence** | Nonprofit and for-profit vendor are separate legal entities | Charityvest and Foundation Source are separate institutions [G03] | Ownership, related-person and assignment details of each contract |
| **Board composition** | Whether directors have paid roles or other connections to vendors | Current Charityvest director also holds senior Foundation Source position [G01]; RCF/Ren board overlap disclosed in 2024 audit [G05] | Applicable fiscal year board roster and independence tests |
| **Conflict policy** | Policy for disclosed or potentially conflicting decisions | Charityvest policy requires recusal and minutes [G02] | Board-approved current version and actual documented application |
| **Procurement and renewal independence** | Ability to select, compare, terminate or replace vendor | Provider contracts exist [G03,G05]; no tested renewal/termination decision | Independent alternatives, disinterested approvals, fee fairness and signed contract rights |
| **Data and fund portability** | Sponsor can retain own donor records and fund ledger after provider exit | AEF earlier migrated its portal to Ren, with limited new portal history; earlier study | Witnessed complete five-year export and grant reconciliation |
| **Financial independence** | Ability to audit what the sponsor pays and vendor receives | Ren/RCF payee amount audited; Charityvest/Foundation Source payee amount unknown [G05,G06] | Vendor invoice trail, sponsor expense note and corresponding vendor revenue booking |

The IRS's [Form 990 Schedule L instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i990sl) [G04] impose disclosure rules for certain transactions involving *interested persons* and specific reporting thresholds. They also describe how transactions can affect classification of governing board members as independent for Form 990 purposes. We **cannot conclude Schedule L was required or that its requirements were violated** solely from an overlap on a current online board roster. The interested person definitions, dates, entity control, consideration, amounts and exceptions have to be examined by a qualified reviewer.

### Charityvest's actual published policy, not assumed execution

The [public policy](https://www.charityvest.org/documents/Charityvest%20Conflict%20of%20Interest%20Policy.pdf) [G02] provides:
- Timely disclosure of actual/potential conflicts, including another relationship or an economic arrangement with a corporation doing business with the charity.
- A director whose issue is under review should leave while non-conflicted directors determine whether a conflict exists.
- A director found actually conflicted may supply information but must not participate in deliberation or vote.
- The minutes should document who was present or absent, the interest involved, the alternatives considered, recusals and votes.
- Annual director disclosure forms are called for.
- The PDF ends with a blank "July ___, 2016" approval date. Its **published implementation history is unknown**.

**Suggested responsible follow-up:** Request a redacted disinterested-board approval summary of the post-2024 Foundation Source service agreement, material fee changes, and annual reviews. No donor-level data, private board minutes, or nonpublic contracts should be put into a public research repository without permission.

## 2. Form 990 fee categories do not reveal the technology vendor

The four comparative rows below come from different entity structures. They describe **FY2024 expense reporting**, not a normalized cost per DAF account or a fair platform-versus-platform price.

| Public sponsor / service relationship | FY2024 total expenses | FY2024 grants | Other-than-grants (difference) | Relevant named provider-payment evidence |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **Charityvest / Foundation Source** | **$52,603,302** | **$51,791,362** | **$811,940** | **No separately verified Foundation Source payment** [G06,G07] |
| **GiveClear / TIFIN Give** | **$33,572,987** | **$31,760,654** | **$1,812,333** | **No separately verified TIFIN payment** [G08,G12] |
| **NPT** (larger sponsor) | **$5,698,880,841** | **$5,563,257,810** | **$135,623,031** | No individual tech vendor identified in extracted categories [G10] |
| **RCF / Ren** | Full operating expense comparison omitted | Full grants comparison omitted | Not derived here | **$19,225,546 paid to Ren** in RCF audited financials [G05] |

The **$811,940** for Charityvest, **$1,812,333** for GiveClear and **$135,623,031** for NPT are arithmetic sums of expense categories **other than grants**, **not software or administration fees**, and **not vendor contract revenues**. They're not inherently comparable without correcting for scale, sponsor functions, costs paid by third parties, and portfolio investment expenses. Moreover the normal sponsor tax return may show no salaried staff while a contracted third-party service provider pays personnel and other costs.

**The expense category trap:** GiveClear's [2024 Form 990](https://philanthropy.org/990/report/813222963/giveclear-foundation) reports **$141,949 management fees**, **$1,561,893 investment-management fees**, **$21,414 information technology**, and **$18,833 other service fees** [G08]. **We have no proof these are TIFIN's invoices.** The same caution applies to NPT's **$74,179,211 investment-management fees**, **$3,649,680 information-technology**, and **$2,500,886 other service fees** [G10]. Its contract parties and internal/outsourced costs are not identified by those totals.

In contrast, RCF's *audited note specifically names Ren and the paid amount* [G05]. That **named-payee granularity** is the evidence tier we should seek from Charityvest/Foundation Source and GiveClear/TIFIN, rather than ranking sponsor operating-cost percentages.

See the [machine-readable comparable-expense dataset](../data/sponsor-expense-category-comparison-2024.csv). It deliberately leaves actual provider payments blank where unverified.

## 3. A broader GiveClear distribution network is now documented

GiveClear's own **2025 impact report** [G09] specifically identifies:

| Name | Publicly described role | What is established and what is not |
|---|---|---|
| **AssetMark** | Financial-advisor integration | Named channel; no signed fee amount or funded client cohort |
| **Blue Trust** | Platform advice and advisor enablement | Named partner; not independently established advisor-sponsored software ARR |
| **Ingalls & Snyder** | Separately managed accounts | GiveClear says its active accounts **doubled in 2025**, but does not give an absolute starting count in the cited statement |
| **Arlington** | Family-office wealth planning channel | GiveClear says joined near end of 2024 and grew during 2025; actual DAF account count unknown |
| **Zions Bank** | Financial institution partner | GiveClear says added during 2025; no fee or program activation count verified |
| **TIFIN Give** | Core technology and advisor enablement | Official partner relationship, not a complete paid vendor contract or independently tested platform [G09,G15] |

This matters competitively because **distribution depends on channels**. A new bank or advisory platform may bring charitable funds even if its own institution doesn't purchase an independently priced DAF software subscription. **A partner logo or an investment adviser channel is not an additional charitable sponsor or a paying software customer**.

The [distribution relationship dataset](../data/giveclear-wealth-partner-observations-2025.csv) stores only what the sponsor actually reported, with an explicit qualifier on missing contractual economic details.

## 4. A useful oversight scorecard, not a claim of regulatory failures

| Question | Charityvest / Foundation Source | RCF / Ren | GiveClear / TIFIN |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sponsor owns assets | Documented [G03] | Documented [G05] | Documented operating model [G15] |
| Technology/service provider identified | Foundation Source [G03] | Ren [G05] | TIFIN, plus Chariot for certain payments [G15] |
| Vendor/board overlap documented | Current Foundation Source officer on Charityvest board [G01] | Overlapping Ren employees in 2024 audit [G05] | Not established in documents reviewed |
| Conflict policy publicly reviewed | Published policy exists, actual application unknown [G02] | Current policy and recusal history not reviewed | Form 990 describes conflict policy; implementation not reviewed [G08] |
| Actual named sponsor-to-vendor service fees | **Unknown** | **$19.2255m FY2024 paid to Ren** [G05] | **Unknown** |
| Current provider contract and termination terms inspected | **No** | Related-party contract **not** inspected; generic MSA separate | **No** |
| Full migration/record export witnessed | **No** | **No**; prior AEF migration documented | **No** |

**No numerical "governance risk ranking" is warranted** without comparing actual disinterested decisions, signed contracts, sponsor policies and current SOC coverage. Public board affiliation is a due-diligence flag that creates a **testable question**, not a finding of misconduct.

## 5. Highest-value remaining work

1. **Retrieve the actual 2024 Charityvest audit note.** Sponsor hosts an audit link, but the file endpoint blocked our tools [G11,G16]. Check related-party, contracts, management fees, recurring expense obligations, and acquisition date; do not guess.
2. **Retrieve GiveClear's 2024 audit.** Its CDN returned 403 [G12]. Seek named technology and investment management vendors, related-party transactions and post-2024 TIFIN platform obligations.
3. **Request legal sponsor procurement oversight evidence.** Obtain the dates and independent approval status for the Foundation Source and Ren service agreements, plus current board/vendor conflict disclosure and documented recusal controls.
4. **Reconcile provider payees and accounts.** Compare approved invoices, expense ledger, payee legal entity, time period, tax treatment and vendor revenue recognition to signed contract. An accounting category is not a payee.
5. **Advance channel research.** Determine which GiveClear financial adviser/bank partnerships actually began billing/funding, on what date, with which contract roles; avoid assuming every logo contributes software ARR.
6. **Validate product decisions across conflict structures.** Apply the existing 2026 donor tax opportunity cases to determine whether a donor is shown a direct gift, cash DAF and invested DAF with full costs, even where an advisor or technology provider benefits from invested accounts.
7. **Return to institutional sponsor comparisons.** NPT and Foundation Source/Charityvest may compete by bundled sponsorship/outsourcing, while Ren and TIFIN may supply technology in different arrangements.

### Bottom line

The next financial comparison depends on **named counterparties, audited payments, and effective board oversight**, not raw donor assets, grant volumes or nonprofit "overhead" percentages. We can now describe concrete points of scrutiny across **Charityvest/Foundation Source, RCF/Ren, GiveClear/TIFIN and NPT**, and identify real distribution channels without manufacturing software revenues.
